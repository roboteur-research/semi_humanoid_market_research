## Company background
Genesis AI is a physical-AI lab that emerged from stealth in July 2025 with one of the largest seed rounds in robotics ($105M, Eclipse + Khosla) to build a universal robotics foundation model. Its roots are in simulation: CEO Zhou Xian created the widely used open-source Genesis physics engine, and the company's differentiator is massive synthetic-data generation (Genesis World 1.0) to train its GENE foundation model, closing the sim-to-real gap [S1][S3]. In May 2026 TechCrunch reported the company had "gone full stack" — building its own robot rather than only models [S2]; in June 2026 it unveiled Eno, a wheeled, foldable general-purpose robot with dexterous five-finger hands (co-developed with Wuji Tech) [S3][S4].

Strategy is deliberately contrarian to legged humanoids: a minimalist telescoping/articulated tower on a wheeled base, sized and priced (~$90k reported) for manufacturing, logistics and lab work, with production and targeted customer deployments planned by end-2026. A multi-phase strategic partnership with LG CNS (announced with the launch) will pilot and scale Eno across LG's US manufacturing/logistics operations, using LG CNS as systems integrator [S4]. The US-France split gives it EU research presence (Bpifrance and Xavier Niel are investors).

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
Genesis AI is one of the best-funded new entrants betting specifically on the wheeled semi-humanoid form factor, with a top-tier simulation/foundation-model story and an anchor enterprise partner (LG CNS). Fox News coverage of the launch noted "industrial customers actually asked for wheels" — the design thesis matches this study's premise. (Discovery attributed a "wheels solve 85-90% of industrial use cases" quote to this lineage; that exact figure could not be verified in launch coverage — treat as unconfirmed.) For an EU entrant, Genesis is a serious emerging competitor with deep pockets, EU footprint, and end-2026 deployment timing.
