## Company background
DFKI's Robotics Innovation Center in Bremen is Germany's leading AI-plus-robotics contract research centre, spanning underwater, space, and assistance robotics. AILA ("Artificial Intelligence Lightweight Android", ~2010) was its semi-humanoid entry: a female-styled anthropomorphic upper body — deliberately styled to counter the male-coded robot norm — on a six-wheeled mobile base, built as a research platform for mobile dual-arm manipulation [S1][S2]. AILA served in projects from SemProM (RFID-based product handling, "semantic product memory") through BesMan (mobile manipulation behaviours, 2012-2016, with space-relevant demos on an ISS mockup), HySociaTea (human-robot team collaboration, 2014-2016) and Robofoot (footwear manufacturing, 2010-2013) [S1]. DFKI lists the system as "not actively used anymore" — AILA is retired [S1].

RIC's centre of gravity is space and extreme-environment robotics plus exoskeletons; it did not pursue a commercial semi-humanoid after AILA. Its relevance to the market is as a German AI-robotics talent pool, a public-funding magnet (BMBF/ESA/DLR projects), and a partner for perception/learning software.

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
Historical/contextual: AILA showed German capability in lightweight dual-arm design (payload-to-weight ratio >1 per arm) a decade before the current wave, but DFKI never productized it. For a German entrant DFKI is a potential R&D partner (AI, manipulation learning, space spin-ins) rather than a competitor; the AILA lineage also illustrates that even well-funded German institutes left the semi-humanoid product space open.
