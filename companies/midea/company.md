## Company background
Midea is one of the world's largest appliance manufacturers and — via its €4.5B acquisition of KUKA (2016-17, fully delisted 2021) — one of the world's largest industrial robot makers. The Midea-KUKA park in Shunde is China's largest industrial-robot production base with >80,000 cumulative units delivered [S3]. In 2024 Midea created a humanoid-robot innovation center inside its central research institute and has since followed a deliberate three-stage roadmap: industrial first (MIRO 美罗 series), commercial second (Meila 美拉 series entering Midea experience stores 2026), household last [S1][S2].

The MIRO industrial line iterated three generations in under a year: MIRO gen-1 (March 2025; wheel-legged, ~1.9 m, 16-DoF arm, six-axis force sensors; deployed in the Jingzhou washing-machine factory for inspection and material handling), MIRO X (gen-2), and MIRO U (gen-3, revealed 5 Dec 2025 by CTO Wei Chang): a six-armed wheeled-column robot Midea brands a "super-humanoid" (超人形) — the industry's coinage for deliberately non-anthropomorphic multi-arm forms [S1][S2][S4]. MIRO U entered the Wuxi high-end washing-machine factory production lines at end-2025 with a claimed 30% changeover-efficiency gain [S1]. The Meila commercial/home series is in final testing for 2026 store deployment (guidance, demos, coffee) [S1][S5].

All core MIRO technology is claimed as in-house Midea R&D (not KUKA-derived), but KUKA provides world-class industrial robotics engineering, channels and service infrastructure that Midea can leverage at will [S3][S4].

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
Midea matters for three reasons. First, it is the clearest "user-manufacturer": it deploys its own robots in its own factories, so its designs optimize for measured line-side ROI, not demo appeal — hence six arms instead of two. Second, the 超人形 "super-humanoid" concept (multi-arm, task-first morphology) is a direct intellectual challenge to the humanoid orthodoxy, and Midea has the scale to prove it. Third — critical for a German audience — Midea owns KUKA: any successful MIRO/Meila platform has a ready-made path into European factories through KUKA's brand, integrator network and service organization, making Midea a latent but potentially dominant EU competitor without needing to build a channel. Trajectory: scaling from internal deployment toward external commercial sales.
