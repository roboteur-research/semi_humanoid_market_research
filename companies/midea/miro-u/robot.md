# MIRO U (美罗U) — Midea Group

> MIRO U is Midea's third-generation industrial robot and the industry's first six-armed wheeled(-legged) "super-humanoid" (超人形), revealed 5 Dec 2025 by CTO Wei Chang and deployed into Midea's Wuxi high-end washing-machine factory at end-2025. Six biomimetic arms with quick-swap end-effectors on a stable wheeled column enable parallel multi-task operations that Midea claims cut line-changeover time by 30%. It matters because it is a robot designed by a factory owner for its own measured ROI — and because its maker owns KUKA, giving any successful iteration a fast lane into European manufacturing.

## Design & morphology
Six biomimetic robotic arms mounted around a wheeled(-legged) column chassis — Midea and Chinese press describe it as 轮腿式 (wheel-legged) with stable vertical motion [S1][S2]. "Breaks human physiological limits": the point of six arms is parallel multi-tasking and rapid tool transitions beyond what any dual-arm anthropomorph can do [S1]. Height/weight/DoF: n/a (not disclosed). Predecessor context: MIRO gen-1 (Mar 2025) was ~1.9 m, 68 kg, wheel-legged with a 16-DoF arm and six-axis force sensing [S3] — MIRO U specifics were not published at reveal.

## Locomotion
Wheeled(-legged) chassis with stable vertical movement and 360° in-place rotation [S1]. Speed/terrain n/a. Factory-floor (flat, structured) environment.

## Upper body & manipulation
Six arms under high-precision coordinated control [S1]. End-effectors support quick-swap (快换) for multi-dimensional collaborative tasks — the changeover use case is precisely tool/fixture swapping at speed [S1]. Payload, reach, per-arm DoF: n/a (not disclosed). Gen-1 heritage suggests in-house joints with six-axis F/T sensing [S3] (estimated for MIRO U).

## Sensing
n/a (not disclosed). Gen-1 had six-axis force sensors and self-developed joints [S3]; factory navigation implies lidar/vision but unconfirmed.

## Actuation & power
Self-developed joint technology (claimed fully in-house Midea R&D) [S2][S3]. Battery/runtime n/a.

## Compute & software
n/a (not disclosed). No VLA/AI-model marketing at all — messaging is entirely about industrial-engineering outcomes (30% changeover-efficiency improvement, line-cycle efficiency) [S1]. Presumably integrated with Midea's manufacturing execution systems; KUKA software involvement not claimed.

## Safety & compliance
n/a (not disclosed). Internal deployment sidesteps external certification; any external/EU sale would trigger full ISO 10218/CE work — where KUKA's compliance machinery would be a decisive asset (analyst note).

## Deployment evidence & traction
- Wuxi "dual-high-end" washing-machine factory: MIRO U entering production lines by end-2025 for changeover adjustment tasks; claimed 30% efficiency improvement in changeover [S1] (vendor-claimed; the factory is Midea's own, so this is a real deployment but self-reported ROI).
- MIRO gen-1 deployed in Jingzhou washing-machine factory (2025): equipment inspection + material handling [S3] (third-party reported).
- Meila (美拉) commercial/household sibling series: final testing, entering Midea offline experience stores across major Chinese cities in 2026 for product guidance/demos [S1][S4].
- CTO Wei Chang framing: "MIRO's core value lies in transcending morphological imitation to achieve operational-efficiency leaps in industrial scenarios" [S1].

## Assessment (analyst view)
*Analyst opinion.* Strengths: real factory deployment with owner-operator ROI discipline; radical task-first morphology (six arms) that dual-arm competitors cannot match for changeover parallelism; effectively unlimited capital; KUKA ownership as a dormant EU distribution/certification superhighway. Weaknesses: zero published specs, no external sales, no AI/autonomy story, unproven outside Midea's own controlled environments; six-arm coordination complexity is nontrivial to generalize. Threat to an EU entrant: low today (internal-only), but strategically the highest-ceiling threat in this batch for the German market — if Midea productizes MIRO via KUKA, it arrives with a trusted German brand, existing service network and factory-proven references. EU entrants should monitor KUKA's roadmap announcements closely.
