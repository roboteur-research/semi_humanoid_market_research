## Company background
Boardwalk Robotics is the commercialization vehicle that grew out of the Florida Institute for Human & Machine Cognition (IHMC) humanoid robotics group — one of the most decorated legged-robotics labs in the US (DARPA Robotics Challenge Atlas team, Nadia humanoid). Boardwalk has collaborated with IHMC since 2017, most visibly on the Nadia research humanoid; Alex, announced in August 2024, is its first fully in-house commercial product, drawing on "over 20 years of R&D in humanoid robots" [S1][S2].

Strategy: deliberately ship manipulation before locomotion. Boardwalk argues "there are many problem sets that don't require legs" but "there aren't very many problem sets that don't require halfway decent manipulation capabilities" — so Alex is a legless torso mounted on a pedestal or a third-party AMR, with legs kept on the roadmap (IHMC involvement expected to accelerate that when customers demand it) [S1]. The company designs its own low-gear-ratio actuators (high joint speed, torque control without expensive torque sensors), a Barrett-flavored philosophy consistent with the CEO's background [S1][S3].

Go-to-market is two-track: immediate sales to researchers (Alex is purchasable by research groups now) and staged commercial pilots in manufacturing, food processing, logistics, and maintenance — with aviation MRO (maintenance/repair/overhaul) as the first commercial pilot application [S1]. Pricing is not published. Manufacturing capacity is presumably low-volume/in-house; no contract-manufacturing partner disclosed.

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
Alex is the cleanest US example of the "torso-first" thesis an EU semi-humanoid entrant is likely pursuing: human-scale dual-arm torso, customer-chosen mobility (pedestal or AMR), industrial task focus. Boardwalk's IHMC lineage gives it credibility disproportionate to its size, and its aviation-MRO wedge is a differentiated, high-value niche. Constraints: apparently unfunded/quietly funded, tiny commercial footprint, and a currently broken website — traction risk is real. Watch whether legged Nadia technology migrates into a future commercial lower body.
