## Company background
TUM's Munich Institute of Robotics and Machine Intelligence (MIRMI) is one of Europe's top-ranked robotics centres. Its Geriatronics lighthouse project, run since 2018 from a dedicated research centre in Garmisch-Partenkirchen ("Campus GaPa") with partners LongLeif GaPa gGmbH and Caritas, develops robotics for independent living in old age. Flagship platform GARMI is a wheeled semi-humanoid: mobile base, extendable lifting column, two 7-DoF torque-controlled (Franka-lineage) arms, chest touchscreen and an expressive-eyes head, aimed at eldercare assistance, tactile telemedicine (remote examination with ECG, blood pressure, ultrasound), and robot-guided rehabilitation [S1][S2][S4].

Institutional context has shifted: founding director Sami Haddadin — the torque-controlled-robotics pioneer behind Franka Emika — left in January 2025 to become Vice President for Research at MBZUAI in Abu Dhabi (verified) [S3]. The GARMI program continued: on 22 January 2026 TUM presented a completely redesigned "new GARMI" (design by Munich Design Institute with studio Leap, clad in Alpine loden wool, deliberately non-humanoid friendly appearance), with cameras at eye level, lidar at leg height, and a chest screen for remote consultations [S2]. Earlier milestones: ChatGPT-based command interface, ~90% grasp success on cups/glasses, model-apartment trials in Garmisch (May 2024), 1 ms control cycle with force-sensing arms and a "Safety Motion Unit" speed governor [S4].

MIRMI's historical wheeled dual-arm platforms include TOMM (2017: omnidirectional base, two UR5 arms, full-body artificial skin — Gordon Cheng's ICS lab) and Rosie (~2009-2013, retired: mecanum base with two KUKA LWR-4 arms, famous for making pancakes, from the CoTeSys era). These establish Munich's long institutional experience with the semi-humanoid form factor.

The discovery notes flag "spin-off intent" for GARMI; no confirmed spin-off company could be verified as of Aug 2026 — TUM materials in 2024-2026 still describe deployment in real care homes as "several years" away [S4]. (Related but distinct: Haddadin's Franka Emika ecosystem and MIRMI spin-offs in other domains.)

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
Directly relevant to any German eldercare semi-humanoid strategy: GARMI is the most visible German care-robot research program, politically backed (Bavaria) and demographically motivated. It validates the wheeled-base + lift-column + dual-compliant-arm architecture for care. Risks to its trajectory: founder departure to MBZUAI and academic timelines. For a commercial entrant it is a potential clinical-validation partner and a benchmark competitor for public funding/attention, not a product competitor.
