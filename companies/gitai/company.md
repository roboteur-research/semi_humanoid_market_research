## Company background
GITAI was founded in Tokyo in 2016 by Sho Nakanose to replace astronaut labor with teleoperated/autonomous robots, cutting the cost of working in orbit. Its early flagship was the G1 humanoid-torso telepresence robot (~2018-2020): in a December 2018 JAXA Kibo-module mock-up test it completed 72% (13/18) of assigned astronaut tasks, using GITAI's signature low-latency compression (2.7K 360° video from 800Mbps to ~2.5Mbps at ~60ms latency). A JAXA joint research agreement followed in March 2019. [S1][S2]

GITAI subsequently dropped the humanoid form for modular single-arm systems: S1 operated inside the ISS (2021), S2 performed an external ISS demonstration (2024), plus lunar rover and inchworm-arm products; the company relocated HQ to the Los Angeles area (~2023) to chase US government/commercial space contracts (NASA ecosystem, DARPA LunA-10 lunar architecture study). Its Japanese entity, GITAI Japan, remains as an R&D base — making it a borderline "Japanese" company today. [S2][S3]

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
GITAI is a second clean example (after Telexistence) of a Japanese team building a credible dual-arm teleop semi-humanoid and then abandoning the humanoid form factor for task-specific arms once real deployment economics arrived — in this case for space, where every kilogram counts. Not a competitor in terrestrial semi-humanoid markets. [S2][S3]
