## Company background
igus is a billion-euro, family-owned Cologne "motion plastics" specialist (founded 1964): the world's dominant supplier of lubrication-free polymer bearings, energy chains and cables, selling into virtually every machine-building sector [S5][S6]. Since the late 2010s it has built a "Low Cost Automation" (LCA) business — cheap plastic-geared robot arms (robolink, then the ReBeL cobot from ~€5k in 2022), the ReBeL Hand bionic gripper (2023), the ReBeLMove AMR family (2024) and the RBTX online robotics marketplace, which matches low-cost components into turnkey systems [S1][S2]. iggyRob (April 2025) is the culmination: a humanoid-format service robot assembled almost entirely from igus's own catalogue parts, priced at €47,999 — deliberately "two to three times" below comparable humanoids [S1].

The strategic logic is unusual among the companies in this study: igus is not a venture-funded robot startup but a profitable components manufacturer using the humanoid as (a) a demonstrator for its lubrication-free plastic gears/joints, (b) a volume driver for those components (it also sells humanoid joints to other robot makers [S7]), and (c) a genuine low-cost product for pragmatic factory/service tasks. Distribution runs through igus's existing global direct-sales network and the RBTX marketplace, with a "test before you invest" on-site trial program [S1]. Internally igus plans to use iggyRob for loading its own injection-molding machines [S1].

By mid-2026 the line had split into at least two variants: the original iggyRob (1.70 m, on the ReBeLMove Pro AMR, €47,999) and the "iggyRob Home" (curvier shell, 2.5 kg/arm, base rated to 250 kg, 2 m/s, wireless charging, ~3-week delivery via RBTX) [S3][S4]. Note: the discovery-phase note of an "Industrial" variant on a 250 kg AMR could not be confirmed under that name — the 250 kg-chassis variant is marketed as "iggyRob Home" [S3][S4].

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
igus matters as the price-floor setter in Europe: a CE-certified, catalogue-orderable wheeled humanoid at €47,999 with 24-hour claimed ship time [S2] reframes what "entry-level" means for EU industrial buyers, even though its 2 kg-per-arm payload and 45°/s joint speeds are an order of magnitude below torque-controlled research platforms (PAL TIAGo Pro) or VC-funded logistics humanoids (Humanoid HMND 01). It is not a direct capability threat — it is a channel and narrative threat: igus can bundle the robot with the components business, sustain thin margins indefinitely, and reach tens of thousands of existing Mittelstand customers through an established sales network. Trajectory: scaling sideways (variants, marketplace) rather than up-market; no evidence of external customer deployments yet.
