## Company background
Dexmate is a 2024-founded Santa Clara startup building "tomorrow's intelligence and automation" — practically, a general-purpose wheeled semi-humanoid called Vega that is **orderable today** with a ~3-week lead time, unusual in a market dominated by waitlists and pilots [S1][S2]. The founding team is described as PhDs from MIT, UCSD and CMU, with staff experience spanning NVIDIA, Toyota Research Institute, Ohio State, Michigan and INSEAD [S1]. Funding is quietly raised: Dealroom attributes ~$41M total; Dexmate itself discloses nothing [S3].

Product strategy is developer-first: Vega is sold direct (from ~$80-90k) to AI developers, research labs and light-industrial users, with a pip-installable Python API, URDF/USD models for simulators, VR/exoskeleton teleoperation support and a grant program for researchers — an open-platform play closer to a "PR2 for the VLA era" than to closed humanoid pilots [S2]. The hardware differentiator is packaging: a foldable 3-DoF torso that compresses to 0.66 m for transport yet reaches ~2.2 m working height, on an omnidirectional base, with two 7-DoF high-payload arms and 10-30+ hour battery life [S2][S4].

Manufacturing scale, customer counts and revenue are undisclosed; the shop lists "In Production Now". Target segments per vendor and press: warehousing/logistics, manufacturing, healthcare and research [S3]. Traction signals (third-party): Vega units in testing with unnamed publicly traded companies across manufacturing, logistics and retail; Red Dot Design Award 2025; sponsor of a $200,000 bimanual-dexterity challenge (WBCD) at ICRA 2025; a "Vega-U" university variant is seeded via a grant program (6 units/cycle, $45k value each); LG Technology Ventures / LG CNS joined the cap table in early 2026, pointing to Asian retail/logistics ambitions [S5].

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
Dexmate compresses the semi-humanoid value proposition to a number: a full-featured omni-wheeled dual-7-DoF platform at ~$80-90k, shipping in weeks. For an EU entrant, that sets the reference price-performance point for research/developer sales in 2025-26 (undercutting Reachy 2 on capability-per-euro and PAL/NEURA on price), and it is already harvesting the academic mindshare an EU platform would want. Its weakness — no European entity, service network, or safety certification story — is exactly where an EU competitor can position. Trajectory: scaling production, courting VLA developers; likely to move up from research into light industrial deployments.
