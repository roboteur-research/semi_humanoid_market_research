## Company background
Flexiv is the leading "adaptive robotics" company to come out of Stanford's dexterous-manipulation lab: its Rizon series arms (launched 2019) combine joint torque sensing, industrial-grade force control (0.03–0.1 N sensing accuracy) and AI to perform contact-rich tasks — polishing, insertion, massage (its arms power massage-therapy chains in China) — that position-controlled cobots handle poorly. It operates as a genuinely bi-national company (Santa Clara + Shanghai/Foshan manufacturing, Munich office for Europe) and has raised ~$222M, including a Meituan-led $100M+ Series B [S3].

Its semi-humanoid line extends the Rizon force-control heritage to dual-arm platforms: AICO (AICO 1 → AICO 2) mounts two 7-DoF adaptive arms on a differential-drive mobile base with laser SLAM (6 h battery in the 2-4 config), while MICO is a compact stationary dual-arm "embodied intelligence" torso (Armor/Core/Plus/Ultra configs, ~150 cm, 53–79 kg) built on the Enlight platform and shown at ICRA 2026 in Vienna [S1][S2]. Both target embodied-AI development and force-sensitive industrial automation rather than consumer service.

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
Flexiv attacks the segment from the force-control high ground: ±0.05 mm repeatability with 0.03 N-class force sensing on a mobile dual-arm is a spec set aimed at precision assembly, polishing and lab automation — exactly where wheeled humanoids must beat humans on quality, not cost. With a Munich office and existing EU/US industrial customers for Rizon arms, Flexiv is one of the few China-rooted players with a live Western channel. Status is still early (AICO 2/MICO in development-to-early-commercial), so the near-term threat is in development platforms for embodied-AI teams and high-value force-critical niches.
