## Company background
Roboligent is a 2017-founded Austin-area robotics company built around patented compliant ("soft robot") manipulator technology — force-controlled, back-drivable Optimo arms designed for safe physical human-robot interaction. Its original commercial thrust is automated physical therapy: the Optimo Robot delivers robotic rehabilitation for neuromuscular limb conditions, and AFWERX/DoD selected the company for STTR Phase I and a $1.8M Phase II to trial robotic rehab for military use cases with UT Austin [S3]. This healthcare/defense funding path substitutes for venture capital and has kept the company niche but technically deep.

From the same arm technology, Roboligent assembled ROBIN — a mobile dual-arm humanoid combining two compliant Optimo-class arms, AMR navigation, and AI imitation learning — targeting machine tending, material handling and logistics in smart factories, plus teleoperation and healthcare tasks [S1][S2]. In 2025 it signed an MOU with Korean gripper specialist Tesollo; at RobotWorld 2025 the partners demonstrated ROBIN with Tesollo's DG-5F five-finger hand performing delicate real-time manipulation, explicitly aiming to "prove the commercial viability of dual-arm humanoid robots" for industrial precision tasks [S4].

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
Roboligent is a differentiated small player: unlike integrators re-badging Chinese arms, it owns compliant direct-drive manipulator IP well-suited to contact-rich tasks (rehab, tending) and safe collaboration — the same technical bet as Duatic (CH) or direct-drive players. Its dual market (physical therapy + machine tending) and DoD funding make it resilient but slow-scaling; the Tesollo partnership signals Korean market/component ties. Threat to an EU entrant is low in volume terms, but it is a reference point for compliant-arm semi-humanoids in healthcare-adjacent applications.
