## Company background
Perceptyne is a Hyderabad deep-tech startup building "dexterous, affordable, contextually aware" robots for assembly-line work, founded in 2021 by Raviteja Chivukula, Jagga Raju N and Mrutyunjaya Sastry. Its flagship **PR-34D** is a **stationary** (pillar/pedestal-mounted) dual-arm semi-humanoid — 34 DoF from two 7-DoF arms plus two 10-DoF articulated hands — with all-joint force sensing and high-resolution tactile fingertips, aimed squarely at **electronics and automotive assembly**: fine insertion, fastening and handling tasks that conventional SCARA/cobot cells can't do without retooling. A single-arm **PR-9D** shares the stack for simpler stations [S2][S3]. The design pitch is drop-in deployment "without changing existing assembly-line infrastructure" [S1].

The company raised a **$3M seed round in October 2024** co-led by Endiya Partners and Yali Capital (India's deep-tech-focused funds) with Whiteboard Capital, to fund product development and customer acquisition; it reports working closely with large automotive and electronics manufacturers in India ("Make in India" electronics push is the demand driver) and is associated with NVIDIA's startup ecosystem (site badges) [S2][S4]. Status: commercial pilots / early production; listed price point ~$120K for PR-34D [S1] (third-party listing).

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
Perceptyne represents the "stationary semi-humanoid workcell" category — dual anthropomorphic arms + dexterous tactile hands without any mobility — competing on price (~$120K) and infrastructure-free retrofit for Indian electronics manufacturing, a segment global players (Apptronik, Figure, Chinese vendors) approach with mobile humanoids instead. For an EU entrant it is less a direct competitor than a signal: Indian OEMs may prefer cheap stationary dexterity over expensive mobile platforms, and Perceptyne could become the local default in that niche.
