## Company background
Noble Machines is a Sunnyvale seed startup (founded 2024 as Under Control Robotics) building a rugged legged humanoid, Moby, for physically demanding and hazardous industrial work — construction, manufacturing, semiconductor, energy, mining, logistics [S1][S4]. The founding team (ex-Apple/SpaceX/NASA/Caltech) built its third-generation humanoid from scratch in four months and claims first deployment at a Fortune Global 500 industrial customer within 18 months of launch; the customer testimonial emphasizes payload: "the only company that ships robots to us with the payload capacity we're looking for" [S1][S4, vendor-claimed]. Technology focus is whole-body AI control with rapid multi-modal learning — natural-language instruction, physical demonstration and gestures — plus teleoperation; edge compute on NVIDIA Jetson Orin [S5]. Partners listed: Schaeffler, NVIDIA, ADLINK, Solomon, Diverge, SHMZ (Shimizu?) [S4]. Go-to-market is two-track: a developer "Platform" and limited RaaS pilots run by Noble's own team.

**Substance check (assignment question):** Real and reasonably credible for its stage — named CEO, institutional micro-VCs, The Robot Report coverage, night-time worksite photos of hardware handling lumber, and a Schaeffler partner logo. But the Fortune-G500 "deployment" is a vendor claim without a named customer, specs conflict across sources (payload 9 vs 23 vs 27 kg), and pricing ($75k) comes only from an unverified aggregator.

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
**Largely out of scope for a wheeled semi-humanoid study**: despite the discovery sweep listing Moby as wheeled, all primary evidence shows a bipedal legged robot (walking speed 0.8 m/s, stairs/scaffolding, shoes on its feet). Include as periphery: it competes for the same industrial budgets and shows US seed-stage teams reaching G500 pilots quickly. Threat to an EU wheeled entrant: indirect only.
