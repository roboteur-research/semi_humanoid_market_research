## Design & morphology
150 cm, 60 kg; friendly cream/teal design: face-display head, humanoid torso
with two arms, slim articulated spine/waist column rising from a rounded box
wheeled base (image). 16 DoF total, 6 hand DoF, 10 fingers. Aluminum frame with
soft-touch polymer covers — deliberate soft-contact styling for care settings
[S1][S4].

## Locomotion
Wheeled base (drive type undisclosed; low box chassis with concealed wheels);
max 4 km/h (1.1 m/s); "quiet operation" emphasized [S1].

## Upper body & manipulation
Two arms with 5-finger hands (6 hand DoF — underactuated); payload 5 kg total.
Tasks claimed: feeding assistance, dressing help, object retrieval — high-contact
manipulation claims that far exceed demonstrated evidence at prototype stage
[S1][S2]. Reach/repeatability n/a.

## Sensing
Head sensor bar (cameras behind visor strip); details undisclosed. Voice
interaction/natural conversation; wayfinding implies depth/lidar navigation
sensors on base (not specified) [S1][S2].

## Actuation & power
Brushless DC servo motors (guide estimate) with harmonic/planetary reducers per
arm joint; battery capacity n/a; runtime ~5 h per charge [S1].

## Compute & software
Compute undisclosed. LLM integration: yes (conversation/companionship); WiFi,
Bluetooth, facility-network integration (nurse-call/records integration
implied); modular skill-training/data-collection platform pitch [S1][S2].

## Safety & compliance
"Safe with humans" (guide attribute); soft covers, quiet/gentle behavioral
design. No ISO 13482 or medical-device certification claims found — notable gap
for the care segment [S1].

## Deployment evidence & traction
None found. No facility pilots, customers or unit counts; company is 2 people,
YC Spring 2026 [S2].

## Assessment (analyst view)
*Analyst opinion.* Tobi's positioning is smart — elder care is underserved,
labor-starved and value-tolerant, and the soft, quiet, $50k package is pitched
well. But a 2-person team attempting feeding/dressing manipulation (safety-
critical, body-contact tasks) faces certification and liability walls that
usually take years (ISO 13482 at minimum, likely medical-adjacent approvals).
Threat to an EU entrant: minimal directly; strategic lesson is high — an EU
company with existing safety-certification muscle could own this niche in
Europe before US startups mature.
