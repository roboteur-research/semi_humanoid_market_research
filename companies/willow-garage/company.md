## Company background
Willow Garage was a robotics research lab founded in late 2006 by Scott Hassan to accelerate personal robotics. Hassan recruited Stanford's Keenan Wyrobek and Eric Berger, who had built the PR1 prototype and were fundraising for a common open robot platform plus an open-source robot OS. At Willow, Berger led software (ROS, first stable release Feb 2009) while Wyrobek designed the PR2 hardware (first prototypes late 2008) [S1]. The 2010 PR2 Beta Program gave 11 robots free to universities worldwide; commercial sales at $400k began September 2010 [S1]. Willow never pursued profitability seriously; it was an ecosystem play, and it worked: ROS became the universal research (and increasingly commercial) robotics middleware.

In February 2013 Hassan announced Willow would seek external funding/commercial footing; instead it dissolved through 2013-14. ROS stewardship moved to the spun-out Open Source Robotics Foundation (2012); most remaining staff moved to Hassan's Suitable Technologies (Beam telepresence). Clearpath Robotics (Canada) took over PR2 hardware support from January 2014 [S2]. Alumni founded or led Fetch Robotics (Melonee Wise), Savioke/Relay (Steve Cousins), Unbounded Robotics, hiDOF, Industrial Perception (acquired by Google 2013) and Redwood Robotics — the "Willow diaspora" that shaped US service robotics for a decade [S3].

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
PR2 is the archetype of the wheeled dual-arm "semi-humanoid": omni base, telescoping spine, two 7-DoF arms — the same morphology today's commercial entrants (Memo, MOVO, TIAGo++, Galaxea R1, etc.) reuse at lower cost. Willow proved the platform-plus-open-software strategy and left the entire industry standardized on ROS. As a defunct company it poses no competitive threat, but any EU entrant inherits (and competes within) the ecosystem Willow created, and the PR2's fate is the canonical cautionary tale: a $400k research platform cannot become a business.
