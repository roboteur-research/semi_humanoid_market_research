## Company background
Ultra is a Brooklyn YC-S24 startup founded by serial YC hardware founders (Layer By Layer, Voodoo Manufacturing) with the stated mission of "building the next million robots to automate American warehouses" [S1]. Its product, Operator OP1, is a stationary AI workcell: a large positioning arm carrying a dual-arm manipulation head (official imagery shows two smaller 6/7-axis arms with grippers plus a stereo depth camera riding on one big white arm), standing in a 5×5 ft footprint on locking casters, covering a 10×10 ft work area and 0-10 ft vertical reach with ~10 lb payload — plugged into a normal 120 V outlet [S3]. Jared Friedman (YC) noted the team built its first AI packaging robot "in less than two months" [S2]. Autonomy comes from imitation learning on teleoperation data ("trained with examples"), targeting e-commerce packing, sorting, kitting and returns [S1][S3].

Business model is RaaS at $2,500-3,000/month with a 3-month trial, deployment "in hours, not weeks" with no fixed infrastructure [S3][S4]. Flagship deployment: Highline Commerce, a Brooklyn/Atlanta 3PL, where OP1 units automate ~30% of a linear pick-tote-scan-conveyor fulfillment workflow in live commercial operation — notable as humanoid-style automation outside the Amazon ecosystem, with "zero down days" claimed at customer sites [S4].

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
**Borderline case, as flagged in discovery: OP1 is a caster-mounted stationary workstation, not a mobile semi-humanoid** — no locomotion, no torso morphology; it competes with fixed pick/pack automation as much as with humanoids. It is included because it delivers the same labor proposition (dual-arm manipulation of human tasks, teleop-to-autonomy, RaaS) at $30-36k/year — a price/efficacy benchmark any mobile semi-humanoid must beat in packing/kitting use cases. Trajectory: scaling US 3PL deployments.
