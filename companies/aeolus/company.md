## Company background
Aeolus Robotics is a US-incorporated, internationally distributed service-robot company founded in 2016 by Alexander Huang, formerly a Microsoft regional director in Greater China [S3][S4]. Engineering is spread across Taiwan (Taipei) and Europe — a Vienna, Austria R&D office focused on arm motion planning and grasping (with links to TU Wien's ACIN institute) plus software roles in Wroclaw, Poland [S5]. The company first showed a humanoid "robot maid" at CES 2018 (3D + 5 MP cameras, interchangeable arms, vacuuming/fetching demos), then converged on Aeo, a dual-arm wheeled service humanoid unveiled in its current form at CES 2023 [S2][S4].

The business model is Robot-as-a-Service: leasing/subscription with continuous support, sold as task packages (Aeo Disinfect — UV-C; Aeo Care — eldercare monitoring; Aeo Delivery; Aeo Security) rather than as hardware [S1]. Commercial deployments began in Japan in 2019 and now span Japan, Hong Kong and Taipei [S2]. Japanese traction is the standout: major eldercare operators Medical Care Service Inc., Gakken's Cocofump and HIMEDIC run Aeo units, alongside property-management deployments with Tokyu Group and Globeship; distribution runs through Marubun Corporation [S1][S2]. Notably, several of these customers (Gakken Cocofump, Saint-Care, MSC) are also investors — a strategic-capital-plus-deployment loop unusual in this segment. Third-party attachment/integration partners include Asratec (Japan) and Malibu AI (Taiwan) [S2].

Funding disclosed is modest (~$30M), and the company keeps hardware specs deliberately vague publicly; its marketing leads with verified capabilities — autonomous door opening and, per the company, "the only robot in the world that can get on and off elevators autonomously" [S1].

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
Aeolus is one of very few companies with a dual-arm wheeled humanoid in paying, multi-year commercial service — specifically Japanese eldercare, the sector most EU semi-humanoid business cases cite. It proves the RaaS-with-task-packages model and the value of door/elevator capability for building-wide autonomy, but also illustrates the constraint: light 7-DoF arms (~3.6 kg lift) confine it to disinfection, patrol and light delivery rather than physical care work. For an EU entrant, Aeolus is less a technology benchmark than a go-to-market benchmark (operator-investors, distributor model, per-task subscriptions); its Vienna R&D presence also makes it a competitor for scarce EU manipulation talent. Trajectory: steady niche scaling, not hyperscaling — public activity has been quiet since the CES 2023 push.
