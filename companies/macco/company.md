## Company background
Macco Robotics, founded 2017 in Seville, builds beverage- and food-serving robots whose signature product **Kime** is a humanoid head-torso-arms unit sealed inside a ~2 m² service kiosk: customers order via app/screen, and Kime's two articulated arms pull beer taps, angle glasses and hand over drinks through a hatch. It gained fame during the pandemic when Seville bars used Kime to pour and deliver beers contact-free (Euronews, Feb 2021), and through a trial with Portuguese fuel retailer **Prio**, which tested a kiosk at a gas station and planned to rotate it between stations while expanding the menu to fountain drinks, milkshakes and fresh food [S1][S2][S3]. A Spanish beer brand also used a rolling-cart Kime for festivals/events [S1].

The business model is kiosk sale/lease to hospitality and retail venues (pricing not public; historical reporting suggested leasing). Kime versions have iterated to v5, with claimed service rates up to 300 drinks/hour, 22 DoF, NVIDIA Jetson Xavier NX compute and 3D lidar/RGB-D sensing in later spec sheets [S4]. The company remains active per 2026 database tracking, but with a low public profile, no disclosed funding and no announced large rollouts — it appears to remain a niche, project-based Spanish vendor [S5].

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
Macco represents the kiosk-humanoid niche: humanoid form used as brand theater around a fundamentally fixed vending/dispensing machine. Its Prio pilot is a rare European example of semi-humanoid tech in fuel retail. For an EU entrant, Macco is not a manipulation/mobility competitor; the takeaway is market evidence that hospitality buyers value humanoid presentation but only at vending-machine economics — a warning about the razor-thin ROI in food & beverage service.
