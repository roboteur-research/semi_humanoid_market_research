## Company background
Genrobotics is Kerala's flagship deep-tech startup, famous for **Bandicoot** (launched 2018) — a pneumatic/electric manhole-cleaning robot with a lowered arm unit that scoops sewage waste, created explicitly to **end manual scavenging** in India. Bandicoot units operate across **22 Indian states and 4 countries**, procured largely by state governments and water authorities; the mission won the founders a PMO invitation, Anand Mahindra's personal investment, and Zoho's ₹20 crore backing [S2][S4][S5]. The company sits at the center of Kerala's startup ecosystem (Technopark Trivandrum, Kerala Startup Mission support) and has diversified into medical rehab (G-Gaiter exoskeleton) and industrial cleaning.

**GenBot** is its R&D moonshot beyond sanitation: a **tracked semi-humanoid** — torso + dual arms + sensor head on rubberized tracks with adaptive multi-terrain suspension — positioned for industrial logistics, perimeter security patrol and inspection in harsh outdoor environments. Vendor-published capability targets: 18 DoF, 200 kg payload, torso extension to 2.5 m, 8 h battery, 3 km/h off-road, 3D lidar + depth cameras, 5G/satellite-ready, modular hot-swap components. Status: **pilot production of the core mobility platform with integrated field testing**; Phase 3 of the roadmap adds AI navigation (non-GPS path planning) and **dual-arm manipulation** [S1].

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
GenBot is one of the very few **tracked** semi-humanoids tracked in this study — a differentiated answer to outdoor/multi-terrain use cases (construction, mining perimeters, waste sites) that wheeled platforms avoid. Genrobotics brings credible harsh-environment engineering experience (sewer robotics is brutal on hardware) and government procurement channels, but limited capital and no demonstrated manipulation autonomy yet. Trajectory: scaling Bandicoot revenue while incubating GenBot; a niche/regional player to watch rather than a near-term competitor for indoor factory semi-humanoids.
