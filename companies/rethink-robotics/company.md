## Company background
Founded by Rodney Brooks in 2008 to bring safe, cheap, trainable robots to manufacturing, Rethink launched Baxter in September 2012: a $22k dual-arm cobot with a screen face and series-elastic actuators, trained by physically guiding its arms instead of programming [S1]. Baxter was a media sensation and a research hit (Baxter Research Edition), but too slow and imprecise for real industrial work; the 2015 single-arm Sawyer ($29k, harmonic-precision class) was the corrective pivot. Rethink burned ~$150M and shut down abruptly on 3 October 2018 after a last-minute acquisition fell through; Germany's HAHN Group bought the IP and trademarks, continuing Sawyer (BLACK edition) via Rethink Robotics GmbH [S2][S3].

Under HAHN's United Robotics Group (URG), Rethink was relaunched at IMTS in September 2024, moved back to the US, and unveiled Reacher cobot arms, Ryder AMRs and the Riser mobile manipulator — notably with NO dual-arm/humanoid product [S4]. The revival was short-lived: with URG's collapse/restructuring, Rethink Robotics Inc. shut down for a second time in August 2025; the fate of the IP and new models is unresolved [S5].

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
Baxter remains the only mass-produced sub-$25k dual-arm "semi-humanoid" ever brought to industrial market, and its failure is the segment's central cautionary tale: human-safe compliance and low price could not compensate for low speed/precision and unclear ROI. For an EU entrant, Rethink is (a) proof there is real demand pull for affordable human-shaped cobots in research/education, (b) a warning about industrial expectations, and (c) no longer a competitor — twice dead, with the German owner (URG/HAHN) itself dismantled.
