# Robo-C / Robo-C2 — Promobot

> Robo-C (and its successor Robo-C2) was Promobot's stationary hyper-realistic android: a seated/pedestal humanoid with a customizable human face capable of 600+ expressions and articulated 12-DoF arms for gestures and light object handling (~1 kg). Sold since 2019 as concierges, celebrity doubles and exhibits, the line is notable as one of very few commercially sold realistic androids — but as of 2026 Promobot's site declares "The humanoid project is over", making Robo-C a discontinued benchmark rather than an active competitor.

## Design & morphology
Stationary android: human-scale head and torso with silicone skin face, mounted on a fixed pedestal/seated base — no locomotion [S1][S2]. Face: "mechanical muscles" driving eyes, eyebrows, lips — 600+ facial expressions; appearance fully customizable, able to "recreate any person's appearance" (used for doubles of public figures) [S1]. Torso shell over aluminum/3D-printed frame; faces produced in-house by 3D printing and silicone casting (see production image) [S3].

## Locomotion
None (stationary pedestal android). n/a.

## Upper body & manipulation
Robo-C2: two articulated arms with 12 DoF per arm (discovery/third-party); light manipulation ~1 kg (e.g., handing small items); primarily gesture and expression support [S2][S4]. No grippers/tool interfaces; anthropomorphic hands (often gloved).

## Sensing
Cameras in eyes for face recognition and eye contact; microphone array; speech recognition — same interactive stack as V.4 [S1][S2]. Force/tactile n/a (not disclosed).

## Actuation & power
Electric micro-servos for facial "muscles" (up to ~18 facial actuators per third-party reports) and arm servos; mains-powered (stationary); battery n/a [S2, estimated].

## Compute & software
Linux + Promobot proprietary dialogue/recognition stack; remembers people and names, holds open-ended conversation, integrates company knowledge bases [S1]. Closed source; no SDK.

## Safety & compliance
n/a (stationary interactive device; no certifications published).

## Deployment evidence & traction
Sold since 2019 in small numbers worldwide: government service halls (a Robo-C "civil servant" double), museums, banks, expos; US office marketed C2 doubles (including an Einstein-style android); Perm State Medical University patient-simulator based on Robo-C (2025) [S3][S5]. Total units n/a (not disclosed; likely low dozens, estimated). Line discontinued by 2026 [S1].

## Assessment (analyst view)
*Analyst opinion.* Robo-C proved there is a niche (exhibits, doubles, simulators) for realistic androids at ~$20-50k, but its discontinuation confirms the niche is too small and service-heavy to sustain a product line — facial hardware is expensive, uncanny-valley reception is mixed, and the arms add cost without useful manipulation. Zero competitive threat to an EU semi-humanoid entrant; relevant only as evidence against investing in hyper-realistic faces for service robots.
