## Company background
Pudu Robotics, founded 2016 in Shenzhen by Felix Zhang, is one of the two global leaders in commercial service robots (with Keenon): 80,000+ robots shipped to 60+ countries — mostly restaurant delivery (BellaBot) and cleaning units [vendor-claimed]. It rode the post-COVID service-robot wave to global distribution: strong overseas dealer network across Japan, Korea, Europe and North America, a large RaaS/leasing business, and consumer-brand recognition unusual for robotics.

In September 2024 Pudu opened a "third curve" beyond delivery and cleaning by unveiling the D7 and explicitly coining the category "semi-humanoid" (半人形机器人) — a deliberate positioning claim that a wheeled chassis plus dual 7-DoF arms is the commercially rational humanoid form factor. It followed with the D9 full-size biped (12/2024, 170 cm/65 kg/42 DoF) and the DH11 11-DoF dexterous hand, giving it a spectrum: specialized robots → semi-humanoid (D7) → humanoid (D9). Company messaging frames the D7 as the near-term deployable product and the D9 as a technology flagship [vendor-claimed/third-party].

Funding: >$300M cumulative; ~$150M raised 2026-04-23 at >$1.5B valuation with an IPO planned — modest war chest vs AgiBot/Galbot but backed by real service-robot revenue and a global channel that none of the humanoid startups have [third-party].

A second semi-humanoid line arrived in March 2025: the FlashBot Arm (announced 2025-03-31 after a CES 2025 showing) mounts dual liftable 7-DoF arms with PUDU DH11 11-DoF dexterous hands on the field-proven FlashBot multi-floor hotel-delivery base (144 cm, ~70 kg, 15 kg payload, 2 m workspace reach, 8 h runtime, VSLAM+lidar). Unlike the ground-up D7, FlashBot Arm inherits Pudu's existing elevator/door IoT integrations and hotel install base, making it arguably the company's more immediately commercial semi-humanoid: it is in pilot/pre-order distribution through Pudu's dealer network at a reported ~$34k [vendor-claimed specs; price third-party]. See [flashbot-arm/robot.md](flashbot-arm/robot.md).

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
Pudu matters for two reasons. First, terminology leadership: "semi-humanoid" — now common in category discourse (and this study's title) — is Pudu's coinage. Second, channel power: an 80k-robot install base and worldwide dealer/service network is a ready-made distribution channel for semi-humanoids into hotels, restaurants and facilities — exactly the segments where D7-class robots will land first. If the D7 reaches price-performance maturity, Pudu can push it through existing accounts faster than any competitor. For an EU entrant, Pudu is the most direct threat in European hospitality/facility channels, where it already has sales and service presence.
