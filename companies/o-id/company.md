## Company background
O-ID is a Japanese robotics manufacturer building "fully modular humanoid
robots for continuous operation". The company's differentiators are
field-serviceability and modularity: swappable, customizable component modules
intended to allow on-site maintenance and field upgrades without extended
downtime, plus a "supply chain security" pitch built on domestic Japanese
manufacturing with shipment from Tokyo [S2]. Public information is sparse — no
founder, funding or customer data surfaced in this research pass.

Its product, plainly named "Modular", is a wheeled dual-arm humanoid (172 cm,
70 kg, 30 DoF, 16 kg payload) rated by the vendor for 24/7 uptime in warehouse
and factory settings, listed at USD 70k in early production / limited release
[S1]. Positioning is deliberately anti-spectacle: a practical, reliable
industrial tool rather than a research showcase [S2].

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
Directly relevant: a Japanese industrial wheeled dual-arm platform at $70k with
a modularity/serviceability thesis very close to what an EU industrial entrant
would pitch (uptime, field service, supply-chain sovereignty — the Japanese
mirror of a German value proposition). Evidence maturity is low (unverified
listing, no deployments found), but the strategic positioning deserves
tracking as a like-for-like competitor concept.
