## Company background
1X began in 2014 as Halodi Robotics in Moss, Norway, around a single technical conviction: humanoid robots need low-inertia, low-friction, back-drivable actuation rather than high-ratio harmonic gearboxes. Its proprietary Revo1 motor drives tendon-based joints that give the robots "human-inspired musculature" — durable enough for work, soft enough to operate around people [S1, vendor-claimed]. The wheeled android EVE (first demonstrated publicly in 2017) was the company's proving ground: by 2021 Halodi had ~$3M in pipeline orders, mostly in security; ADT invested in April 2021, and a March 2022 agreement had ADT Commercial leasing 140 EVEs per year at ~NOK 500,000 per unit per year (~$7M/yr revenue) for night-guarding duties — later marketed by Everon (ex-ADT Commercial) under the "EvoGuard" autonomous-guarding brand launched at CES 2023 [S1][S5, third-party; unit totals estimated 150-250].

In March 2023 Halodi rebranded as 1X Technologies, raised $23.5M led by the OpenAI Startup Fund, and shifted strategy from commercial pilots to consumer humanoids — Børnich's stated logic being that "the home and consumer has to happen first" to reach the data scale embodied AI needs [S1]. The $100M Series B (01/2024, EQT Ventures, Samsung NEXT) funded NEO, a soft-bodied bipedal home robot announced at $20,000 or $499/month with first US deliveries targeted for late 2026 (teleop-assisted early on). EQT's agreement reportedly covers up to 10,000 NEOs for 2026-30 [S2][S3]. 1X also absorbed the team of Kind Humanoid (2024) [S6]. Manufacturing remains in Norway with US expansion; go-to-market for EVE was RaaS (hardware+software+maintenance bundled), for NEO direct-to-consumer purchase/subscription.

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
1X is the clearest case study of a company that succeeded with a wheeled semi-humanoid — EVE was among the largest real-world humanoid fleets in the West (est. 150-250 units night-guarding US commercial buildings) — and then deliberately walked away from the form factor for a legged consumer bet. EVE is no longer marketed; its fleet now functions mainly as a data engine for the Redwood VLA behind NEO [S1][S4][S5]. For an EU entrant this cuts both ways: it validates wheeled platforms as the fastest route to deployed fleets, cheap 24/7 runtime and certification (EVE cannot fall over when powered down), while also vacating the European wheeled-security/logistics niche that 1X (a Norwegian company!) once occupied — an opening, since no Western vendor currently ships a wheeled security semi-humanoid at scale.
