# uBot (uBot-5 / uBot-6 / uBot-7) — UMass Amherst LPR

> The uBots were toddler-sized, two-wheel dynamically balancing dual-arm mobile manipulators from UMass Amherst's Laboratory for Perceptual Robotics — a two-decade academic study of the balancing semi-humanoid form factor, ending with uBot-7's series-elastic build. Research only; effectively retired.

uBot-5 was a 13-DoF dynamic balancer: two drive wheels, rotatable trunk, two 4-DoF arms with unactuated spherical "hands", and a stereo pan/tilt head; it demonstrated whole-body control learning, postural transitions (prone to standing, "pushups") and was studied as a teleoperated first-responder/trauma-assessment platform [S1][S3]. uBot-6 kept the toddler scale but roughly tripled joint strength and supported multiple postural configurations and mobility modes (dynamic balancing plus statically stable stances using its arms), enabling tasks like box flipping under stochastic-action experiments [S2]. uBot-7 (14 DoF, 95 cm, ~27 kg) replaced all arm and torso actuation with series-elastic actuators for force sensing and human-safe interaction — claimed as the first wheeled dynamically balancing mobile manipulator with SEAs [S4]. The series is the most persistent academic exploration of the small balancing dual-arm morphology; its base concept also contributed to MIT's Nexi/MDS robot.
