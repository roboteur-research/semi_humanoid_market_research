## Company background
UMass Amherst's Laboratory for Perceptual Robotics (Rod Grupen) developed the uBot series — small, two-wheel dynamically balancing dual-arm mobile manipulators — as a long-running "personal robot" concept study. uBot-5 (~2007) was a 13-DoF toddler-sized balancer with two 4-DoF arms used for whole-body control learning and emergency-response/telemedicine studies; uBot-6 (~2012) tripled joint strength and added postural transitions between balancing and statically stable modes; uBot-7 (2017-18) rebuilt all arm and torso joints with series-elastic actuators — per its paper the first wheeled dynamic balancer with SEAs [S1][S2][S3]. The balancing-toddler concept also fed MIT's Nexi/MDS program, whose mobile base was developed with UMass. Activity on the series has since wound down (Grupen is emeritus).

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
Academic lineage only, but a rare sustained study of the two-wheel balancing dual-arm form factor at small scale — relevant prior art for anyone weighing balancing bases (1X EVE-style) against statically stable ones, and for low-cost compliant actuation in mobile manipulators.
