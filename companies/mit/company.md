## Company background
MIT produced three generations of influential upper-body/semi-humanoid research robots. **Cog** (Brooks' Humanoid Robotics Group, 1993-2003) was the landmark embodied-AI torso: human-scale head/torso/arms used to argue that intelligence requires a body; it never manipulated usefully but set the agenda (and is now in the MIT Museum). **Domo** (2004-07) was Aaron Edsinger's doctoral platform — a fully force-controlled compliant dual-arm torso (SEAs in every joint) for manipulation in human environments, sponsored by Toyota and NASA [S1]. **Nexi** (Media Lab Personal Robots Group, 2008) was the "MDS" — Mobile, Dexterous, Social — robot: an expressive social head and small dual arms on a two-wheel dynamically balancing base developed with UMass Amherst (uBot lineage) and Xitome Design; it became famous for emotive HRI studies rather than manipulation [S3].

The commercial thread: Brooks co-founded iRobot and later Rethink (Baxter); Edsinger and Weber founded Meka Robotics (acquired by Google 2013), and Edsinger later founded Hello Robot (Stretch) — making the Domo→Meka→Hello Robot line one of the clearest lab-to-market lineages in mobile manipulation.

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
Historic/academic. Domo's all-SEA force-controlled arms directly seeded Meka's products (and thus Google's robotics program), and MIT's compliant-manipulation philosophy is embedded in today's cobot-grade semi-humanoid arms. No current competing platform.
