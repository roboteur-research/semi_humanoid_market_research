# Ameca — Engineered Arts

> Ameca is the world's most recognizable expressive humanoid: a pedestal-mounted (non-walking), grey-skinned robot whose Mesmer-derived face and 50+ axes of articulation deliver uncanny human expression, driven by LLM conversation (GPT-class) or telepresence. Sold quote-only (~$130k–250k, custom builds to $500k) and widely rented for events, it dominates the interaction/entertainment niche of the semi-humanoid market rather than manipulation or mobility.

## Design & morphology
Human-scale upper body (≈187 cm standing) on a fixed pedestal/base — **does not walk or drive**. Gen 2.6 adds articulated but non-walking legs (standing poses); full-body units are mains-powered and static [S1][S2]. ~52 axes of articulation total, concentrated in face (~15+), neck, arms and hands; grey "genderless" silicone-free aesthetic to avoid uncanny valley [S2][S3]. Modular: head-only, torso, or full-body configurations.

## Locomotion
None (pedestal). Mobility explicitly out of scope; company positions expression as the product. (Ameca Desktop Gen 3: table-top head+shoulders unit.)

## Upper body & manipulation
Articulated arms and multi-fingered hands used for gesturing, pointing, drawing demos — not payload manipulation; no payload rating published (effectively ~0.5-1 kg gesture-class, estimated). No end-effector tooling or flange media (n/a).

## Sensing
Binocular eye-mounted cameras + chest camera; embedded microphone array; facial-recognition and person-tracking software [S2][S3]. No lidar/navigation sensors (static robot).

## Actuation & power
Electric actuators throughout (earlier RoboThespian heritage was partly pneumatic; Ameca is fully electric). Mains-powered; no battery in standard full-body config [S2].

## Compute & software
Proprietary **Tritium** robot operating system (browser-based control, cloud-managed content) on Mesmer hardware platform; conversational AI via integrated LLMs (GPT-3/GPT-4-class, configurable) or scripted/telepresence control [S2][S3]. Public API/content tools for customers; no ROS.

## Safety & compliance
Static pedestal robot interacting at arm's length; CE marking as machinery/electrical product assumed, no robot-safety (ISO 13482/10218) certification published (n/a). Gesture-force limited arms.

## Deployment evidence & traction
- Permanent/long-term installations: National Robotarium Edinburgh; Computer History Museum, Mountain View; Sphere Las Vegas (via EA robots); GSK trade-show use [S4].
- 200+ robot install base company-wide (mostly RoboThespian) across NASA KSC, National Geographic etc. [S4].
- Rental demand for trade shows/brand events is a core revenue stream; $500k two-unit school sale Jan 2026 [S2].
- Viral reach: >2.3M views flagship video; arguably the most-photographed humanoid at CES since 2022 [S4].

## Assessment (analyst view)
*Analyst opinion.* Strengths: unmatched facial expressiveness and brand recognition, 20-year animatronics moat, profitable-looking niche with rentals, fresh $10M and US base. Weaknesses: zero locomotion/manipulation utility — as task-capable humanoids proliferate, "face-only" robots risk commoditization by cheaper desktop social bots; price is very high for what is functionally a conversational kiosk. Threat to a new EU entrant: low in industrial/care segments; moderate in reception/visitor-experience deals where Ameca's fame wins tenders. An entrant should not compete on expressiveness — partner or concede that niche.
