## Company background
Engineered Arts was founded in 2004 by Will Jackson in Falmouth, Cornwall, out of science-museum exhibition work; its first product RoboThespian (2005, for the Eden Project's Mechanical Theatre) became the standard entertainment/visitor-attraction humanoid with **200+ robots deployed globally** — customers include NASA Kennedy Space Center, National Geographic and The Sphere Las Vegas [S3][S4]. Its Mesmer line contributed hyper-realistic silicone heads and facial actuation. **Ameca** (unveiled Dec 2021, viral with >2.3M YouTube views) fused this expressiveness with GPT-based conversation to become the world's best-known "social" humanoid face [S3].

The business model is unusual: quote-only sales (~$130k–250k standard full body; head-only configs from ~$25k; enterprise customizations to ~$500k) plus a substantial **rental/events business** for trade shows and attractions [S2]. Ameca does not walk — full-body units are pedestal/standing static and mains-powered (Gen 2.6 has non-walking legs); mobility is explicitly not the product. In December 2024 the company restructured under a US parent, raised $10M Series A (Helium-3 Ventures lead), and opened a Redwood City site (~20 hires) to scale production and US sales [S3]. In June 2026 it released **Ameca Desktop (Gen 3)** — a head-and-shoulders unit for research/education/brand engagement [S1][S2].

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
Engineered Arts owns the expression/interaction niche: no manipulation, no locomotion, but unmatched facial animation and public-facing charisma — the robot most consumers picture when they hear "humanoid". For an EU entrant building working semi-humanoids, Engineered Arts is not a task-automation competitor but competes for the same HRI/reception budgets in museums, retail flagships and events, and sets public expectations for what robot "faces" should do. Trajectory: scaling US commercial ops; doubling down on the entertainment/education niche rather than pivoting to utility.
