## Company background
Wandercraft was founded in Paris in 2012 by Nicolas Simon, Matthieu Masselin and Jean-Louis Constanza — motivated by family members with Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease — to build self-balancing walking exoskeletons. Its Atalante X rehabilitation exoskeleton (launched 2019) is used in ~100 rehabilitation and clinical research centers worldwide and holds two FDA clearances (stroke 2024; spinal cord injury and multiple sclerosis extension Nov 2025). The personal exoskeleton Eve entered clinical trials in Feb 2025 with a US launch targeted for 2026 from its New York hub [S1][S5].

In 2025 Wandercraft leveraged a decade of dynamic bipedal-balance control into industrial humanoids: Calvin (first generation, April 2025) and Calvin-40 — a headless biped "developed in 40 days" on NVIDIA's AI/Isaac stack. In June 2025 Renault Group took a minority stake (the $75M Series D) to industrialize the Calvin family via design-to-cost and scaling, and began deploying Calvin-40 at its Douai EV plant (Renault 5 line) — reported as the first production-line humanoid deployment by a European automaker, with 350 units planned within ~18 months (by 2027) [S2][S3][S4]. Sapa Group (plastic components) signed as a second industrial customer in late 2025 [S6].

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
**Scope note: Calvin-40 is a two-legged (bipedal) robot — EXCLUDED from the semi-humanoid (wheeled/pedestal) category.** The earlier survey-sweep claim of a wheeled base is wrong (verified against Renault/press primary reporting: "first two-legged, headless robot"). Wandercraft still matters to this study as (a) the benchmark European humanoid deployment — a real automotive production line, 350-unit commitment, $65k reported price — that any wheeled semi-humanoid entrant will be compared against on cost and uptime; and (b) proof that European industrial customers (Renault, Sapa) will buy domestic humanoids at scale. Trajectory: scaling up fast, dual-track medical + industrial.
