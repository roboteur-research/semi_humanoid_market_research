## Company background
Hanson Robotics is the world's best-known maker of hyper-realistic social androids. Founder David Hanson invented "Frubber," a patented elastomer skin whose low actuation force lets small servos produce lifelike facial expressions. Sophia, activated 2016, became a global media phenomenon: Saudi citizenship (Oct 2017, first robot "citizen"), first non-human UNDP Innovation Champion (Nov 2017), Tonight Show appearances, and hundreds of conference keynotes [S1][S4]. The business model is closer to "android as media/ambassador platform" than product sales: revenue mixes appearance fees, R&D-unit sales (a "Sophia 2020 R&D Version" was listed via RobotShop, quote-on-request), custom androids, and the Little Sophia consumer toy [S3][S5].

In January 2021 the company announced mass-production plans ("thousands of robots in 2021") across four models — this scale never materialized (analyst assessment; no evidence of high-volume delivery). Grace, a nurse-assistant android launched with SingularityNET's Awakening Health JV (2021), targeted eldercare pilots in Hong Kong/Korea. As of 2025-26 Sophia remains active but is not a retail product: Hanson marked Sophia's tenth birthday (Feb 2026) and exhibited "Sophia and Robert" with RB Labs at the ITU AI for Good Global Summit (Geneva, July 2026) [S2]. Claimed price figures of $100-150k circulating online trace to content farms; no official price has ever been published [S2].

Academic and industry criticism is substantial: AI researchers (most vocally Yann LeCun, who called Sophia's presentation "complete b***t" / "Potemkin AI") argue Sophia's conversation is largely scripted/chatbot-driven and that citizenship stunts mislead the public about AI capability; Hanson counters that Sophia is a research and arts platform (she runs a hybrid of scripted dialogue, chatbot and, recently, LLM integration) [S4][S6].

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
Hanson defined the hyper-realistic-android niche and still owns its media mindshare, but it is not a manipulation or logistics player: Sophia's arms are expressive, payload negligible, volumes tiny. Trajectory is flat — an events/ambassador business with periodic AI-upgrade announcements. For an EU semi-humanoid entrant, Hanson is irrelevant as a competitor but instructive on (a) the PR power and backlash risk of humanoid hype, and (b) the small real market behind celebrity androids.
