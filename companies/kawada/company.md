## Company background
Kawada Robotics carries Japan's deepest continuous humanoid-manufacturing lineage. Parent **Kawada Industries** (a bridge-construction group with an aircraft division) was the hardware builder of the national **HRP humanoid program** with AIST/METI — HRP-2 "Promet" (2002), HRP-3, HRP-4 — and translated that expertise into **NEXTAGE** (2009): a pedestal-mounted, dual-arm, headed "hito-gata" (human-form) industrial robot for cell production, i.e. flexible many-variety/small-lot assembly lines where robots take over individual human workstations without line rebuilds [S1][S4]. The robotics business was spun into Kawada Robotics on 1 April 2013 [S1]. A research derivative, **HIRO**, seeded university labs (ROS support via TORK) before being discontinued — the ancestor of today's "OPEN" research SKUs.

NEXTAGE's design premise predated the cobot boom: low-power motors, human footprint, camera-based awareness, safe fence-free coexistence, and above all *re-taskability* — it was pitched as an employee you re-train, not a machine you re-engineer. Deployments accumulated over 15 years across electronics, automotive parts, cosmetics and food manufacturing; a third-party database counts **100+ companies** using NEXTAGE [S3]; the flagship public case is GLORY's Saitama factory cell lines (from ~2011, estimated). Sales are direct plus distributors **THK Intex** (Japan) and **Rollomatic SA** (Switzerland — watch/precision industry) [S2].

In March 2022 Kawada launched **NEXTAGE Fillie** (from ¥6M, options extra) — smaller, cheaper, friendlier-styled, extending from factory cells into reception/customer-facing roles — followed by **Fillie OPEN** as the current research platform [S5]. The company stays deliberately pedestal-bound (no mobile base), betting that most cell-production value needs arms + eyes, not locomotion.

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
Kawada is the world's longest-running proof that torso-form dual-arm robots can hold real manufacturing jobs — 15 years before "semi-humanoid" became a category. Its installed base, Swiss distribution and ¥6M price point define the incumbent benchmark for pedestal semi-humanoids in Japan. Trajectory is steady/niche rather than scaling: no mobility, no VLA-AI story yet, and new AI-native rivals (Yaskawa MOTOMAN NEXT, Chinese dual-arm platforms) are attacking the same cell-production niche. For an EU entrant, Kawada is both a validation reference (customers already accept humanoid-form coworkers) and a warning about how slowly this niche grows without software differentiation.
