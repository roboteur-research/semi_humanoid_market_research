# OriHime-D (オリヒメD) — Ory Laboratory

> OriHime-D is a 120cm wheeled teleoperated avatar robot (分身ロボット) through which people who cannot leave home — ALS patients, the severely disabled, hospitalized or caregiving people — do paid physical service work. It is the serving staff of the Avatar Robot Café DAWN ver.β, permanent in Nihonbashi, Tokyo since 21 June 2021 with 70+ employed remote pilots, winner of Japan's Good Design GRAND Award 2021. Its competitive significance is not hardware but the model: the world's longest-running permanent deployment of teleoperated humanoid service robots, monetizing human presence rather than automation.

## Design & morphology
~120cm tall child-sized humanoid on a wheeled base; white rounded "spirit-like" design language shared with desktop OriHime (deliberately neutral, non-gendered, non-uncanny so the pilot's personality comes through) [S1][S2]. 14 joint motors in the upper body (head + two arms) [S2].

## Locomotion
Wheeled base: drives forward/backward and rotates in place; pilot-controlled navigation around café floor [S2]. Speed/nav autonomy n/a (teleop).

## Upper body & manipulation
Two arms with 14 upper-body joints total; gestures for expressive communication plus simple carrying tasks — trays, paper cups, drink delivery to tables [S2]. No dexterous hands; payload n/a (tray-scale, <1kg estimated).

## Sensing
Camera (wide-angle), microphone and speaker for two-way presence; pilot sees the café and converses through the robot [S1][S2]. (4K-class video quality is documented for the current OriHime line [S3].)

## Actuation & power
Servo joint motors; battery operation. Details n/a (not disclosed).

## Compute & software
Proprietary OriHime teleoperation platform: pilots operate from PC/smartphone/tablet over the internet; accessible input supported (eye-tracking via OriHime eye+switch for ALS pilots — gaze-only operation is a core capability) [S1][S3]. Speech via pilot's own voice or synthesized text-to-speech.

## Safety & compliance
Human-teleoperated at low speed in controlled venues; no certifications published (n/a).

## Deployment evidence & traction
- Avatar Robot Café DAWN ver.β, Nihonbashi Life Science Building, Tokyo — PERMANENT since 2021-06-21; entrance-ticket + OriHime Diner reservation model [S1][S4] (third-party confirmed, extensive international coverage).
- 70+ disabled pilots employed as of 02/2024 (up from ~50 at opening era); pilots work from across Japan, incl. ALS patients [S3] (third-party).
- Good Design GRAND Award 2021 — top prize among all Japanese products, chosen by finalist vote [S4].
- Pop-up experiments since 2018 (WEF, Japan Today coverage); corporate avatar-employment programs (e.g. Microsoft Japan) using the OriHime family [S3][S5].

## Assessment (analyst view)
*Analyst opinion.* OriHime-D wins on meaning, not mechanics: 14 joints and a tray is trivially replicable hardware, but five years of continuous revenue-generating operation, a national design Grand Prix, and a pilot workforce with lived-experience moats make DAWN the reference for socially-licensed teleoperation. For an EU entrant the takeaways are (1) teleop semi-humanoids can be monetized today through inclusion/employment services and hospitality, ahead of autonomy; (2) accessibility-grade interfaces (gaze-only control) are a differentiator no Western semi-humanoid vendor offers. As a competitor OryLab is confined to its niche and to Japan; as a model it is the category's strongest social proof.
