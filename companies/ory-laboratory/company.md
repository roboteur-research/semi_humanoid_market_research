## Company background
Ory Laboratory was founded in 2012 by Kentaro Yoshifuji to solve loneliness and social exclusion — "impossible into possible" — through 分身 (alter-ego/avatar) robots that let people who cannot leave home (ALS, severe disability, hospitalization, caregiving) be present and work remotely. Its small desktop OriHime became widely used in schools, offices (Microsoft Japan employment programs) and homes; the 120cm OriHime-D added mobility and carrying arms so pilots can do physical service work. [S1][S2]

Its flagship proof is the Avatar Robot Café DAWN ver.β: piloted as pop-ups from 2018, it opened as a permanent café in Nihonbashi, Tokyo on 21 June 2021, staffed by disabled pilots teleoperating OriHime and OriHime-D from home across Japan. DAWN won the Good Design GRAND Award 2021 (Japan's top design prize) and grew from ~50 to 70+ employed pilots by February 2024. The company monetizes via café operations, OriHime rentals/subscriptions (docomo kikito), corporate avatar-employment programs, and the FLEMEE staffing service for disability telework. [S2][S3][S4]

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
OryLab defined a business model nobody else in the category has: the semi-humanoid as an employment prosthesis — selling remote human labor participation, not automation. DAWN is the longest-running permanent deployment of teleoperated humanoid service robots in the world and a governance/PR blueprint (Good Design Grand Award, national sympathy) for socially-licensed teleop robots. Technically OriHime-D is modest (14 upper-body joints, tray-carrying), so it poses no hardware threat; its relevance to an EU entrant is the model itself — teleop semi-humanoids monetized through inclusion services and public-sector goodwill.
