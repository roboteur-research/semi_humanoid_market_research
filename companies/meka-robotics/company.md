## Company background
Meka Robotics commercialized the MIT Domo lineage: human-safe, series-elastic, torque-controlled robot modules (arms, hands, heads, torsos, omni bases) sold to research labs, culminating in the M1 mobile manipulator (2011). Its hardware appeared across the era's landmark research robots — Georgia Tech's Cody used Meka A1 arms; UT Austin's HCRL "Dreamer" combined a Meka torso/arms with the anime-eyed S2 head [S1][S2]. In December 2013 Google bought Meka as one of the eight robotics acquisitions forming Andy Rubin's "Replicant" program (alongside Boston Dynamics, SCHAFT, Industrial Perception, Redwood Robotics et al.); the team disappeared into Google's robotics effort, whose mobile-manipulation strand eventually surfaced as Everyday Robots (shut down 2023) [S3]. Edsinger left to found Hello Robot (Stretch) in 2017.

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
Meka defined the "compliant modules for labs" business model TU-era startups (Trossen, Pollen) now run at lower price points, and its acquisition marks the moment big tech began hoarding mobile-manipulation talent. Defunct — relevance is lineage only: Google/Everyday Robots, Hello Robot, and the SEA-based safety approach that today's cobot-grade semi-humanoid arms inherit.
