## Company background
Clone Robotics is the outlier of European humanoid robotics: instead of servomotors it builds faithful musculoskeletal anatomy — polymer bones (206 in the full android), "Myofiber" artificial muscles (McKibben-type contractile actuators), and a water-based hydraulic "vascular" system with an electric pump heart — producing eerily lifelike motion [S1][S2]. Founded 2021 in Wrocław by Dhanush Radhakrishnan and Łukasz Koźlik, it went viral repeatedly with videos of its twitching Torso and the full-body "Clone Alpha" android announced Dec 2024 in a limited run of 279 units on pre-order [S2][S3]. Funding ~$17M to date with a $50M round underway; a Mountain View office signals a Silicon Valley shift; manufacturing cost claimed under $20k per unit [S1].

The commercial semi-humanoid-relevant product is the Clone Torso: a dual-arm musculoskeletal upper body (~164 articulation points in the upper body incl. articulated spine, shoulders, arms and the 27-DoF-class Clone Hands) sold/pre-ordered as a research and development platform [S1][S3]. Roadmap: "surgically accurate" torso able to use scalpels/power drills by late 2026, natural walking 2027, enterprise "Robo Butler" products 2028 [S1] — aggressive claims that remain vendor-only.

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
Clone is technologically orthogonal to every other player: hydraulic artificial muscles vs. electric joints. Near-term it competes only for research budgets (Torso/Hand pre-orders) and attention; its reliability, controllability and force-control story is unproven, and no third-party deployment evidence exists. For a German entrant it is not a practical competitor in industrial/care semi-humanoids this decade (analyst view), but its sub-$20k cost claim and viral reach shape price/expectation narratives — and its muscle technology could become interesting IP if matured.
