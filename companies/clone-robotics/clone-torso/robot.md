# Clone Torso — Clone Robotics (T3 compact)

> The Clone Torso is a musculoskeletal dual-arm upper body from Polish startup Clone Robotics: polymer skeleton, McKibben-style "Myofiber" water-hydraulic artificial muscles and anatomically faithful hands, sold via pre-order as a research platform. It is the only muscle-driven semi-humanoid torso commercially offered anywhere; capability and reliability remain unproven outside vendor videos. (The full-body legged Protoclone/Clone Alpha is excluded from this study.)

The Torso reproduces human upper-body anatomy: articulated spine, shoulder girdles, two arms and Clone Hands with ~164 articulation points across the upper body [S1]. Actuation is by "Myofiber" artificial muscles — contractile McKibben-type pneumatic/hydraulic fibers anchored to polymer bones — driven by a water-based hydraulic vascular system with an electric pump ("heart"); the full Alpha android extends this to 206 bones and ~1,000 muscles [S1][S2]. Vendor videos (viral on X/YouTube, covered by Live Science and others) show human-like twitching, hand dexterity and arm motion; no independent evaluation of payload, precision, endurance or controllability exists — all capability claims are vendor-only [S2][S3]. Company: founded 2021 (Radhakrishnan/Koźlik), ~$17M raised, $50M round underway, cost target <$20k, roadmap of surgically-capable torso (late 2026) → walking (2027) → "Robo Butler" (2028) [S1]. Competitive read: a fascinating actuation bet with real IP potential, but years from industrial or care-grade semi-humanoid deployment; watch for funding close and any verified Torso deliveries.
