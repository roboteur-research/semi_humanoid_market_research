## Company background
WorkFar is an unusual entrant: a US manufacturing family (Advantage Plastics, Louisville, KY — 40+ years of plastics/metal parts for industrial machinery and consumer products) building a teleoperation-first wheeled humanoid in-house, without VC [S2][S3]. It debuted "Syntro" in August 2024 with breathless PR ("sentient humanoid robots... unleashed into the workforce" — the "sentience" being a remote human operator in VR with eye tracking and haptic gloves) [S4]. The business model is labor-as-a-service: leasing a robot together with a trained remote operator monthly, no down payment, aimed at logistics and manufacturing clients; the store also sells units outright in three tiers (Base / Telepresence with VR headset+gloves+suit / Intelligent with AI, fleet management and IoT) from $9,999.99 to $59,999.99 [S4][S5].

**Substance check (assignment question "is it real?"):** Real but small and unproven. Positives: a physical manufacturing base (Advantage Plastics), an active LinkedIn page, continuous product iteration (Syntro V17 with overhauled wheeled lower body, Jan 2026 store refresh), detailed spec sheets and package pricing. Negatives: no named founders or executives anywhere, Delaware registered-agent HQ, all deployment claims vendor-authored (PR Newswire paid releases), zero independent press or customer evidence found, and store ETAs of 6-12 months mean "in production" is really pre-order/build-to-order. No verified deployments.

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
WorkFar matters as a data point that US mid-market manufacturers are attempting bootstrap humanoids at sub-$30k teleop-first price points — potentially credible in cost terms given in-house injection molding, and its 45-DoF spec at $29,999 undercuts nearly everyone on paper. Until third-party deployment evidence appears, treat it as a marketing-forward pre-revenue effort. Threat to an EU entrant: low; watch the lease-with-operator model, which parallels Reflex/Eden.
