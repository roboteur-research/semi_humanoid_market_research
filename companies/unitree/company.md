## Company background
Unitree, founded 2016 in Hangzhou by Wang Xingxing, became the world's volume leader in legged robots by relentlessly vertical-integrating motors, reducers, encoders, controllers and recently dexterous hands (purchased parts only 14-18% of costs per prospectus coverage), and pricing far below Western rivals. It shipped >5,500 humanoids in 2025 — a claimed 32.4% global unit share, #1 worldwide — while growing revenue to RMB 1.708B at a 60.27% gross margin (up from 44% in 2022) and turning solidly profitable [S2]. Its IPO on Shanghai's STAR Market (CSRC approval 3 Jul 2026, ~$610M raise, ~$6B valuation) makes it the first profitable pure-play humanoid maker to list on fundamentals [S2].

Product strategy is "performance floor at a consumer price": G1 biped from $16k (2024), R1 biped from RMB 39,900 (Jul 2025), and in May 2026 the R1-D — Unitree's first non-biped commercial form factor: a dual-arm humanoid upper body sold with a fixed desktop base or an optional wheeled lifting base, from RMB 26,900 / $4,290 [S1][S3]. R1-D reuses the R1 biped's arm and actuator lineage, spreading BOM across form factors.

Sales are global through webshop and distributors; software openness (SDKs, secondary development) has made Unitree hardware the default in research labs worldwide.

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
Unitree entering the wheeled/semi-humanoid category collapses the price floor: $4,290 for a dual-arm manipulation platform is 10-20x cheaper than incumbent research SKUs (Galaxea R1 Pro $70k; Western platforms $100k+). Even with light payloads (2-4 kg arms, 1.5 h battery), it commoditizes entry-level data collection and education/research use, forcing everyone above it to justify their premium with payload, runtime, safety certification and support. With 60% gross margins, IPO capital and in-house actuators, Unitree can sustain a price war no EU entrant should fight head-on. Trajectory: aggressive expansion into every embodiment.
