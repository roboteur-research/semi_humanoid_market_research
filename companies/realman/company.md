## Company background
RealMan is one of China's established ultra-lightweight robotic-arm and joint-module incumbents: founded in Beijing in 2018 by Zheng Suibing, it built its business on integrated joint modules (claimed annual capacity >500,000 units, scaling toward 1M) and compact 6/7-DoF arms (RM65/RM75 etc.) that are widely embedded in third-party robots — e.g. the US OpenDroids R2D3 uses RealMan RM75-B arms and hands [S5]. The company claims >8,000 enterprise customers and joint-module MTBF of 50,000 hours with CRL3 certification — reliability positioning it markets as "real-work robots" [S3][S4].

At WAIC 2026 (Shanghai, 2026-07-17/20, unveiling 07-19) RealMan debuted two full-size wheeled dual-arm humanoids built from its own modules: the folding-leg RealBot-S2 and lift-column RealBot-L2 [S1][S2][S4]. Around the robots it sells an operations stack: the GLN teleoperation network (millisecond-latency cross-city teleop, demoed Beijing→Shanghai tea-making; data centers in Beijing and Changzhou; >10,000 h of real-robot data) and the Darwin teleoperation/data-collection platform, feeding a data-services business (RealSource) [S3][S4]. Deployments include back-kitchen work (noodle pulling, skewering, grilling) at a restaurant in Yining, Xinjiang, and 24/7 industrial inspection robots with CASI Vision [S3]. Funding has accelerated: Ecovacs-led A+ (2023), Series C at ~RMB 3.2B valuation, and a ~RMB 500M round in 03/2026 [S6].

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
RealMan attacks the category from the components side: unlike demo-first humanoid startups it owns the joint-module supply chain, quotes MTBF instead of backflips, and monetizes teleop/data services as much as robots. Its modules already power other vendors' semi-humanoids, so it profits from the category whether or not RealBot wins. For an EU entrant, RealMan is both a potential low-cost component supplier and a reliability-message competitor; its 50,000 h MTBF claim (vendor-tested) is a marketing bar EU vendors will be measured against.
