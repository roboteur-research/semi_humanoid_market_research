## Company background
LuxAI is a University of Luxembourg spin-off (founded 2016 by Pouyan Ziafati and Aida Nazarikhorram) that builds QTrobot, a small expressive pedestal social robot for autism therapy, special-needs education and HRI research [S1][S2]. Development started 2016, final prototype mid-2017, trials from 2018 in centers across Luxembourg, France, Belgium and Germany [S2]. The product line matured into three offers: QTrobot for Research (ROS-based platform used by research institutes), QTrobot for Schools (assistive teaching tool with session reporting), and QTrobot for Home (launched 2021 as "first in-home AI tutor for autistic children", $1,977 + $149/month support) [S1][S3]. The professional version sells around €6,800-$10,000 [S2][S3]. Robots ship globally ("from the US to China") [S3]. Awards include a WEF Smart Toy Award (2021) and CES Innovation Award (2019) [S1].

Unlike almost every other company in this study, LuxAI has been quietly commercial for years with a sustainable niche: a stationary, non-mobile semi-humanoid (head + torso + gesture arms on a desk pedestal) whose value is content and evidence-based curricula, not manipulation.

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
LuxAI demonstrates the viable low end of the semi-humanoid market: no mobility, no manipulation, ~€2-10k price points, recurring-revenue content subscriptions, and a defensible clinical/education niche. For a German entrant it is not a competitor in industrial/care manipulation, but a reference case for (a) EU-based hardware+SaaS economics and (b) how far "semi-humanoid" value can be delivered without arms that grasp. Trajectory: stable niche player, shipping for ~8 years.
