## Company background
Walden Robotics is the commercial spin-out of Toyota Research Institute's Large Behavior Model (LBM) program. It was formed in January 2026 and came out of stealth on 2026-07-15 with one of the largest seed rounds ever in robotics: $300M at a $1.1B valuation, co-led by Toyota entities and Deviation Capital, with NVIDIA, Boeing, Samsung Ventures, CoreWeave Ventures, Prologis Ventures, Menlo Ventures and others participating [S1][S2][S3]. CEO and co-founder Russ Tedrake — MIT robotics professor (underactuated robotics, Drake toolbox) and formerly SVP of Large Behavior Models at TRI — leads a founding team drawn from TRI, MIT, Stanford and Amazon; the group pioneered Diffusion Policy and LBM techniques now central to the robot-foundation-model field [S2][S4].

The company positions itself as a "full-stack Physical AI company": it builds the hardware (wheeled semi-humanoid), the LBM foundation models, and the application/deployment layer. Its differentiating go-to-market claim is deployment-first: its robots had been working eight-hour production shifts at a North American Toyota manufacturing plant since February 2026 — before the company even unveiled itself — moving from first pilot to real production work in under two months [S1][S4]. Launch materials list machine tending, tool setting, parts kitting and assembly as live applications, with pilots booked across automotive, aerospace, semiconductors, electronics, logistics and life sciences [S1][S3].

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
Walden is arguably the strongest new US entrant in the wheeled semi-humanoid category: top-tier research pedigree (Tedrake/TRI LBM team), a captive anchor customer and co-lead investor in Toyota (echoing the Toyota ELEY internal program — see companies/toyota/ and companies/tri/), and a compute/AI investor set (NVIDIA, CoreWeave) that signals a foundation-model-centric strategy. Its explicit choice of a wheeled, legless form factor for factory work validates the semi-humanoid thesis against bipedal competitors (Figure, Tesla). The July 2026 US FCC import restrictions on foreign robotic devices (DA 26-786) further advantage a domestic US builder. For an EU entrant this is a high-threat benchmark for credibility, capital access and deployment velocity.
