## Company background
Kinova is Canada's best-known robot-arm maker, founded 2006 in the Montréal area around the JACO lightweight assistive arm for wheelchair users. It expanded into research arms (Gen2, then the vision-equipped 7-DoF Gen3 and low-cost Gen3 lite, both ubiquitous in university labs) and, from 2022, the Link 6 industrial cobot. Distribution is direct plus global research distributors; manufacturing in Boisbriand QC [S1].

MOVO was Kinova's one venture into full semi-humanoid mobile manipulation: a holonomic base with a torso lift carrying up to two JACO²/Gen2 arms, developed with Stanley Innovation and released to a beta community of universities around 2016-17 [S2][S3]. It never became a volume product; Kinova ended MOVO support and archived the GitHub repo in July 2023 [S2]. Kinova has since concentrated on arms — it participates in today's semi-humanoid wave as a component supplier (its arms appear on third-party mobile platforms) rather than a platform vendor.

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
MOVO is a cautionary data point: an established arm maker with distribution could not sustain a ~$100k-class research semi-humanoid in the pre-VLA era (small market, PR2-style economics). Kinova today matters mainly as a North American arm supplier an EU platform builder could source from — or compete against when customers assemble their own dual-arm mobile rigs from Gen3 arms on AMR bases.
