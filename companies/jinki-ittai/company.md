## Company background
Jinki Ittai ("man and machine as one") spun out of Katsuya Kanaoka's Ritsumeikan University research on **Man-Machine Synergy Effectors** — power-amplifying force-feedback master-slave control. Founded 2007 in Kusatsu, Shiga, its mission slogan is to "eliminate physical drudgery from the world" [S1]. The company deliberately does **not** mass-produce robots: it develops force/torque-control and power-amplified bilateral control patents, proves them in spectacular prototypes (the Zero-shiki Jinki series, styled with input from Macross mecha designer Shoji Kawamori), and **licenses the IP to manufacturing partners** who productize [S2][S3]. Its stated framing: "our intellectual property is knowledge... we create robot hardware and software first, then show how our technology solves specific challenges" [S1].

The breakthrough came through the railway consortium: **Nippon Signal** (manufacturer/licensee) and **West Japan Railway (JR West)** (customer/test field). The Zero-shiki Jinki ver.2.0 PoC (2019-21, partly built under Fukushima's robot-industry reconstruction program; ver.1.3 is exhibited at Fukushima Robot Test Field) became the **"Multifunctional Railway Heavy Machine" (多機能鉄道重機)**, which entered **revenue service on JR West lines in July 2024** — a humanoid torso on the crane boom of a road-rail vehicle, doing overhead-line painting and vegetation cutting at up to 12m [S2][S3]. This is the deployment that made 人型重機 ("humanoid heavy machinery") a recognized product category in Japanese media. Announced follow-on collaborations target power transmission (Tohoku Electric Power Network), civil engineering (Takenaka Civil Engineering) and other infrastructure sectors [S2].

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
Jinki Ittai owns the highest-profile *paying* semi-humanoid deployment in Japan (2024) and demonstrates a distinct commercial thesis: teleoperated force-amplified torsos on vehicle booms for dangerous infrastructure work — no autonomy, no navigation, pure human skill projection. Its licensing model means the competitive unit is the patent portfolio plus consortium, not a factory; expansion is bounded by partner appetite but capital-light. For an EU entrant, it proves customers will pay for teleop-first heavy semi-humanoids in safety-critical niches (comparable EU analog: none shipping), and that category creation ("人型重機") can be done by a tiny company with the right anchor customer.
