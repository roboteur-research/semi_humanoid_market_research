## Company background
Mirsee Robotics, based in Cambridge, Ontario, develops industrial wheeled
humanoids for "physically demanding and hazardous" work. Its MH3 is described
as a third-generation machine: 180 cm dual-arm humanoid with 5-finger dexterous
hands (6 DoF per hand), a deliberately wheeled base ("harder to knock over",
longer battery life vs legs), 10 h runtime, IP54, and immersive-VR
teleoperation with haptic feedback. Target sectors: manufacturing, healthcare,
defense and critical-infrastructure maintenance [S1][S2].

The company lists MH3 at USD 200k, "in production" per humanoid.guide, though
press reporting indicates commercial production launches planned for 2027 —
i.e. current status is closer to late prototype / early availability [S1][S2].
One aggregator flags "possible ties to China" (supply chain or engineering,
unconfirmed) [S2]. Funding, team size and customers are undisclosed — a
notably thin public footprint for a $200k-class product.

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
One of few North American industrial wheeled-humanoid vendors, positioned at
the premium end ($200k) with strong paper specs (30 kg per arm, 31 DoF, IP54,
satellite connectivity option) and a teleoperation-first model for hazardous
work. If specs hold, MH3 competes directly with EU industrial semi-humanoid
offerings in infrastructure/defense niches. Verification is weak: no deployment
evidence, no funding trail, "Not verified" listing — treat capability claims
cautiously.
