## Company background
KIT's High Performance Humanoid Technologies (H2T) lab has built the ARMAR family of humanoid robots continuously since 1998, making it — alongside DLR — the defining German academic lineage in semi-humanoid (wheeled, dual-arm) robotics [S1]. ARMAR-III (2006) was the landmark "kitchen robot": a wheeled humanoid with two 7-DoF arms and pneumatic-actuated hands that loaded dishwashers and fetched items in a model kitchen; it was still operational 19 years later and was exhibited in 2025 as an icon of KIT research history [S1][S4]. ARMAR-6 (2018) was developed as the flagship of the EU H2020 SecondHands project with UK online grocer Ocado, EPFL, UCL and Sapienza: a 152-192 cm (40 cm telescopic torso), 160 kg collaborative humanoid on a four-Mecanum-wheel holonomic base, with two 8-DoF torque-controlled arms (10 kg payload each), underactuated five-finger hands, designed to hand tools and physically assist human maintenance technicians in Ocado's automated warehouses — proactively recognizing when help is needed [S2][S3].

ARMAR-7, unveiled at Automatica 2025 (Munich) and shown again at Hannover Messe 2026, shifts focus from industry to households and care facilities: it learns tasks from human demonstration, understands speech and gestures, and autonomously executes chores such as unloading a dishwasher or sorting laundry [S1][S5]. Hardware: holonomic three-omni-wheel platform, 2-DoF knee/hip kinematics for bowing and torso height adjustment, two 8-DoF torque-controlled arms, 4-DoF underactuated five-finger hands with in-hand RGB cameras, 3-DoF sensor head, ~32 actuated DoF over EtherCAT, running the lab's open ArmarX cognitive architecture [S5].

H2T is an academic lab, not a vendor; its robots are one-offs. Its transfer channel is people, publications, the open-source ArmarX stack, and industry projects (Ocado). It anchors Karlsruhe as a German robotics cluster together with FZI (see companies/fzi/).

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
For a German entrant, H2T matters three ways: (1) proof that wheeled dual-arm humanoids can do genuinely useful bimanual work (power-tool handovers, dishwasher unloading) — a demo vocabulary any eldercare/industrial semi-humanoid will be measured against; (2) talent pipeline in Karlsruhe; (3) the Ocado SecondHands project remains one of the few cases of a major retailer co-funding a humanoid assistant, evidence of industrial pull in Europe. No commercial threat; strong potential research partner. Trajectory: active and accelerating (new generation in 2025, eldercare focus aligned with German demographics).
