## Company background
Pollen Robotics was founded in 2016 in Bordeaux by Matthieu Lapeyre and Pierre Rouanet, who previously built the open-source, 3D-printed Poppy humanoid at INRIA. The company's identity has always been radical openness: hardware CAD, firmware, and software for its Reachy robots are published openly, and its signature technology is the "Orbita" family of parallel spherical actuators used in Reachy's wrists, neck and shoulders. Reachy 1 launched in 2019 as an open research platform; Reachy 2, shown at CES 2025, is the mature bimanual product with human-scale 7-DoF arms and an optional omnidirectional mobile base, priced around $70,000 [S1][S2].

In April 2025 Hugging Face — the dominant open-source AI model hub — acquired Pollen Robotics as its hardware arm for "physical AI". The ~30-person team joined Hugging Face, which committed to open-sourcing Reachy 2's full hardware and software design while continuing to sell assembled units [S3][S5]. Reachy 2 is the flagship physical platform for Hugging Face's LeRobot open-source robot-learning library (started 2024 by Rémi Cadène, ex-Tesla Optimus, who has since left to found the separate startup UMA). LeRobot passed 12k+ GitHub stars in its first year and gives Reachy 2 a uniquely large developer funnel [S6].

Post-acquisition strategy: sell Reachy 2 to AI/robotics labs (deployments at Cornell, Carnegie Mellon and other institutions), seed a mass community with the cheap Reachy Mini desktop robot ($299 lite / $449 wireless; pre-orders opened July 2025, rolling deliveries through 2026), and position Hugging Face as the "GitHub of robotics" — models, datasets, teleop data pipelines and hardware in one vertically integrated open stack [S5][S6][S7]. Go-to-market is direct sale (quote/order online), four Reachy 2 kit variants (single/dual arm, with/without mobile base). Manufacturing remains small-batch in France; scale ambitions are community-replication (3D-printable designs) rather than mass production.

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
Pollen/Hugging Face matters far beyond its unit volume: Reachy 2 is the de-facto reference open-source wheeled bimanual humanoid in Western research, and the LeRobot ecosystem is becoming the default software entry point for embodied-AI developers. For a new EU entrant, Pollen is less a commercial competitor (research niche, 3 kg payload, no industrial certification) than an ecosystem gravity well: talent, datasets, and tooling standardize around it. Trajectory is scaling up in community reach, not in industrial deployment.
