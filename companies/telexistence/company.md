## Company background
Telexistence (TX) commercializes the "telexistence" concept pioneered by Prof. Susumu Tachi — remote embodiment through robots with low-latency VR interfaces. Founded January 2017, TX built the humanoid teleop prototypes Model H (2017) and Model-T (2020), the latter a 22-DoF wheeled dual-arm robot that stocked convenience-store shelves at FamilyMart and Lawson pilot stores in Tokyo in 2020, operated by VR pilots from the TX office with 50ms end-to-end video latency. [S1][S2]

Strategically, TX then de-humanoidized: it concluded that beverage restocking — the highest-value convenience-store task — is better served by a cheaper single-arm robot, and deployed TX SCARA (with AI autonomy "GORDON" and human teleop fallback) to 300 FamilyMart stores from 2022, one of the largest in-store robot fleets anywhere. The 2023 $170M Series B (SoftBank-led, with Airbus Ventures, KDDI, Foxconn/CTBC, Globis) funds North American expansion with SoftBank Robotics and mass manufacturing of the next-gen GHOST arm by Foxconn. [S3][S4]

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
TX is the canonical cautionary tale for humanoid form factors in retail: it built one of the best-publicized wheeled semi-humanoids (Model-T), proved teleop economics in real stores, then abandoned the humanoid morphology for task-specific single arms at scale. Its AWP teleoperation platform, latency stack and remote-labor operating model remain directly relevant to any semi-humanoid vendor, and its investor base (SoftBank, Airbus, KDDI, Foxconn) keeps it a heavyweight in Japanese robot automation. [S2][S3][S4]
