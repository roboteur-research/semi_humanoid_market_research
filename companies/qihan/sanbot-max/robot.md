# Sanbot Max / King Kong — Qihan Technology (旗瀚科技)

> Dormant/legacy: Qihan's 2017 flagship was among the first Chinese wheeled dual-arm service humanoids marketed internationally — 1.46 m, ~100 kg, two "bionic" arms on a fast four-wheel mecanum base with 75 kg towing capacity and IBM-Watson-backed cloud AI. The line (and Qihan's robotics business) went dormant around 2019. This dossier also notes the smaller Sanbot Elf.

Sanbot King Kong — sold in Western markets as Sanbot Max — was Qihan's large service humanoid, unveiled at the 2017 World Robot Conference and CES 2018 [S1]. Against the reception-robot norm of its era it emphasized muscle: a mecanum four-wheel base, high claimed speed, and towing/delivery functions alongside interaction features inherited from the 2016 Sanbot Elf (902 mm, 19 kg, 10.1-inch touchscreen, 360° perception, auto-charging; deployed at Shenzhen Bao'an airport and Gongbei Port for passenger service/security) [S1]. Both robots ran Qihan's cloud robotics platform with Watson-based AI. The company's robotics activity ceased around 2019 with no successor products; the line is treated as discontinued in this study [S2]. Its arms were not manipulation-grade, placing Sanbot Max in the interaction/delivery generation displaced by today's VLA-driven semi-humanoids.
