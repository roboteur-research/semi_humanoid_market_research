## Company background
CMU's Robotics Institute is one of the world's top robotics research organizations. In the semi-humanoid space its flagship was HERB (Home Exploring Robot Butler), built from 2006 by Siddhartha Srinivasa's Personal Robotics Lab with Intel funding: two Barrett WAM arms on a Segway base, used for a decade of manipulation-planning research (CHOMP trajectory optimization and much of modern grasp/motion-planning literature was validated on it). The lab and its research moved to the University of Washington around 2017; HERB itself is retired [S1].

Note — Ballbot with arms: Ralph Hollis's CMU Ballbot (2006-) balances on a single ball, giving an omnidirectional, slender, physically compliant base; around 2016-19 it gained a pair of 7-DoF arms (the "SIMbot"/ballbot-with-arms configuration) for physical human assistance research (helping people stand, object handoff). It remains a niche but genuinely novel take on semi-humanoid mobility [S2].

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
Historic/academic only — no commercial activity. HERB matters as the research platform that produced much of the manipulation-planning canon (and alumni across US robotics); the Ballbot line is a reminder that base morphologies beyond wheels-on-a-box exist for narrow, human-dense spaces.
