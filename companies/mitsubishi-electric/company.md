## Company background
Mitsubishi Electric is one of Japan's largest electric-machinery conglomerates, with an established industrial-robot business (MELFA arms) and a major space-systems division that builds satellites and delivered the precision remote-control systems for the Subaru telescope (Mauna Kea) and the ALMA telescope (Chile, 5,000m altitude). DiaroiD (ディアロイド) — its teleoperated dual-arm humanoid for dangerous work — was developed inside the space-systems organization precisely from that large-structure remote-precision-control lineage, initially motivated by maintenance work at ALMA's high-altitude site where human labor is physically punishing. [S1][S2]

DiaroiD has iterated through three prototype generations (first giho paper 2021, progress report 2023). The current generation moved from wired 3-phase 200V AC power to onboard 48V batteries (~1h runtime), and rides on CuboRex CuGo MEGA electric crawlers — making it one of the very few tracked semi-humanoids anywhere — chosen after benchmarking overseas alternatives for cost, slope payload and straight-line tracking. It was shown with Kubota's KATV all-terrain vehicle at Kansai Robot World 2024, and Mitsubishi targets practical deployment from FY2025 onward in hazardous-work substitution (with space/lunar applications as a stated long-term direction). [S1][S2][S3]

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
Mitsubishi Electric represents the "heavy incumbent enters via hazardous-work niche" pattern: essentially unlimited engineering resources, space-grade teleoperation know-how (visual haptic feedback, voice-zoom cameras, model-based design toolchain), and a tracked platform aimed at terrain no wheeled semi-humanoid can handle. It is not yet a commercial product and has no disclosed pricing or customers, but if productized it would own a defensible niche (disaster response, radiation environments, remote infrastructure) adjacent to — rather than competing head-on with — indoor wheeled semi-humanoids.
