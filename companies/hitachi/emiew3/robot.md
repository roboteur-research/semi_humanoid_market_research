# EMIEW3 (エミュー3) — Hitachi

> BORDERLINE semi-humanoid, never commercialized: a 90 cm, 15 kg customer-service robot (2016) with gestural arms only — no manipulation — on a fast (6 km/h) wheel-and-caster base, backed by a cloud "remote brain" robotics IT platform. Third generation of Hitachi's EMIEW line (2005 Aichi Expo original was two-wheel self-balancing). PoCs at Haneda Airport and stations/banks targeted 2018 commercialization that never came; the program went quiet ~2018-19. Included here for completeness; it fails the manipulation test of this study's core definition.

EMIEW3, announced 8 April 2016, was the productization push of a decade-long Hitachi R&D line whose signature was mobility: the 2005 original balanced on two wheels and moved at human walking pace, and EMIEW3 kept the ~6 km/h speed with autonomous fall recovery — capabilities aimed at real public-space service rather than kiosk duty [S1][S3]. Intelligence lived off-board in Hitachi's robotics IT platform, an early "remote brain" architecture for fleets of service robots sharing cloud perception and dialogue [S1]. PoCs ran at Haneda Airport (multilingual guidance, 2016-17), rail stations and financial institutions, with Hitachi Review documenting a push toward 2018 commercialization [S2]. No product launch followed; EMIEW activity faded from Hitachi communications after ~2018-19, making it the highest-profile Japanese corporate service-humanoid program to die before market. Competitive takeaway: strong locomotion and cloud AI without manipulation arms did not produce a sellable value proposition — the same lesson as Pepper, from a company with far deeper engineering resources.
