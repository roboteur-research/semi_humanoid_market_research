## Company background
Hitachi's EMIEW line ("Excellent Mobility and Interactive Existence as Workmate") ran from the 2005 Aichi Expo original (130 cm, two-wheel self-balancing) through EMIEW2 (2007; 80 cm, 14 kg) to EMIEW3 (2016; 90 cm, 15 kg) plus a cloud "robotics IT platform" remote brain [S1]. The program showcased Hitachi's mobility (self-balancing, 6 km/h — once billed as the world's fastest-moving small humanoid) and acoustics research (14-ch mic array), and ran customer-service PoCs at Haneda Airport, rail stations and banks, with commercialization targeted for 2018 [S1][S2].

Commercialization never happened: no EMIEW model was ever sold as a product, and public activity faded after ~2018-2019. Hitachi's robotics business today is industrial (JR Automation acquisition, logistics), not humanoid.

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
Borderline entry in this study: EMIEW3's arms are gestural only (no grasping payload), so it sits at the interaction-robot edge of the semi-humanoid definition. Included as (a) Japan's most prominent corporate service-humanoid program of the 2010s to die pre-launch, and (b) evidence that even strong mobility + cloud-AI engineering could not close the service-robot business case without manipulation.
