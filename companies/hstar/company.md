## Company background
Hstar Technologies was a small Cambridge MA medical-robotics R&D company that, with US Army TATRC/SBIR funding, developed the RoNA (Robotic Nursing Assistant) between roughly 2011 and 2015: a human-safe dual-arm nursing robot on a mecanum omnidirectional base designed to lift heavy patients (2nd generation: up to 500 lb / 227 kg) under direct or remote nurse supervision [S1][S2]. The company also marketed the underlying AMP omnidirectional mobile platform. RoNA never advanced beyond prototype/clinical-study stage; Hstar's web presence has since disappeared and the company appears inactive (estimated).

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
Together with Vecna's BEAR and RIKEN's RIBA, RoNA defines the small historic cluster of "heavy-lift nursing semi-humanoids" — evidence both that patient lifting is a recurring, well-funded use case (US nursing-injury costs) and that no one has yet cracked it commercially. Useful market-history datapoint for healthcare-targeted platforms; no competitive threat.
