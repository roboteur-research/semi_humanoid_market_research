# RoNA (Robotic Nursing Assistant) — Hstar Technologies

> RoNA (~2011-2015) was Hstar's prototype nursing semi-humanoid: a dual-arm humanoid torso on a mecanum omnidirectional base, built to lift patients of up to 500 lb (227 kg) in its arms under nurse supervision or telepresence. Army/SBIR-funded; never commercialized.

RoNA combined a humanoid dual-arm upper body with a mecanum-wheeled omnidirectional base able to maneuver in confined hospital rooms where portable patient lifts cannot [S1][S2]. Lift capacity grew from ~150 lb with one arm / 250 lb with both (Gen 1) to 500 lb (227 kg) in the more powerful 2nd generation — cradle-carrying patients RIBA-style in its arms [S2]. Safety and control relied on compliant human-safe actuation with haptic feedback: nurses physically guided the lift while the robot sensed the load's center of gravity and auto-adjusted its stability; a telepresence mode let a remote nurse supervise a less-trained attendant [S1][S2]. The system was documented in IEEE papers ("Giving patients a lift — the robotic nursing assistant", 2014) and healthcare-economics case studies, motivated by the high cost of nurse back injuries, but stayed at prototype/evaluation stage and vanished along with the company's web presence [S1][S3].
