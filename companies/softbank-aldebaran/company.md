## Company background
The Pepper story spans two corporate lineages. **Aldebaran Robotics** (Paris, founded 2005 by Bruno Maisonnier) built the NAO small humanoid, and was acquired by **SoftBank Group** for ~$100M in 2012 [S2]. SoftBank unveiled Pepper in Tokyo on 5 June 2014 and sold it from June 2015 through SoftBank Mobile shops; Aldebaran was rebranded **SoftBank Robotics Europe** in 2016. Pepper was manufactured by Foxconn [S1]. After weak demand, Pepper production was paused in June 2021 [S1]. In 2022 SoftBank sold SoftBank Robotics Europe to Germany's **United Robotics Group** (URG, Hahn Group/RAG-Stiftung), which restored the Aldebaran name and immediately cut nearly half the staff (165 of 330) [S2].

The European entity never recovered: cumulative net deficit of ~€156M over 2019-22 and ~€26M operating loss in 2023 (~$29M in 2024 per The Robot Report), with debts reported around €150M [S2][S5]. URG stopped funding Aldebaran in 2024; Aldebaran **filed for insolvency in February 2025**, cut roughly half its remaining staff in April 2025, and was placed in **judicial liquidation/receivership in June 2025** (panel decision 3 June 2025) [S2]. Two prospective buyers fell through before **Maxvision Technology Corp. (Shenzhen)** acquired Aldebaran's core assets and IP for NAO and Pepper on **10 July 2025**; Maxvision, a biometrics/security-tech company diversifying into robotics, said it would establish a French subsidiary preserving "original teams, product lines, and client support operations". It has since promoted a NAO revival; concrete Pepper continuation plans remain undisclosed [S3][S4].

Meanwhile **SoftBank Robotics Group (Japan)** — a separate, continuing SoftBank subsidiary — never stopped supporting Pepper's Japanese fleet. It ended the original consumer cloud service (units not migrated by 28 February 2025 became unusable) [S6], but kept renting Pepper for Biz/Education/Home, and on **2 February 2026 launched "Pepper+" (ペッパープラス)**, a rental-only relaunch with a generative-AI customer-service agent and upgraded tablet — verified by the official product page and independent press (GIGAZINE) [S7][S8]. SoftBank Group has pivoted its robotics ambitions to "Physical AI", announcing the acquisition of ABB's robotics division (October 2025).

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
Pepper remains the largest semi-humanoid install base ever (~27,000 produced / ~17,000 sold) and the category's defining cautionary tale: an emotional-engagement robot priced as a subscription that could not renew customers (reported ~15% renewal at first contract expiry) [S2]. The 2025 insolvency transferred iconic European humanoid IP to a Chinese buyer — a data point for EU sovereignty debates. The Pepper+ relaunch shows SoftBank still believes there is a Japanese B2B rental market for a talking greeter robot at ~¥80k/month, now repowered by LLMs; any EU entrant selling "interaction" semi-humanoids competes against this legacy fleet and its pricing anchor.
