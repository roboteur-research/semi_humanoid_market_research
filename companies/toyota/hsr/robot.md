# Human Support Robot (HSR) — Toyota  ⚠ BORDERLINE (single-arm)

> **Classification note:** HSR has ONE folding arm on a telescoping cylindrical body —
> outside this study's two-arm definition, included (like Hello Robot Stretch) as a
> historically decisive borderline platform: the archetype of the compact home-assistance
> mobile manipulator, distributed to 40+ research institutions, and direct ancestor of
> Toyota's dual-arm ELEY (Dec 2025), which IS a semi-humanoid.

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Status (2026) | research program (since 2012; HSR Developers' Community from 2015); succeeded by dual-arm ELEY (WRS 2025) |
| Form | 430 mm diameter cylinder, 1005–1350 mm telescoping height, ~37 kg, omni wheels |
| Arm | single folding arm, shoulder height 340–1030 mm; grasp ≤1.2 kg, objects ≤130 mm wide; suction + gripper |
| Speed / terrain | 0.8 km/h; 5 mm steps, 5° inclines |
| Interface | voice, tablet, autonomous; telepresence video/audio relay |
| Sensors | head RGB-D + stereo, wide-angle cams, base lidar [estimated: standard HSR config] |
| Software | ROS-based; HSR Developers' Community SDK; RoboCup@Home Domestic Standard Platform |

## History & significance
Prototyped 2011-12 with the Japan Service Dog Association and trialed in homes of
limb-disabled users (Yokohama Rehabilitation Service, 2011); accelerated 2015 with the
HSR Developers' Community; deployed as telepresence/assistance robot at Tokyo 2020
Olympics; framed by Toyota's 2018 "mobility company" repositioning [S1]. As RoboCup@Home's
standard platform and the hardware behind Preferred Networks' famous tidying-up demos, HSR
shaped a decade of Japanese home-robot research. Its limits — one arm, 1.2 kg grasp —
are precisely what Toyota's dual-arm ELEY successor (WRS 2025 Aichi) addresses [S2].
