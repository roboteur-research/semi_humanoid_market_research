## Company background
Toyota's Partner Robot program (from ~2004, trumpet-playing bipeds at Expo 2005) is Japan's longest-running automaker robotics effort. Robina (2007) — a 1.2 m, 60 kg wheeled guide humanoid that led tours at the Toyota Kaikan Exhibition Hall — was its first robot in sustained public-facing service [S1][S2]. The program then bore the single-arm Human Support Robot (HSR, 2012), distributed to 40+ research institutions worldwide and used at the Tokyo 2020 Olympics, plus the T-HR3 teleop biped (2017) [S3].

In December 2025 Toyota unveiled ELEY ("Embodied Learning robot for Enhanced Yield") at the World Robot Summit 2025 Aichi — a dual-arm successor to HSR aimed at assembly and box handling with learning-based asynchronous bimanual manipulation — signaling Toyota's re-entry into semi-humanoid manipulation after years of research-community focus [S4]. TRI in the US separately develops Punyo (soft whole-body bimanual) and large-behavior-model manipulation research (see US dossiers). In 2026 Toyota co-led the $300M seed of TRI spin-out Walden Robotics, whose wheeled W0.5 humanoids work in a North American Toyota plant — see [companies/walden-robotics/](../walden-robotics/company.md).

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
High-latent threat: Toyota has manufacturing scale, three decades of robot R&D, the HSR academic ecosystem, and now a declared dual-arm platform (ELEY). Robina itself is only historical, but the lineage Robina → HSR → ELEY shows Toyota consistently choosing wheeled, home/service-oriented semi-humanoids over commercial bipeds. Timing and commercialization intent for ELEY remain undisclosed — a key watch item.
