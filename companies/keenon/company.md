## Company background
Keenon Robotics (擎朗智能, Shanghai, founded 2010 by Li Tong) is the world's #1 commercial service-robot vendor by share — IDC puts it at 22.7% of the global commercial service robot market in 2024 — with 100,000+ robots deployed across 60-70+ countries and 600+ cities, generating "billions of environmental sensing data points daily" from its delivery and cleaning fleets [third-party/vendor-claimed]. SoftBank Vision Fund 2 led its ~$200M Series D in 2021; SoftBank Robotics also distributes Keenon products in Japan/EMEA.

In 2025 Keenon opened a humanoid line under the XMAN brand with an explicit "robot job position" (岗位型/岗位化) philosophy: rather than a general laborer, each robot is designed into a defined service job and choreographed with Keenon's existing special-purpose fleet ("general-purpose + special-purpose collaboration"). The wheeled XMAN-R1 (2025-03-31) is the semi-humanoid service worker; the bipedal XMAN-F1 (WAIC, 07/2025) is the F&B/reception flagship [vendor-claimed/third-party]. Keenon frames the XMAN series as completing its ecosystem — the humanoid does the manipulation "last meter" (pouring, plating, folding) while T-series robots run transport, closing a full service loop.

Go-to-market: direct + global distributor network built on the delivery-robot business; hotel/restaurant chains as first XMAN customers (Shangri-La Traders Hotel Hongqiao deployment 10/2025; NOWWA coffee chain partnership at WAIC 2026). XMAN-R1 pricing reported around $100k [third-party].

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
Keenon is the strongest "channel incumbent" entering semi-humanoids: it owns the world's largest commercial service-robot install base, the customer relationships (hotels, restaurants, hospitals), and a fleet-data corpus for training. Its multi-robot orchestration story (humanoid + delivery + cleaning robots on one scheduler) is genuinely differentiated and already demonstrated in a revenue Shangri-La property. Trajectory: scaling XMAN-R1 pilots into hotel chains through 2026. For an EU entrant targeting hospitality, Keenon is the most direct competitive threat — it already sells and services robots across Europe.
