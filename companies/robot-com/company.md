## Company background
Robot.com began life in 2017 as Kiwibot, the Colombian-American sidewalk-delivery startup that scaled to 500+ delivery robots on US college campuses (with food-service giant Sodexo as partner and investor) and completed over 2.5 million deliveries/tasks [S2]. In October 2025 it rebranded to Robot.com and in mid-2026 launched R-noid, a wheeled humanoid for "the work that burns people out — no legs, all lift to the bottom line" [S1][S3]. The delivery-robot heritage matters: the company brings seven years of fleet operations, teleoperation infrastructure, and hospitality/food-service channel relationships into the humanoid market.

The go-to-market is pure RaaS with an aggressive deployment playbook: site visit → lab data capture → onsite teleoperated data collection → autonomy integration, going live in as few as 8-12 weeks; initial rollouts run ~70% autonomous with teleop backfill, and some tasks need ~50 hours of demonstration data before independent operation [S2][S4]. Manipulation runs on Physical Intelligence's π0.7 vision-language-action model (custom models co-developed since 2025); launch partners also include NVIDIA Robotics, Astribot (hardware lineage — R-noid's form closely resembles Astribot's S1 platform), FieldAI, Formic, Robots for America and Yukai Engineering [S3].

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
Robot.com is one of the few semi-humanoid players with real fleet-operations DNA and existing hospitality/food-service customers, and it published concrete traction at launch: <40 units at ~a dozen customers (e.g., Harbor Links Golf Course, NY) [S2][S4]. Its "5 job-ready roles" packaging (Restaurant Assistant, Packer, Picker, Folder, Host) and 8-12-week deployment promise set the commercial benchmark an EU entrant will be measured against in hospitality and light logistics. Trajectory: scaling US deployments; hardware differentiation is thin (likely Astribot-sourced), so its moat is operations + PI partnership.
