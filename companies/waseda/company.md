## Company background
Waseda's Sugano Laboratory is the institutional heart of Japanese human-symbiotic robotics, descending from WABOT-1 — the world's first full-scale anthropomorphic robot (1973). Its TWENDY-ONE (2007) was a landmark omni-wheeled dual-arm care robot with 13-DoF hands and whole-body passive compliance, demonstrating bed-to-wheelchair patient transfer more than a decade before the current semi-humanoid wave. [S4]

Since 2020 Sugano leads the JST Moonshot Goal 3 project "AIREC" (AI-driven Robot for Embrace and Care) — Japan's flagship nursing-humanoid program, with a staged roadmap: Level 1 individual task autonomy (now), Level 2 semi-autonomous service/housework (2025), Level 3 collaborative caregiving autonomy (2030), toward the 2050 Moonshot goal. The current hardware, Dry-AIREC ver. 1.0, is a ~150kg full-size humanoid with torque-sensing 7-DoF arms, multi-finger hands and an articulated column lower body on a WHEELED round base (built by Tokyo Robotics) — not bipedal. Demonstrations include rolling a patient onto their side, supine-to-sitting transfer, sock dressing, scrambled-egg cooking and autonomous ultrasound. Sugano targets care/medical facility use around 2030 at no less than ¥10M initially; care-facility trials began by 2024. A future "wet" actuation phase (hydraulic back-drivable elements, self-healing materials) is planned. [S1][S2][S3]

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
AIREC is the reference point for the nursing/care vertical worldwide: government-flagship funding, real physical-assistance tasks (patient repositioning — heavier-contact work than any commercial semi-humanoid attempts), and a stated price/timeline that anchors expectations (≥¥10M, ~2030). For vendors, it signals both the size of Japan's care-robot pull and the difficulty: after 5+ years the project remains at lab/facility-trial stage. Its hardware supply chain (Tokyo Robotics/Yaskawa) also shows how Japanese academia seeds domestic industry. [S1][S2][S3]
