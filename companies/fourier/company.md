## Company background
Fourier started in 2015 as a rehabilitation-robotics company (exoskeletons, rehab force-feedback devices) and built a global rehab install base before pivoting into general-purpose humanoids with GR-1 (2023) — one of the first commercially delivered Chinese full-size bipeds — followed by GR-2 (2024) and the care-oriented GR-3 (2025). The medical/rehab heritage shapes its positioning: "care-bots", emotional interaction, eldercare and healthcare facilities rather than pure factory work [S1][S3]. Funding through Series E (Jan 2025, ~RMB 800M) includes Saudi Aramco's Prosperity7, giving it Gulf market channels [S3].

In July 2026 at WAIC Shanghai, Fourier launched GRW, its first wheeled dual-arm robot ("轮式双臂机器人"), extending the GR family from bipeds into the semi-humanoid/wheeled category with a heavy-payload (16 kg) narrow-shoulder platform aimed at warehouse intake, component transfer and eldercare lifting assistance [S2]. GRW plugs into Fourier's FOCUS fleet-control system for multi-robot task allocation, path planning and collision avoidance — software shared with the biped fleet [S2].

Fourier is vertically integrated in actuators (self-developed FSA joint modules sold to third parties) and positions itself as a "general robot platform enterprise" with full-stack in-house technology [S1].

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
Fourier's entry validates the wheeled form factor from the biped side: a top-5 Chinese biped maker concluding that eldercare and warehouse payload tasks need wheels, hot-swap batteries and 16 kg capacity rather than legs. Its eldercare focus (a segment German/EU buyers care about, with certification sensitivity) plus Aramco-linked international capital make it a relevant mid-term threat in exactly the care/logistics niches an EU entrant would target. Trajectory: scaling up, product line broadening rapidly.
