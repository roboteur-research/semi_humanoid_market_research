## Company background
JAKA ("Just Always Keep Amazing") was founded in Shanghai in 2014 out of a Shanghai Jiao Tong University-linked team and grew into one of China's top collaborative-robot vendors, with over 10,000 cobots deployed worldwide across automotive (incl. a Toyota Tianjin plant), electronics and precision manufacturing, and subsidiaries/offices in Germany and Japan [S1][S3]. It is the most-funded company in the cobot segment: the ~RMB 1bn D round (2022) was led by Temasek, TrueLight, SoftBank Vision Fund 2 and Aramco's Prosperity7, explicitly to expand overseas channels [S4]. A STAR Market IPO (target raise ~RMB 700m) was filed in May 2023 but withdrawn in December 2025; the company completed a fresh equity round in April 2026 and has re-filed listing tutoring, so a new listing attempt (venue unconfirmed) is expected [S5][S6][S7]. Note: the company is frequently described as "SSE-listed" in secondary sources — this is incorrect as of mid-2026.

Since 2024 JAKA has layered an "embodied intelligence" product family on top of its cobot joint/controller technology: the K1 dual-arm humanoid (29 joints excl. hands, 7-axis arms, ±0.1mm), the K1L legged humanoid, the K1W wheeled humanoid, the single-arm lift-column JAKA Lumi data/served platform (vision partnerships with Albert and Orbbec), and the S³ autonomous composite robot. JAKA states it delivered 300+ embodied-family units in 2024 across data collection, model training and industrial applications [S2][S3]. At CIIF 2025 (23 Sep 2025) it launched JAKA EVO, marketed as an "AI + industrial intelligent robot operating system", together with Kargo — a real, distinct logistics model (verified 2026-08): dual 7-axis 5 kg arms on a 0–800 mm lift column inside a gantry frame over an omni AMR base with integrated cargo deck, ~$65k (see kargo/) [S8].

Go-to-market is direct + integrator/distributor sales into factories, leveraging the installed cobot base and automotive-supply-chain validation partners [S3].

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
JAKA attacks the wheeled-humanoid space from the industrial cobot side: proven ±0.05mm-class arms, force-controlled joints, EtherCAT real-time control and an existing Fortune-500 factory customer base — exactly the assets most humanoid startups lack. K1W is positioned as an "out-of-the-box" precision mobile manipulator for sorting, machine tending and inspection rather than a general-purpose humanoid, and is already piloting in at least one Fortune-500 plant [S2]. The withdrawn IPO adds funding uncertainty, but the cobot cash engine and deep-pocketed investors make JAKA a credible, industrially-grounded T2 competitor.
