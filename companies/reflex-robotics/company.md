## Company background
Reflex Robotics builds "affordable, general-purpose robots to automate repetitive work at warehouses and factories" around a deliberately contrarian thesis: ship teleoperated robots into paying operations first, then let autonomy grow from supervised, in-production data ("robots learn from supervised work in real operations, enabling them to become more capable") [S1][S2]. Its wheeled humanoid — compact base, dynamic-height telescoping spine, two arms — deploys with "zero integration and day one readiness", using the customer's existing carts, monitors, scanners and touchscreens; GXO reported the robot was operational within 60 minutes of arrival [S1][S2].

The flagship deal is with GXO Logistics, the world's largest pure-play contract logistics provider: a RaaS pilot (GXO's second-ever RaaS agreement, announced 2024-09-18) at a Fortune 100 retailer's omni-channel fulfillment site, where Reflex robots do tote transfers between automation systems, product/shelf picking, order replenishment and quality assurance [S2]. The Robot Report named Reflex an RBR50 2025 winner for multi-tasking teleoperated wheeled manipulation [S4]. Business model is lease/RaaS only, with reported target pricing below $50,000 per robot [S3]. For scale, Nuevo León's governor Samuel García announced on 2026-02-02 that Reflex will build its manufacturing plant in Nuevo León, Mexico — billed as Latin America's first humanoid-robot factory, making multi-purpose robots for industrial automation [S5].

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
Reflex is the purest test of the teleop-first, autonomy-later business model on a wheeled platform — the model Interact Analysis expects to dominate near-term deployments. Its combination of tiny footprint (<2×2 ft), 50+ lb payload, 16+ h battery and hour-one deployment is aimed exactly at brownfield logistics economics, and the GXO relationship gives it the same marquee 3PL channel Agility uses for bipeds. The Mexico factory signals nearshored volume ambitions at a sub-$50k price — directly relevant to any EU entrant's cost planning. Risks: thin disclosed funding ($7M) for a hardware scale-up, and a demonstrated-but-not-yet-published autonomy transition.
