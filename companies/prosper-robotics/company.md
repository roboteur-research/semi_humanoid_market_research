## Company background
Prosper Robotics was founded in London in 2021 by Shariq Hashme, a former OpenAI engineer (Dota 2 project) and early Scale AI employee. The company is building **Alfie**, a wheeled, dual-arm "robot butler" for private homes, with a contrarian thesis: don't wait for autonomy — ship a teleoperation-first robot where remote human operators (planned in the Philippines) control the robot by VR for hard tasks while automation share grows over time (~20% autonomous at launch) [S1][S2]. Hashme has largely self-funded (~£2M) plus small angel money (Soma Capital among backers), intentionally staying lean (~8 people) and avoiding VC pressure [S1].

Product economics target consumer affordability: hardware from roughly $5k (early figures £5-10k / $10-15k) plus a ~$500/month (earlier £400) subscription covering maintenance, insurance and human teleoperation minutes [S1][S4]. Design signature: a toolbox of **50–100 swappable tool attachments** (mops, suction cups, specialized grippers, task "gloves" against cross-contamination) instead of a universal dexterous hand [S1][S4]. Early prototypes have been tested in Prosper employees' homes; pilot criteria for London testers: no kids, no pets, no stairs. Commercial launch has slipped repeatedly (2023 reporting said market-ready "~2 years"; as of 2025-26 units are still not publicly purchasable) [S4].

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
Prosper matters as Europe's purest consumer-home semi-humanoid bet and as a test of the teleop-subscription business model (mirroring 1X's operator model but at hobbyist-scale funding). Its price target undercuts every industrial player by an order of magnitude, but with ~8 staff and no factory, execution risk is extreme. For an EU entrant focused on B2B, Prosper is not a near-term competitive threat; it is a signal of where home-service pricing expectations may settle, and a potential acquisition target for its teleop tooling.
