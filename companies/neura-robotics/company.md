## Company background
NEURA Robotics was founded in March 2019 by David Reger in Metzingen, initially incorporated as Han's Robot Germany GmbH (reflecting an early tie to Shenzhen's Han's Robot ecosystem), and rebranded to NEURA Robotics in November 2020 when it announced its first cobot, LARA [S6]. Reger — who previously built up the robotics division of the Swiss machine-tool firm Mabi AG — positioned the company around "cognitive robotics", a term the company claims to have coined: robots with fully integrated in-house sensing (force, vision, audio, the patented "OmniSensor") rather than externally added sensors [S6][S1]. The product ladder runs from cobots (LARA, MAiRA) through the MAV mobile platform to the MiPA semi-humanoid service robot and the 4NE-1 bipedal humanoid.

Strategy centers on the "Neuraverse", an app-store-like ecosystem plus NEURON OS in which skills are shared and robots "learn from each other", supported by physical "NEURA Gym" training facilities that generate real-world training data [S1][S8]. Reger's stated ambition: "NEURA is doing for robotics what the iPhone did for smartphones", with a target of five million robots delivered by 2030 [S1] ("several million" on the Series C page [S2]) — vendor-claimed and extremely aggressive relative to any shipped volume to date. The company claims a "multi-billion euro order book" [S3] (third-party reports say ">$1B" [S9]); neither is independently verifiable.

Funding escalated fast: after a €120M Series B in January 2025 led by Lingotto (Agnelli family's investment manager) [S5], NEURA announced in June 2026 a Series C "of up to $1.4 billion — the largest round ever for a full-stack robotics company", led by crypto/stablecoin firm Tether, with strategic participation from Qualcomm, Amazon, Bosch, Schaeffler and the European Investment Bank, plus imec.xpand, Lingotto Horizon and InterAlpen [S2][S3]. The round is CONFIRMED: CNBC reported it on 2026-06-10 (naming Amazon and Nvidia among participants), and it is corroborated by NEURA's own Series C page (with attributed quotes from Tether CEO Paolo Ardoino, Bosch CEO Stefan Hartung, Schaeffler CEO Klaus Rosenfeld, EIB VP Nicola Beer, Amazon VP Nafea Bshara and Qualcomm's Nakul Duggal), a BusinessWire release, and a Tether press release [S2][S3][S11]. Caveats: the "up to" framing implies tranched/conditional commitments rather than $1.4B cash received; the ~$7B valuation is third-party reporting, not confirmed by NEURA [S4]. Confidence: round existence = high (vendor + multiple third parties); cash actually drawn and valuation = medium/low.

Partnerships: Bosch and Schaeffler are simultaneously investors and technology partners (Schaeffler partnership announced Nov 2025); NVIDIA and Qualcomm provide AI compute/connectivity; early distribution ran through Kawasaki Robotics for cobots. NEURA publicly rules out military applications ("We serve humanity") [S7]. Manufacturing is in Metzingen ("Designed and engineered in Germany"); Series C funds are earmarked for serial production scaling, expansion to the US, China and Japan, Neuraverse/NEURA Gym build-out, and industrial 4NE-1 deployment [S2].

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
NEURA is the single most important domestic (German) competitor for any new EU semi-humanoid entrant: same home market, massive war chest, household-name strategic backers (Bosch, Schaeffler, EIB), aggressive consumer pricing (MiPA Home at €9,999 — far below every western competitor), and a full-stack ecosystem story. Its weakness is execution risk: MiPA's delivery date slipped from "2025" (claimed at Automatica, June 2025 [S1]) to a "late 2026 release" [S10]; no verified real-world MiPA deployment exists; and the 4NE-1's on-stage collapse at Computex 2026 [S9] illustrates the gap between marketing and field readiness. Trajectory: scaling up rapidly on capital, still pre-scale on shipped semi-humanoid product.

## Discontinued / other context: 4NE-1 (biped, excluded from study scope)
The 4NE-1 ("for anyone", ~1.80m, ~80kg, claimed 100kg lift, third generation premiered at Automatica 2025) is NEURA's flagship humanoid for industry; MiPA is explicitly the wheeled, near-term, lower-cost sibling for household/care/service [S1][S7]. The contrast matters competitively: NEURA hedges the locomotion question by running both form factors on one software stack.
