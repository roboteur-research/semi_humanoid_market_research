## Company background
Dexterity was founded in 2017 in Redwood City to give industrial robots "human-like dexterity" through what it calls Physical AI — a large orchestrated set of AI skill agents for perception, motion planning and force control that runs on standard industrial arms. Its first products were AI-driven parcel handling and truck-unloading systems (DexR) using machine vision, force sensing and integrated motion planning to handle everything from stiff cardboard to soft polybags [S1]. In March 2025 it launched Mech, a dual-armed mobile manipulator on an omnidirectional rover, branded an "industrial superhumanoid", one week after closing a $95M round at a $1.65B valuation [S1].

Strategy is deliberately anti-humanoid-hype: "Mech is not a humanoid trying to do everything. It's an industrial machine purpose-built for logistics and manufacturing... Where humanoids offer demos, Mech delivers production KPIs across real-world shifts, 24/7" [S2, vendor-claimed]. Dexterity concentrates on the AI layer and partners for hardware scale: Mech's arms are manufactured by Kawasaki, precision motion components come from HiWin, and in October 2025 Sanmina became contract manufacturer, assembling and testing Mechs at its California facilities with NPI, supply-chain and quality services — targeting parcel/ground logistics, e-commerce, retail and air-cargo customers [S2][S3]. Go-to-market is direct to industrial/enterprise customers, with task capability sold as installable software "apps" (truck loading first) on the same hardware [S1].

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
Dexterity stakes out the heavy end of the wheeled semi-humanoid spectrum: 60 kg dual-arm payload, >2.4 m vertical reach and outdoor-capable dock operation — specs no biped or lightweight wheeled humanoid approaches. It validates the "AMR base + two industrial arms + AI" formula for brownfield logistics (matching Interact Analysis's finding that wheeled platforms are the preferred near-term industrial form factor) and the 1-operator:10-robots supervision economics. With Kawasaki/HiWin/Sanmina supply partners it has a credible scaling path Chinese rivals will find hard to dismiss on quality grounds. For an EU entrant, Dexterity is the benchmark competitor in truck loading/palletizing; its weakness is breadth — Mech is optimized for box-flow logistics, leaving lighter-payload, human-adjacent European niches (retail, healthcare, flexible manufacturing) open.
