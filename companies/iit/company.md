## Company background
IIT is Italy's national flagship technology institute and one of the world's best-known humanoid robotics centres thanks to iCub, the open-source child-sized humanoid replicated in dozens of labs worldwide. R1 ("R1 — your personal humanoid", unveiled 2016) was IIT's deliberate attempt to translate iCub-class capability into an affordable wheeled service robot: ~1.2 m tall, ~50 kg, two-wheeled base with castors, extensible torso (1.15-1.35 m), two 8-DoF arms, simplified 2-DoA/4-DoF sensorized hands, and a body built substantially from polymer to hit a target cost "in the range of a small family car," with lower prices at scale [S1][S2][S3]. It shares the iCub software ecosystem (YARP middleware, open APIs, Gazebo simulation, ROS bridge) [S2].

R1's application track has been healthcare/assistive: a joint lab with Fondazione Don Carlo Gnocchi Onlus developed the open "assistive-rehab" framework, using R1 to guide patients through upper-limb/torso rehabilitation exercises and act as a ward assistant, with clinical scenarios developed in Don Gnocchi rehabilitation facilities [S4]; an "HR1" healthcare-assistant variant is documented in literature [S5]. R1 was completed in 2016 and remains an active demo/research platform (shown at IIT's 20th-anniversary event, Dec 2023), but IIT's humanoid investment focus has shifted to the legged ergoCub (with workplace-insurer INAIL) and iCub evolution — R1 was never commercialized in volume [S6].

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
R1 is the clearest European precedent for a "low-cost plastic semi-humanoid for services": it validated the two-wheel + extensible-torso + dual-8-DoF-arm architecture and an automotive-style cost ambition a decade ago, yet never reached the market — an instructive gap between research capability and productization (no sales channel, 1 kg arm payload, research-grade autonomy). For a German entrant, IIT is a technology/talent source (YARP/iCub ecosystem, sensorized skin, series-elastic hands) and a reminder that institutional platforms rarely convert into products without a dedicated commercial vehicle. Trajectory for wheeled semi-humanoids at IIT: de-prioritized in favour of legged ergoCub.
