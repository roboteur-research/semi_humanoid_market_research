## Company background
KIST is Korea's flagship national research institute and ran one of the country's earliest sustained humanoid programs. Its MAHRU/AHRA biped series (mid-2000s, partly with Samsung) included MAHRU-M, a wheeled mobile-humanoid variant: 6-DoF arms, 4-DoF hands, 2-DoF neck, 1-DoF waist on a mobile base, using a dual-network (IEEE 1394 + CAN) control system for coordinated service-task execution [S3][S4].

CIROS ("Center for Intelligent Robotics" service robot), developed from 2005 with robotics labs at SNU, KAIST, Korea University, Sungkyunkwan, Sogang and POSTECH, was its kitchen-assistant line: a 160 cm, 150 kg omnidirectional wheeled dual-arm robot with HUBO-type dexterous hands, demonstrated making salad (knife-slicing cucumbers, tossing, pouring) at Robot World 2012 [S1][S2]. The program faded after ~2012; no commercialization followed.

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
Historical only — but notable as evidence that Korean national labs converged on the wheeled dual-arm form for domestic service a decade before the current commercial wave, and as a talent/technology antecedent to today's Korean ecosystem.
