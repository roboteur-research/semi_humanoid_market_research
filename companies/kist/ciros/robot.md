# CIROS — KIST

> CIROS was KIST's wheeled dual-arm kitchen-assistant research robot (developed from 2005, third version by 2012): 160 cm, 150 kg, omnidirectional base, HUBO-type dexterous hands, best known for autonomously making salad — slicing cucumbers with a knife, tossing with dressing, pouring drinks — at Robot World 2012. Long discontinued, it matters as early Korean national-lab validation of the wheeled semi-humanoid form for domestic service, a decade before the current commercial wave.

Specs (third-party [S1]): height 160 cm, weight ~150 kg, omnidirectional wheeled base; dual arms with dexterous hands "identical to those on HUBO", able to hold tools including a kitchen knife. Sensing: stereoscopic cameras, a Kinect-like 3D IR sensor, a 12-microphone array, two laser rangefinders and six ultrasonic sensors. Demonstrated tasks: cucumber slicing, salad tossing, beverage pouring, dishwasher loading, tray carrying [S1][S2]. Sibling platform note: KIST's MAHRU-M (~2010-11) was a wheeled mobile-humanoid with 6-DoF arms, 4-DoF hands, 2-DoF neck, 1-DoF waist and a dual-network (IEEE 1394 + CAN) real-time control architecture for coordinated mobile manipulation [S3] — together they show KIST explored the wheeled dual-arm form factor systematically in 2005-2012. Neither platform was commercialized; no deployment beyond exhibitions.
