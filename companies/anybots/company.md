## Company background
Anybots was Trevor Blackwell's personal robotics lab turned company. Around 2007 it showed two remarkable prototypes: Dexter, arguably the first dynamically balancing walking biped outside Japan, and Monty, a human-scale two-wheel self-balancing teleoperated robot with two arms and an 18-DoF dexterous hand controlled through VR goggles and a sensor glove [S1][S2]. In 2010 the company shelved the manipulation line and launched QB, a two-wheel balancing telepresence robot (reusing Monty's balancing wheel system) for ~$15k; QB found modest sales, and Anybots faded from the market by the mid-2010s [S2][S3].

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
A very early (pre-deep-learning) proof of the teleop-first, self-balancing wheeled humanoid concept that 1X's EVE later commercialized — and an early example of the classic pivot: dexterous manipulation proved too hard/expensive, telepresence shipped instead. Defunct; historic interest only.
