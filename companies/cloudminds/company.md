## Company background
CloudMinds — renamed Dataa Robotics (达闼) in China — was the original "cloud robotics" company: founded 2015 by telecom veteran Bill Huang on the thesis that robot intelligence should live in a cloud "brain" (HARIX) connected to lightweight robot bodies over secure mobile networks. It raised from SoftBank and Foxconn, was once China's most valuable service-robot unicorn (peak ~¥22.3B), and built a vertically integrated stack: in-house SCA (Smart Compliant Actuator) integrated joint servos (34 of them in the XR-1/Ginger), the HARIX cloud AI/teleoperation platform, and a family of service robots deployed in exhibition halls, telecom stores, schools and COVID-era field hospitals [third-party].

The company was placed on the US entity list in 2020 (blocking its planned US IPO and chip access), and its state-fund-heavy cap table failed to deliver follow-on financing. Salary delays began January 2024, half-pay in February 2024, full wage/social-security stoppage by spring 2024; by late September 2024 the company was effectively at a standstill, facing 20+ lawsuits with court-ordered payments totaling ~¥35.3M ($5.2M) by March 2025 [third-party]. Huang publicly acknowledged the difficulties (April 2025), citing funding-chain rupture and US sanctions rather than insolvency; a January 2025 investment cooperation agreement with Tianjin Jinnan state-owned investment group and an April 2025 joint venture "Hong Kong Boy Robotics" with HK-listed Guohua Group are attempts to restart the story [third-party]. As of mid-2026 the dataarobotics.com site remains online with the Ginger product pages, but there is no evidence of resumed volume production — status: effectively dormant/salvage, robots best classed as discontinued in practice.

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
CloudMinds matters historically and as a cautionary tale: the XR-1/Ginger was the first mass-produced wheeled semi-humanoid with fully compliant actuation (2019), and its SCA actuator line seeded China's integrated-joint supply chain. Its collapse — despite $800M+ raised, real deployments and a ¥698k list price — demonstrates that cloud-dependent service humanoids without a self-sustaining commercial use case burn out even in a booming market. Competitive threat to an EU entrant today: negligible; but its actuator IP, HARIX teleop patents, and talent diaspora feed the current Chinese wave.
