## Company background
MHI is one of Japan's largest heavy-industry conglomerates. Its consumer robot venture, wakamaru (ワカマル), launched September 2005 as a ¥1.575M wheeled "communication robot" for households — arguably the world's first serious attempt to retail a humanoid home robot [S1][S2]. After a 100-unit test sale to Tokyo households undersold expectations, MHI pivoted to B2B rental (reception/events, from ¥20k–120k/day via Daiwa Rakuda Industrial, 2007) before withdrawing the product around 2010 [S2][S3].

MHI has not returned to humanoid/service robotics as a product business since; its robotics activity today is industrial and special-purpose. Wakamaru's significance is genealogical: it validated the child-height, wheeled, gesturing-arms communication-robot formula that SoftBank's Pepper commercialized at scale a decade later.

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
Historical only — no current offering, and no announced re-entry. Relevant as the archetype (and first commercial failure) of the Japanese communication-robot category.
