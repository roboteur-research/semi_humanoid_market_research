# wakamaru (ワカマル) — Mitsubishi Heavy Industries

> Discontinued pioneer: a 1 m, 30 kg yellow wheeled communication robot sold to households from September 2005 at ¥1,575,000 — the first retail attempt at a conversational home humanoid. Arms were for gesturing only (no manipulation). Sales flopped (100-unit Tokyo test sale), the model pivoted to event/reception rental in 2007, and was withdrawn around 2010. It is Pepper's most direct Japanese ancestor and the category's founding cautionary tale.

Wakamaru was MHI's bet that a conglomerate could open a consumer robot market: a child-height wheeled robot that made eye contact, recognized family members, spoke proactively, and connected to the internet for news, weather and remote monitoring of elderly relatives [S1][S3]. The September 2005 launch was a deliberately limited test sale of 100 units to ordinary households in Tokyo at ¥1.575M — roughly the price of a car for a robot that could talk but not fetch [S1]. Demand fell far short, and by June 2007 MHI repositioned wakamaru as a B2B rental for receptions and events (about 30 event deployments incl. Expo 2005 Aichi; day rates ¥20k–120k via Daiwa Rakuda Industrial) [S2]. The product was quietly withdrawn around 2010 with total volumes believed well under a few hundred units (estimated). Its failure mode — high price, no physical utility, novelty-driven demand — defined the pattern later repeated at larger scale by Pepper, and is the baseline argument for why current-generation semi-humanoids emphasize manipulation payload over conversation.
