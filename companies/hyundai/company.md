## Company background
Hyundai Motor Group's in-house Robotics Lab (est. 2018 under Dong Jin Hyun) develops service robotics adjacent to the car business: showroom/customer-service robots, indoor delivery, automated EV charging arms, parking robots and industrial wearables. **DAL-e** (2021) was its first customer-facing humanoid-styled robot — a 1,160 mm, 80 kg wheeled greeter piloted in a Seoul showroom [S1]. The humanoid form did not carry forward: the follow-on **DAL-e Delivery** (introduced Dec 2022, redesigned April 2024) dropped the arms entirely in favor of a cargo-cabinet body on four Plug & Drive (PnD) wheel modules, with 10 kg payload, 1.2 m/s, KISA-certified 99.9% face-ID door opening, and smart-building elevator/door integration; deployed at IGIS's "Factorial Seongsu" smart office building from Q2 2024 [S3][S4].

The group's headline robotics move was acquiring control of **Boston Dynamics** (June 2021, ~80% across HMC/Mobis/Glovis and Chung Euisun personally, valuing BD at $1.1B). Boston Dynamics' electric Atlas humanoid (legged — excluded from this semi-humanoid study) is being prepared for deployment in Hyundai's US Metaplant, making HMG one of the world's biggest humanoid investors overall, just not in the wheeled semi-humanoid category. Robotics Lab also ships wearable exoskeletons (X-ble Shoulder, 2024-25) commercially.

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
HMG's trajectory is instructive rather than threatening within this segment: it tried a gesturing wheeled semi-humanoid for customer service (DAL-e), concluded arms added little value for delivery/service tasks, and pivoted to armless logistics robots — while betting on fully legged Atlas (via Boston Dynamics) for industrial manipulation. DAL-e itself was a limited showroom pilot, never a product line. HMG matters as a potential large-scale adopter (factories, showrooms) and as Boston Dynamics' owner, not as a semi-humanoid vendor.
