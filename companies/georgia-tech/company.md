## Company background
Georgia Tech ran several influential semi-humanoid research programs around 2008-2014. The Healthcare Robotics Lab (Charlie Kemp) built Cody, a robotic-nursing platform (Segway omni base + torso lift + two Meka SEA arms) used for the first serious robot bed-bathing and patient-hygiene studies. Kemp later co-founded Hello Robot (Stretch), carrying the assistive-manipulation agenda to market in single-arm form.

Compact notes on the other two platforms: **Golem Krang** (Mike Stilman's Humanoid Robotics Lab, ~2010) was a large two-wheel dynamically balancing dual-arm robot (Schunk arms, ~150 kg class) that could sit, stand and balance while manipulating whole-body loads — one of the few balancing dual-arm robots ever built; work ended after Stilman's death in 2014. **Simon** (Andrea Thomaz's Socially Intelligent Machines Lab, 2009) was a pedestal-mounted upper-torso social robot with Meka arms and an expressive ear-lit head, used for learning-from-demonstration and social-HRI studies; Thomaz went on to co-found Diligent Robotics (Moxi hospital robot).

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
Academic/historic only, but the alumni pipeline is directly commercial: Kemp → Hello Robot, Thomaz → Diligent. Cody's nursing studies remain among the only published evidence on patient acceptance of robot physical care — useful market-validation literature for anyone targeting healthcare with a wheeled dual-arm platform.
