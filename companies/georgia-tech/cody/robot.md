# Cody — Georgia Tech Healthcare Robotics Lab

> Cody (2009) was Georgia Tech's robotic-nurse research platform: a Segway omnidirectional base, Festo linear torso lift and two 7-DoF Meka series-elastic arms. It produced the first published robot bed-bathing / patient-hygiene studies — foundational evidence for healthcare use of wheeled dual-arm robots. Retired.

Cody combined a Segway RMP 50 Omni mecanum base (3-DoF holonomic), a 1-DoF Festo linear actuator torso lift and two 7-DoF Meka A1 anthropomorphic arms with series-elastic actuators — 18 DoF total; 155 cm tall, 136 kg, 1.26 km/h [S1][S2]. Wrists carried ATI 6-axis force/torque sensors and a forearm tactile pressure array; vision came from a Point Grey camera and Kinect; compute was two Mac Minis plus a real-time PC, running Python/ROS on Ubuntu [S1]. Its signature research: wiping patients clean with a padded "bath mitt" end-effector using compliant force control (2010-11 bed-bath studies, including trials probing how patients interpret robot touch), and coordinated base+arm door/drawer opening [S1][S3]. The platform validated that low-force compliant arms plus an omni base can perform intimate physical care tasks safely — still-cited evidence for the healthcare semi-humanoid use case. Lab lead Charlie Kemp later co-founded Hello Robot.
