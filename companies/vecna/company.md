## Company background
Vecna Technologies, a Cambridge MA healthcare-IT and robotics R&D firm founded by MIT alumnus Daniel Theobald, developed the BEAR (Battlefield Extraction-Assist Robot) from ~2004-05 with US Army TATRC funding: a hydraulic dual-arm torso on articulated tank treads that could dynamically balance upright and carry a 227 kg casualty [S1][S2]. Several prototypes were built and tested (including soldier-evaluation exercises at Fort Benning ~2010-11), but it never passed into a program of record and development stopped around 2011 [S2].

The robotics group was spun out in 2018 as Vecna Robotics, which today is a materials-handling automation company (autonomous forklifts, pallet jacks, tuggers with Pivotal orchestration) — no humanoid or dual-arm products [S3].

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
Historic curiosity with a differentiating lesson: BEAR remains one of very few semi-humanoids ever built around heavy whole-body lifting (227 kg — two orders of magnitude beyond today's gripper payloads), showing hydraulics + tracked dynamic balancing as a path to "strong" humanoids. No competitive relevance today; Vecna Robotics competes in AMR logistics, not humanoids.
