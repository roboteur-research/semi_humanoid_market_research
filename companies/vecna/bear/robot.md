# BEAR (Battlefield Extraction-Assist Robot) — Vecna

> BEAR (~2005-2011) was Vecna's hydraulic casualty-extraction semi-humanoid: a dual-arm torso with a teddy-bear head on articulated, dynamically balancing tank treads, able to lift and carry 227 kg (a wounded soldier). Army-funded prototype line, discontinued without fielding — still the payload record-holder among semi-humanoids.

BEAR combined a hydraulically actuated upper body (two 6-DoF arms) with two 4-DoF articulated tracked "legs": driving low on full tracks for speed and stability, then unfolding to balance dynamically on track tips to stand 195.6 cm tall and carry loads at human height — 20 DoF total, 181.4 kg, up to 21.6 km/h [S1]. A lithium-polymer battery drove the hydraulic pump; sensing included cameras, laser rangefinders, structured-light sensors, joint force sensors, microphones and GPS, with teleoperation (including a motion-capture glove and rifle-mounted controller concepts) plus partial autonomy [S1][S2]. It demonstrated lifting 227 kg (500 lb), carrying human dummies through rubble, and fine handling of fragile objects; soldiers evaluated it at the Army Infantry Center ~2010-11, but TATRC funding lapsed and the program ended without deployment [S2]. The friendly bear face was deliberate — designed to reassure wounded soldiers.
