# ROBEAR (ロベア) — RIKEN / Sumitomo Riko

> Research-only, ended 2015: the third and last of RIKEN's bear-headed patient-lifting robots (RIBA 2009 → RIBA-II 2011 → ROBEAR 2015). A wheeled/castered dual-arm robot that could lift a patient from bed to wheelchair and assist standing, using compliant rubber tactile arms. The host lab closed weeks after launch; never commercialized, but still the canonical demonstration that a semi-humanoid can safely lift an adult human.

ROBEAR was developed by the RIKEN-SRK Collaboration Center for Human-Interactive Robot Research (Nagoya), a joint lab with Sumitomo Riko (ex-Tokai Rubber), led by Toshiharu Mukai [S1][S2]. Its design thesis was gentle high-force manipulation: low-gear-ratio backdrivable actuators plus three layered force/tactile sensing systems so the robot could cradle-lift a person, support standing transfers, and assist rehabilitation without hard contact [S1]. At 140 kg it nearly halved RIBA-II's weight while improving speed and precision [S1]. The bear face was a deliberate "cute" choice for care-recipient acceptance. The collaboration center closed at the end of March 2015 and RIKEN states the project is no longer carried out; no commercialization followed, with cost, certification and facility workflow fit cited by observers as the blockers [S2][S3]. The patient-lifting mission has since passed to Waseda's Moonshot AIREC program (see waseda dossier if present).
