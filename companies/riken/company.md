## Company background
RIKEN is Japan's largest comprehensive national research institute. Its care-robot work ran through the RIKEN-TRI (later RIKEN-SRK) Collaboration Center for Human-Interactive Robot Research in Nagoya, a joint lab with Tokai Rubber Industries — renamed Sumitomo Riko in 2014 — combining RIKEN robotics with the partner's rubber materials expertise ("Smart Rubber" tactile sensors). The lab produced three generations of bear-headed patient-transfer robots: RIBA (2009, could lift a 61 kg person), RIBA-II (2011, 230 kg, lifting from floor level, 80 kg patient), and ROBEAR (2015, 140 kg). [S1][S2]

The collaboration center closed at the end of March 2015 — one week after the ROBEAR announcement — and RIKEN discontinued the line; none of the three robots was commercialized. The lineage remains the most cited example of Japan's 2010s push (and commercial failure) in physical patient-lifting robots, a use case now re-targeted by Waseda's Moonshot AIREC project. [S2][S3]

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
Historical/cautionary reference only: proof that dual-arm wheeled robots can physically lift adults with compliant tactile arms, and equally that cost, safety certification and care-facility economics killed the category a decade ago. No commercial trajectory today.
