## Company background
DexForce was founded in June 2021 by Jia Kui, a computer-vision professor at CUHK-Shenzhen, and grew out of industrial 3D vision (Sim2Real synthetic data for bin picking) before building its own humanoids. Its distinguishing technology thesis: train manipulation skills almost entirely in simulation on synthetic data via the DexVerse engine, then transfer to hardware with a "Sim2Real VLA" architecture — DexForce claims the W1 Pro is "China's first product with full end-to-end self-research across AI engine, vision, brain and body" [S1][S2]. The company positions itself as the first in China to commercialize general embodied intelligence at scale, citing partnerships/customers including Huawei, BYD, Foxconn, Panasonic, Toyota and state-owned aerospace/chemical/shipbuilding groups [S1].

The W1 (2024/25) and second-generation W1 Pro (2025) are wheeled dual-arm humanoids for parts assembly, inspection, logistics and service scenarios, with pure-vision (binocular) perception rather than lidar-first navigation, 1000 Hz whole-body control and optional five-finger dexterous hands [S1][S3]. Funding accelerated through 2025-26 to a ~RMB 1B Series B (June 2026) at a reported ~RMB 10B valuation, with Forbes Asia 100 recognition (2025) and reported HK IPO preparation [S2].

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
DexForce is the strongest Chinese exponent of the synthetic-data/Sim2Real route to industrial manipulation — a capital-efficient alternative to teleoperation data farms (AgiBot/Galaxea approach). If its claim of skill deployment without on-site data collection holds in practice, it undercuts a key cost driver of the entire category. Its industrial pilot list (BYD, Foxconn-class) makes it a serious mid-term threat in EU-relevant manufacturing niches, though its stack is closed-source and China-centric today. Trajectory: scaling up fast on fresh Series B capital.
