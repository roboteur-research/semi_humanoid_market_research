## Company background
SwitchBot began in 2015 with a Kickstarter-funded "robot finger" that mechanically pushes existing buttons — a retrofit philosophy (automate the home you already have) that it scaled into one of the largest independent smart-home ecosystems, with strong consumer channels in Japan, North America and Europe and native Matter/Home Assistant integration [S3]. Core technology claims: robot localization/mapping, AI machine vision, and distributed neural control networks accumulated across millions of shipped devices [S3].

The Onero H1 semi-humanoid, revealed at CES 2026, is the company's step from device-level automation to whole-home embodied robotics under a "Smart Home 2.0" narrative: a ~1.3 m wheeled robot with dexterous arms/hands that acts both as a manipulator (laundry folding, dishes, window wiping) and as an orchestration hub for the existing SwitchBot device fleet [S1][S2]. The parent's rebrand to "OneRobotics" and a filed HK IPO signal that home robotics is now the corporate thesis, with the consumer distribution machine (Amazon, retail, D2C in 100+ markets) as its unfair advantage.

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
SwitchBot is the most credible near-term consumer channel play in wheeled humanoids: unlike robotics startups, it already owns global consumer distribution, brand trust, support infrastructure, and an installed smart-home base for the robot to control. Official store pricing of USD 9,999 ("coming soon") — against CES press chatter of $1,000–1,500 targets — still resets consumer price expectations far below industrial humanoids. If the H1 ships at scale, it defines the entry-level of the category the way its Bot defined retrofit smart homes. Trajectory: scaling up (pre-production, retail launch promised later 2026).
