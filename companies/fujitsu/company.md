## Company background
Fujitsu is Japan's largest IT services company; Fujitsu Frontech is its front-end hardware subsidiary (ATMs, POS, biometrics). In the mid-2000s Japanese service-robot wave, Fujitsu Laboratories and Frontech jointly developed the enon service robot (prototype 2004, limited sales from 13 Sep 2005) for guidance, escorting, object transport and security patrol in offices and commercial facilities [S1]. Deployments included shopping-center guidance and a museum guide role (Kyotaro Nishimura Museum, 2007) [S2]. Marketing faded around 2010–2012 with no successor; enon no longer appears in Frontech's portfolio, and Fujitsu's later "robot" work (e.g. Robopin, 2016) was a small desktop gesture device, not a service humanoid [S3].

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
Historical only. Enon is a representative casualty of the 2005 first wave: a technically competent multi-task wheeled dual-arm robot (10 kg cargo bay, 0.5 kg-per-arm handling) that never found unit economics beyond pilot sales. No re-entry signals.
