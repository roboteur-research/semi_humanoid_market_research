## Company background
Honda is the automaker with the deepest humanoid heritage anywhere: the P2 (1996) — IEEE-Milestone-recognized as the turning point of self-contained bipedal humanoid research — through ASIMO (2000-2018). After retiring ASIMO, Honda redirected that robotics stack away from legs and toward "ability extension": in September 2021 it announced the Honda Avatar Robot, a teleoperated wheeled dual-arm robot whose centerpiece is an ASIMO-derived multi-finger hand (4 fingers, 16 joints, driven by 11 forearm motors via wires) combined with AI "shared autonomy". Technology demonstrations ran from FY2023; Honda targets practical use in the 2030s, naming remote work, emergency medicine and space as use cases (a lunar/remote teleoperation collaboration with JAXA is part of the same program family). [S1][S2][S3]

Honda R&D frames the avatar robot as "a second self that transcends time and space" — maximizing time value rather than replacing labor — and is pursuing open innovation with external partners for the 2030s rollout. [S1][S3]

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
Honda is a sleeping giant in this category: world-class hand hardware (14 grasp postures covering ~80% of daily hand use), force-sensing fingertips inherited from ASIMO's object-state sensing, mass-manufacturing capability, and a brand synonymous with humanoids. But its explicit 2030s timeline means it exerts no near-term commercial pressure; the risk to incumbents is a well-capitalized late entry once teleop/shared-autonomy semi-humanoids standardize. Watch the hand IP — it is among the best-documented dexterous teleop hands from any automaker. [S1][S2][S3]
