## Company background
DLR's Institute of Robotics and Mechatronics (part of the DLR Robotics and Mechatronics Center, RMC) in Oberpfaffenhofen is one of the world's most influential robotics research labs. Its signature contribution is torque-controlled lightweight robotics: the LWR (Light-Weight Robot) arm series developed there was licensed to KUKA and became the LBR iiwa, arguably the archetype of the modern cobot. The same joint-torque-sensing technology base underpins the institute's humanoid systems and has seeded much of the German robotics industry through technology transfer and alumni — Franka Emika (Sami Haddadin, ex-DLR), Agile Robots SE (Zhaopeng Chen, ex-DLR), and Reactive Robotics among others [S1][S4].

Justin, first shown in 2006 as a fixed dual-arm torso and publicly presented as the mobile "Rollin' Justin" in 2008-2009, is the institute's flagship semi-humanoid: a 1.91 m wheeled mobile manipulator built from LWR-III arms and DLR Hand II four-finger hands [S1][S2]. A second variant, Agile Justin (2012), received faster arms (~1.5x via changed gear ratios), new bus/wheel electronics and whole-body coordination good enough to throw and catch balls [S2]. Justin became famous for dynamic demos (catching flying balls with ~80% success, preparing coffee, cleaning windows) and, more strategically, as the ground segment of DLR/ESA space telerobotics: METERON SUPVIS-Justin (astronaut Paolo Nespoli commanding Justin from the ISS, 2017) and the multi-year Surface Avatar campaign (2023-2025), in which ISS astronauts (Frank Rubio 2023, Tracy Dyson/Jeanette Epps 2024, Jonny Kim 2025) commanded a heterogeneous robot team including Rollin' Justin in a simulated Mars environment, with scalable autonomy from direct teleoperation to task-level commands [S3][S5].

DLR is not a commercial vendor: Justin is a one-off research platform and is not for sale. Its market relevance is indirect but large — it defined the technical template (torque-controlled compliant arms, multi-fingered hands, wheeled base with sprawling suspension, shared-autonomy teleoperation) that European semi-humanoid startups now productize, and DLR actively licenses technology and spins off companies.

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
High indirect relevance for a German entrant: DLR RMC is the national talent pool and technology source for torque-controlled manipulation (hiring ground and potential licensing/cooperation partner), and Justin's 15+ year demo record sets the credibility bar for compliant bimanual mobile manipulation in Germany. No commercial threat as a product, but DLR-descended startups (Agile Robots, Franka successor entities) are competitors-by-proxy. Space telerobotics (Surface Avatar) keeps the platform funded and visible through at least 2025 [S3].
