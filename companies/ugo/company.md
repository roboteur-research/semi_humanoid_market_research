## Company background
ugo was founded in 2018 as **Mira Robotics** by Ken Matsui, initially pitching a teleoperated laundry-folding home avatar. It pivoted to B2B "avatar robots" for building security and facility management, rebranding as **ugo, Inc.** with a ¥225M raise [S1][S2]. Generations: ugo gen-1 (2019), gen-2 (2020, security pilots), gen-3 (2021), **ugo G4 / today's ugo Pro** (2022). The model is teleop-first hybrid autonomy: robots patrol autonomously on mapped routes and escalate to a human pilot for exceptions — a pragmatic path to revenue that predates the current "physical AI" wave [S1].

Strategically ugo sits at the center of Japan's robot-industrial complex: **Yaskawa Electric and Kawasaki Heavy Industries took strategic stakes in 2022**, NTT Group invested 2023, and the **April 2026 Series B3 was led by NTT West** with JR East Startup, Daiwa House, Tokyo Gas, Hankyu Hanshin, EXEO, Denkyo Group and an Edion-linked retail fund — a who's-who of facility owners that doubles as a customer pipeline [S3]. Cumulative funding is ~¥4B. Funds go to physical-AI R&D, mass production, and new products including a NEDO-supported multi-legged outdoor inspection robot and a "general-purpose humanoid" national-competition entry [S3]. ugo holds J-Startup designation (METI, 2025) [S1].

Go-to-market is RaaS: monthly-fee robots sold through security/facility-management partners (Taisei Co. Nagoya, Toyo Tech, Telwell West Japan) rather than direct. In October 2025 ugo opened a second business line — selling the **ugo Pro R&D** imitation-learning kit (LeRobot-integrated, bilateral haptic teleop) to labs and corporate AI teams, monetizing its teleop stack as a data-collection tool [S4].

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
ugo is Japan's most commercially grounded semi-humanoid company: real multi-year, revenue-generating deployments in office towers, universities and HQ buildings doing security patrol, elevator riding and card-reader/button operation with functional (if light-payload) 7-axis dual arms. Its strength is the operations layer (ugo Platform, human-in-the-loop escalation), not manipulation payload. Trajectory is scaling up — new capital, blue-chip strategic investors, and a pivot to physical AI/imitation learning. For an EU entrant, ugo is the reference model for "teleop-first, autonomy-later" B2B service robotics economics in Japan, and its investor web makes the Japanese building-services channel hard to enter without a local partner.
