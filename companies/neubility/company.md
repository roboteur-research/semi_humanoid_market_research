## Company background
Neubility is a Seoul-based autonomous-robot startup best known for Neubie, a boxy four-wheeled sidewalk delivery robot that navigates with camera-based V-SLAM instead of lidar to keep unit cost low. Neubie has delivered food and packages in Korea since 2021 (golf courses with Samsung Welstory, campsites, a 7-Eleven delivery pilot in Seoul/Incheon) and is sold as Robotics-as-a-Service; the company claimed >100 units/month manufacturing capacity and a plan for 400 deployed robots by end-2023 [S2][S3].

In March 2026 Neubility announced its entry into semi-humanoids with "Billy", a half-human/half-vehicle robot that mounts a dual-armed, five-fingered humanoid upper body on a four-wheeled cargo vehicle base — explicitly positioned against bipedal humanoids for industrial and delivery work [S1][S4]. VP Kang Ki-hyuk framed the strategy: "The essence of humanoid robots is not copying the human form, but achieving work range and capabilities that surpass humans" [S4].

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
Neubility is a datapoint for the Korean industry-wide shift (with T-Robotics and I.L) toward wheeled semi-humanoids over bipeds, documented by Seoul Economic Daily (2026-03-08). Its differentiators are a proven outdoor mobile-robot fleet, low-cost vision-only navigation, an existing RaaS/customer base (SK, Samsung, retail), and a cargo-carrying base concept that merges last-mile delivery with manipulation. Billy is still pre-prototype (concept video only as of March 2026), so threat level is currently low but the go-to-market channel is real.
