## Company background
Weave Robotics is a YC S24 startup founded in 2024 by Evan Wineland (ex-Apple AI PM) and Kaan Dogrusoz with the explicit philosophy of "shipping before demos": "Robots aren't useful unless they're deployed" [S1]. Its original YC pitch promised personal home robots shipping in 2025 (first 30 units) [S2]. It delivered by de-scoping: Isaac 0, a stationary laundry-folding robot, launched in February 2026 and has been delivered to homes and businesses "nearly every week since", accumulating 2,000+ field hours and folding 1,000+ lbs of laundry per week across the fleet [S1, vendor-claimed].

Isaac 1, launched July 2026, is the mobile follow-on: a wheeled, collapsible-torso semi-humanoid for laundry and daily tidying, priced at $7,999 or $449/month with a refundable $250 deposit, shipping fall 2026 to California first and the broader US through 2027 [S3]. Weave is vertically integrated — it designs its own actuators, end-effectors, linkage systems, remote actuation (teleop) system and safety systems, and assembles in California [S1]. The operating model is autonomy-by-default with human teleoperation fallback "to guarantee we complete tasks" — the same playbook as 1X's NEO but at roughly half the hardware price. Design language leans domestic: swappable soft fabric shells (five colorways), physical camera shutters, and a furniture-like charging "home" with privacy screen [S1][S3].

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
Weave sets the current Western price floor for a two-armed mobile home robot ($7,999 — below Sunday's ~$10k target and 1X NEO's $20k) and is one of only two US vendors actually taking consumer orders for one in 2026. Its wheeled base + telescoping torso + teleop-fallback formula is exactly the pattern the market study identifies as the pragmatic home configuration. Trajectory: scaling up from a shipped stationary product to mobile — evidence of unusual execution discipline for a seed-stage team, but fleet size remains tiny and funding thin; watch whether deliveries actually begin fall 2026 and whether the teleop labor model scales economically. For an EU entrant it is both a template (de-scoped, shippable home product) and a warning that the sub-$10k consumer price point is already being claimed.
