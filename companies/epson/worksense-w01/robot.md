# WorkSense W-01 (ワークセンス W-01) — Seiko Epson

> Discontinued autonomous dual-arm industrial robot (2017–2020): a torso-format robot with two independent 7-axis arms, four head cameras plus two arm cameras, Epson precision force sensors and multipurpose hands, on a manually wheeled stand. Pitched as one flexible cell for small-lot assembly that "sees, senses, thinks, works" with human tools and jigs. Epson ended sales 31 Dec 2020 after ~3 years — a key datapoint on why pre-AI dual-arm humanoid-format cells failed commercially.

WorkSense W-01 was Epson's attempt to leap from SCARA market leadership to autonomous general-purpose assembly: the robot located parts in 3D by itself, adapted to imprecise part presentation, and could be wheeled to wherever demand arose — replacing "a complicated multi-robot setup" for small-lot, high-mix production [S1][S2]. It launched commercially in Japan from winter 2017/18 and in Europe at automatica 2018, with at least one global manufacturing customer piloting in Europe [S2]. Epson terminated sales on 31 December 2020, announcing (notice dated 23 Mar 2021) that it would concentrate resources on core sensing/smart-automation technologies and carry the knowledge into its wider robotics business; support runs to end-2027 [S3]. No installed-base figures were ever published (estimated: very low double digits at most). The commercial lesson repeatedly cited for this generation: without AI task generality, a dual-arm cell costs more than two conventional arms plus fixturing while being slower — the exact gap that VLA-era semi-humanoids now target.
