## Company background
Seiko Epson has run an industrial robot business since the early 1980s (born from watch assembly automation) and is the long-standing world market leader in SCARA robots. Its one semi-humanoid venture was WorkSense W-01, an autonomous dual-arm robot announced 22 Nov 2017 and rolled out from winter 2017/2018, aimed at "seeing, sensing, thinking, working" automation of multi-step, small-lot assembly — one wheeled cell replacing a multi-robot line [S1][S2].

Sales ended 31 December 2020 (support until end-2027), roughly three years after launch, with Epson stating it would concentrate on core sensing/automation technologies and fold the learnings back into its mainstream robot line [S3]. Independent commentary and the product's fate point to the classic dual-arm economics problem: high price and integration complexity versus two cheap single arms or a redesigned line. Epson remains a robotics force in SCARA/6-axis and force sensing, but has shown no intent to return to humanoid-format robots.

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
A rare, well-documented commercial failure of a major automation vendor's dual-arm humanoid-format product (2018–2020). Useful as evidence of why "torso + two arms" struggles in industrial settings without mobility autonomy or an AI-driven generality story — the gap today's VLA-based semi-humanoids claim to close. Epson's force-sensor and precision-actuation assets would make it a credible re-entrant, but nothing is announced.
