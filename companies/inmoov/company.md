## Company background
InMoov is the original open-source 3D-printed life-size humanoid: a head-torso-dual-arm robot with five-fingered tendon-driven hands, designed by sculptor Gaël Langevin so that every part prints on a hobby printer with a 12x12x12 cm bed [S1]. Started as the first open-source 3D-printed prosthetic-style hand (2011/2012, posted to Thingiverse), it grew into the full torso platform controlled by Arduino (custom "Nervo Board") and hobby servos, running MyRobotLab (gesture UI; ROS integrations exist), with sensor options from PIR/ultrasonic to Kinect and Leap Motion [S1][S2]. Build cost is commonly cited under $1,000 plus printing [S3].

InMoov has no legs; builders worldwide mount it on DIY wheeled bases (the Wikipedia lead photo shows a wheeled InMoov), making it the archetypal hobbyist wheeled semi-humanoid. It is reproduced in countless university labs, schools and maker spaces; files are CC BY-NC, so commercial derivatives require Langevin's permission — a licensing constraint that has kept it non-commercial for 14 years [S2].

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
The grandfather of the open semi-humanoid tier: it seeded a decade of talent and derivative projects before LeRobot-era kits (XLeRobot, BamBot) took over with learning-first stacks. Zero commercial threat (NC licence, servo-grade hardware), but high cultural/educational reach.
