## Company background
RoboForce was founded in June 2023 by Leo Ma (CMU) and Calvin Zhou with a contrarian focus: instead of indoor logistics humanoids, build "Physical AI-powered Robo-Labor" for demanding **outdoor** industrial environments — solar construction, mining, ports/shipping, data-center construction, manufacturing, and eventually space. The team aggregates talent from CMU and Michigan robotics plus Amazon Robotics, Google, Waymo, Cruise, Tesla, ABB, Apple and UBTech [S1][S2].

Its robot TITAN is a dual-arm manipulator on interchangeable wheeled or tracked bases, rated 40 kg per arm with a claimed 1 mm manipulation accuracy — pitched at tasks like solar-module installation "from ground to grid" (pick, place, press, twist, connect) in dust, heat and rough terrain. Hardware is co-designed with RF-Net, RoboForce's 3D foundation model for spatial awareness in unstructured outdoor scenes [S1][S3].

Commercial cadence: public debut at Intersolar (San Diego) February 2025; pilots with early customers through 2025 (incl. demonstration at the Three Rivers Solar installation); commercial launch targeted end-2025; "available in USA" per third-party robot databases in 2025-26. NVIDIA's Jensen Huang name-checked TITAN in his GTC keynote on October 28 2025. The company claims 11,000+ robot orders via letters of intent across six industries and certified early partners in 12+ countries — treat as vendor-claimed LOI pipeline, not firm purchase orders [S1][S3][S4]. The $52M YZi Labs round (March 2026) funds manufacturing and supply-chain scale-up.

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
RoboForce is the leading US attempt to take the dual-arm wheeled form factor **outdoors** — a segment (solar farms, mining) that most semi-humanoid players ignore and where European EPC/energy customers are plentiful. Its 40 kg/arm payload and tracked variant differentiate it sharply from indoor tabletop rivals. For an EU entrant, RoboForce is less a direct competitor in factory/lab niches and more a signal that outdoor industrial semi-humanoids can attract top-tier validation (YZi Labs, NVIDIA keynote) — and a likely future competitor in EU utility-scale solar. Claims discipline matters: the 11,000-unit figure is an LOI number and should be quoted as vendor-claimed.
