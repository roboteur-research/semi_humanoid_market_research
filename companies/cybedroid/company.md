## Company background
Cybedroïd was a hand-built humanoid startup from Limoges making Leenby, a 1.35 m wheeled "butler" semi-humanoid for companionship, reception and light assistance, aimed particularly at elderly users [S1][S2]. After a €600k raise (May 2017) it planned 25 units in 2017 and 300 in 2018, deliberately avoiding larger dilutive rounds ("finding a lot of money means losing control of the company" — Raimbault) [S1]. By 2019 only ~10 Leenbys existed: 6 sold (companies, nursing homes, research centres), 3 rented for events, 1 in a six-month reception pilot at a Crédit Agricole branch in Toulouse [S3]. The company became insolvent in mid-2019 — just as it prepared a small factory for industrialization — and was liquidated in July 2019 with 11 employees [S2]. (Discovery note said "dead ~2018"; correct date is liquidation July 2019.)

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
Cautionary tale for the segment: artisanal production, sub-scale funding (€600k against hardware industrialization costs), and a companionship/reception use case with weak willingness-to-pay killed the company despite genuine early customers. The pattern — pilot traction without a repeatable ROI case — recurs across European social semi-humanoids (cf. Aldebaran/Pepper).
