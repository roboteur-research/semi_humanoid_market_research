# Leenby — Cybedroïd (T3 compact)

> Leenby was a 1.35 m hand-built wheeled semi-humanoid "butler" from Limoges startup Cybedroïd (2011-2019): reception, companionship and light assistance for eldercare and events. Roughly 10 units ever existed; the company was liquidated in July 2019 after failing to fund industrialization. It matters only as a cautionary tale on sub-scale funding and weak use-case economics in social semi-humanoids.

Leenby was a small white humanoid, 1.35 m tall, on a wheeled base (tricycle-style skirt), with two gesture-capable arms and hands able to shake hands, a face whose gaze followed the interlocutor, speech interaction for simple Q&A and orientation, and a vendor-claimed ~10 h battery autonomy [S1][S3]. It was semi-autonomous, not a manipulation platform — assistance was social/informational (greeting, guiding, small daily tasks). Traction by 2019: ~10 robots in circulation — 6 sold to companies, nursing homes and research centres, 3 rented for events, and one six-month public-reception pilot at a Crédit Agricole branch in Toulouse [S3]. Cybedroïd raised €600k (announced 23 May 2017) and targeted 25 units in 2017, 300 in 2018; the scale-up never happened and the company entered liquidation in July 2019 with 11 staff [S1][S2]. No specs beyond the above were ever formally published (DoF, weight, sensors: n/a — not disclosed).
