## Company background
UBTech was founded in Shenzhen in 2012 by James Zhou around in-house servo actuator development and grew through consumer/education robots (Alpha, Jimu) into commercial service robots (Cruzr, 2017) and full-size humanoids (Walker line since 2018). It listed on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange in December 2023 as the first pure-play humanoid robotics IPO globally [S1]. FY2025 revenue reached ~RMB 2.01B (+53% YoY); the humanoid segment exploded from RMB 35.6M (2024) to RMB 820.6M (2025, +2,203.7%), with over 1,000 Walker S-series units delivered in 2025 — which UBTech claims is the only four-digit annual humanoid delivery volume worldwide [S2]. The company remains loss-making but narrowed its net loss in 2025 [S2].

Strategically UBTech runs a two-track embodiment portfolio: bipedal Walker S for automotive/3C factory work (NIO, BYD, Foxconn, Zeekr pilots widely reported) and, since August 2025, the wheeled full-size Cruzr S2 for commercial and light-industrial scenarios where wheels beat legs on cost, runtime and stability. Its software layer (Co-Agent multi-robot collaboration, BrainNet 2.0) is shared across form factors [S3]. Go-to-market is direct in China plus distributors abroad; in Japan, GA Robotics (Kawaguchi, Saitama, founded Feb 2025) began selling Cruzr S2 on 15 Oct 2025 with WMS/API integration and cloud fleet management [S4].

Manufacturing is in-house (Shenzhen), with vertical integration of servo joints — a legacy differentiator. UBTech also keeps the older Cruzr (2017) service-robot line alive in reception/guidance niches, giving it nearly a decade of wheeled service-robot deployment experience that most humanoid startups lack.

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
UBTech coined the launch phrase "full-size general-purpose wheeled humanoid" (全尺寸通用轮式人形机器人) for Cruzr S2 and is the most visible Chinese company legitimizing the wheeled form factor at full industrial scale (185 kg, 15 kg payload). Its listed-company resources, actuator vertical integration, dexterous-hand program (4th/5th-gen hands) and existing Walker customer base make it a top-tier threat: it can bundle wheeled + biped fleets under one orchestration stack, something an EU entrant cannot easily match. Japan distribution from late 2025 shows active internationalization.
