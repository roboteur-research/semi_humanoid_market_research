## Company background
AeiROBOT ("A Robot for All", 에이로봇) was founded in 2018 as a spin-off of Professor Han Jae-kwon's humanoid team at Hanyang University ERICA. Han is one of Korea's best-known humanoid researchers (RoboCup adult-size competitor lineage; the joint AeiROBOT–Hanyang team won the "Best Own-Developed Humanoid" award at RoboCup). The company's signature technology is a self-developed **linear actuator** (in development since 2021): no reduction gear, high current sensitivity, back-drivable, low-noise, low-inertia for impedance/force control, with heatsink-integrated frame and real-time FOC motor drives — a deliberate contrast to the rotary-joint approach of most humanoids and often compared in Korean press to Tesla Optimus' linear-muscle approach [S1][S2].

Product strategy is two-pronged by terrain: the **bipedal ALICE 4** for shipyards and construction sites (ladders, stairs) and the **wheeled semi-humanoid ALICE M1** for flat manufacturing floors [S2]. AeiROBOT is a hardware lead in South Korea's government-backed **K-Humanoid Alliance** (~$770M national program to 2030) and participates in the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy's M.AX (Manufacturing AI Transformation) program. R&D partners include POSCO E&C plus one other construction firm, seven shipbuilders (incl. HD Hyundai Mipo, Samsung Heavy Industries), Hanyang University and Pusan National University [S2].

Commercially the company is early: Series A of ~USD 7.2M was reported in July 2025 [S3], ALICE M1 pre-orders opened at launch in September 2025 [S4], and the first industrial deployment (HL Mando's Wonju steering-components plant) began December 2025 [S5]. Both ALICE 4 and ALICE M1 were shown at CES 2026 in the HUMANOID M.AX Alliance pavilion [S6]. In a July 2026 interview the company argued "cost-effective hardware is the bottleneck" and stated a plan to lower prices by "building robots with robots" [S7].

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
One of only three Korean players with a commercially offered wheeled dual-arm semi-humanoid (alongside Rainbow Robotics RB-Y1 and ROBOTIS AI Worker — Korean media explicitly compare the three [S8]). Its distinctive parallel-link variable-height waist (1.3–1.8 m) and gearless linear actuators are genuine technical differentiators for force-controlled manufacturing work. Backed by national programs and blue-chip industrial pilot partners, but funding (~$7M) is small versus Chinese/US rivals; trajectory is scaling up within Korea's manufacturing ecosystem.
