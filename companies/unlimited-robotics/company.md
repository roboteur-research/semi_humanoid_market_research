## Company background
Unlimited Robotics started with a platform thesis: Gary, launched October 2021, was marketed as a general-purpose two-armed home/office robot ("much more functional than your mobile device") with a $99 waiting-list fee and a planned monthly-subscription model, plus the free Ra-Ya developer platform letting any Python developer write robot apps without robotics background — an explicit "app store for robots" strategy contrasted with "closed-garden" competitors [S3][S4]. Early ambitions spanned homes, hotels, factories, airports; a 2022 target of "500 units by end of year" was not met (analyst note) [S4].

Around 2023-24 the company pivoted decisively to healthcare. Gary units were deployed at Israel's two large public hospitals — Rabin Medical Center (Petah Tikva) and Shamir Medical Center (Be'er Ya'akov), about a dozen units working since early 2024 — doing supply fetching, medication delivery, patient guidance, real-time translation, and consultation transcription into medical records [S1]. Unlimited Robotics signed an agreement with Israel's Ministry of Health to supply robots to public hospitals, and ~10 Garys began working at a Philadelphia hospital in July 2024 (US pilot; specific hospital not named in coverage) [S1][S2]. The May 2024 $5M seed (lool ventures, Wix/Fiverr founders) funds healthcare expansion [S5]. Reported pricing in hospital deployments: ~$30,000 per robot plus a monthly fee [S6].

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
Unlimited Robotics is one of very few companies with dual-arm wheeled robots actually working daily in hospitals — a segment EU entrants covet (labor shortage, reimbursable value, structured environments). Its differentiators are the healthcare workflow integration (translation, transcription, MoH channel) and the Ra-Ya SDK; its manipulation capability is modest (5 kg combined). With only ~$8M raised it is thinly capitalized against US/Chinese rivals; trajectory is a focused healthcare niche play scaling through government channels. For an EU entrant it is a template and potential competitor in hospital logistics tenders, but not a hardware-performance benchmark.
