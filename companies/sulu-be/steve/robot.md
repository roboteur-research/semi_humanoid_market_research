## Design & morphology
210 cm, 140 kg. Round black head with animated eyes/mouth display, dual-arm
torso with orange armor styling, flexible-conduit limbs; legs stand on an
orange wheeled cart (drive unit with large front wheels + smaller rear casters,
external electronics box) [S1][S4]. 22 DoF overall; 10 fingers, 2 hand DoF.

## Locomotion
Wheeled cart base; up to 8 km/h (2.2 m/s) — fast for events use, implying
teleoperated/attended driving rather than autonomous navigation [S1].

## Upper body & manipulation
Two arms with styled hands (10 fingers, 2 DoF total in hands); grip strength
~5 kg. Arms are expressive/gestural; no functional manipulation claims [S1].

## Sensing
n/a (not disclosed). Face display + presumably camera/mic for interaction;
no navigation sensor suite disclosed — consistent with operator-driven
character performance [S1].

## Actuation & power
DC motors with custom aluminum gears; battery ~2 h runtime per charge [S1].

## Compute & software
Arduino-based control electronics, WiFi connectivity [S1]. No AI/autonomy
stack, SDK or fleet software disclosed; behavior is scripted/teleoperated
character performance (estimated).

## Safety & compliance
n/a (not disclosed). No standards claimed; 140 kg at up to 8 km/h in public
venues implies attended operation [S1] (estimated).

## Deployment evidence & traction
Real, if niche: deployed as character host in Belgian movie theaters (with
media company Brightfish), corporate receptions, cultural festivals, brand
activations [S1][S2]. Unit counts n/a (bespoke, likely single digits).

## Assessment (analyst view)
*Analyst opinion.* Steve is craftsmanship, not competition: a charismatic
one-off-style character robot with Arduino-era internals. Its only market
signal is that European brands pay ~$60k for humanoid presence at events —
a possible secondary revenue channel for real platforms, and evidence that
"emotional design" sells in hospitality. Zero threat to an EU industrial
entrant.
