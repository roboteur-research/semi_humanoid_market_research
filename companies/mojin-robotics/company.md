## Company background
Mojin Robotics was spun out of Fraunhofer IPA (Stuttgart) to commercialize the fourth generation of the institute's long-running Care-O-bot service-robot line (Care-O-bot research since 1998; Care-O-bot 4 presented 2015). Its flagship deployment was "Paul", a Care-O-bot 4 configured as a retail greeter that welcomed customers and guided them to products at Saturn electronics stores from October 2016 (first in Ingolstadt), plus museum/airport information roles and elderly-care trials [S1][S2][S3]. The company won the 2017 Fraunhofer Founder's Prize.

The humanoid business never scaled beyond small numbers. In 2020 Mojin was acquired into the Schiller Automatisierungstechnik / SCIO Automation group; in June 2022 the mobile-robotics activities of Mojin and Schiller were merged under the new brand **4am Robotics**, and on 13 April 2023 Mojin Robotics GmbH was formally renamed 4am Robotics GmbH — today a pure AMR/intralogistics vendor [S4][S5]. **The Care-O-bot 4 product line is no longer marketed by the company (status 2026: commercially discontinued);** the platform survives as a Fraunhofer IPA research asset and in legacy installations. Care-O-bot 4 units historically cost roughly $66k–232k depending on configuration [S3].

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
Mojin/Care-O-bot 4 is a cautionary and instructive precedent: a technically sophisticated, modular, omni-wheeled European service humanoid (spherical torso/neck joints, dual 7-DoF Schunk arms, ROS) that found charming pilot deployments (retail greeter "Paul") but no repeatable business case in the 2016-2022 window, ending in a pivot to plain AMRs. For a new EU entrant it defines both the German engineering talent base available (Fraunhofer IPA lineage) and the commercial trap of social/service humanoids without a hard ROI task. Trajectory: pivoted away — no longer a competitor in this category.
