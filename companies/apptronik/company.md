## Company background
Apptronik spun out of UT Austin's Human Centered Robotics Lab in 2016. Its founding team contributed actuators and control software to NASA's Valkyrie humanoid (44 DoF, series-elastic actuators) for the DARPA Robotics Challenge, and the company subsequently built 10+ platforms — bipeds (Draco, QDB), upper-body manipulation testbeds (QDA, QDH, Astra), a mobile manipulator (Scorpio) and exoskeletons — before consolidating everything into Apollo, unveiled August 23 2023 [S1][S2]. NASA formalized a partnership with Apptronik in 2022 to accelerate Apollo's commercialization [S6].

Apollo is a 5'8" (173 cm), 160 lb (73 kg) humanoid with 55 lb (25 kg) payload and 4-hour swappable batteries — but crucially it is modular below the waist: it "can be configured as a bipedal walking humanoid, wheeled base unit, or stationary/pedestal-mounted system", and third-party coverage notes legs can be swapped for wheels "in under an hour" [S1][S4]. That makes Apptronik simultaneously a legged-humanoid flagship and a semi-humanoid (wheeled/pedestal) supplier.

Commercially, Apptronik has assembled the strongest partner web in US humanoids: Mercedes-Benz piloting Apollo on factory floors since March 2024 (and investing); Jabil both piloting Apollo in its own plants and serving as contract manufacturer to build Apollo "at automotive-grade scale"; GXO Logistics warehouse deployments scheduled 2026; Google DeepMind partnership (announced December 2024) putting Gemini Robotics foundation models on Apollo alongside NVIDIA GR00T; and the Artemis onboard autonomy stack plus Fleet Connect orchestration [S3][S4][S5]. The staged ~$935M Series A at ~$5B valuation (Google, Mercedes, John Deere, Qatar Investment Authority among backers) is the second-largest war chest in Western humanoids after Figure.

## Relevance to the semi-humanoid market
Apptronik matters to a semi-humanoid study in three ways. First, Astra (circa 2021-22) was a pure semi-humanoid — a dual-armed upper body on fixed stand or AMR — and everything Apptronik learned about human-safe manipulation flowed from it into Apollo. Second, Apollo's modular lower body means the same torso ships as biped, wheeled unit, or pedestal workcell: an explicit admission that many commercial tasks don't need legs, from the best-funded "legged" player. Third, its Gemini Robotics + Jabil manufacturing combination could flood exactly the wheeled/stationary industrial niches an EU entrant targets, with automotive-grade unit costs. Public pilot footage (Mercedes, GXO) so far shows the legged configuration; no named customer has been publicly confirmed running the wheeled/pedestal variants (as of Aug 2026) — worth monitoring closely.
