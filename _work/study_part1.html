1  Executive summary

The semi-humanoid — a human-like dual-arm upper body on a wheeled, tracked or pedestal base — is where humanoid robotics is actually being deployed in 2026. While bipedal humanoids dominate headlines and venture valuations, wheeled dual-arm robots dominate paying work: of the 217 systems documented in this study, 82 are shipping products and a further 31 are formally announced. China's "2026 Wheeled Humanoid Robot Industry Development Blue Book" counts >5,000 wheeled-humanoid units shipped in China in 2025 (≈25–28% of Chinese humanoid volume), and Interact Analysis states plainly that wheeled platforms are "the preferred near-term form factor for real-world industrial deployment." Morgan Stanley's influential humanoid forecasts exclude wheeled-base torsos entirely — meaning the segment this study covers is systematically under-represented in headline market numbers, and correspondingly under-analyzed by competitors.

Seven findings that shape the opportunity:
  • China leads on volume and velocity. 63 of 217 documented robots are Chinese; AgiBot alone shipped 5,100+ units in 2025 and passed 15,000 cumulative robots by June 2026. Real factory orders exist (Fulin Precision ~100× A2-W; Longcheer's hundreds-of-millions-RMB G2 order), and the supplier layer (chassis, actuators, hands) is commoditizing fast — entry prices now reach ¥26,900 (Unitree R1-D) and ~$1,000–1,500 (SwitchBot Onero H1 target).
  • The US pivoted from bipeds to economics. The 2025–26 US wave (Sunday Memo, Weave Isaac at $7,999, Dexterity Mech, Dyna, Walden Robotics at a $1.1B valuation out of stealth with Toyota as investor-customer) sells labor economics — RaaS by the hour, home robots by subscription — not anthropomorphism.
  • Regulation just redrew the map. FCC Public Notice DA 26-786 (28 July 2026) added foreign-produced "advanced robotic devices" to the US Covered List: new foreign-made mobile, networked, AI-enabled robots — wheeled semi-humanoids squarely included — lose US equipment authorization unless granted a conditional approval. This fences the largest single market against Chinese, Korean, Japanese and European vendors alike, and materially raises the value of domestic/allied manufacturing footprints.
  • Europe is strong in research, fragmented in product. PAL Robotics, Pollen/ Hugging Face, Neura's MiPA (€9,999 announced, deliveries slipping), igus's €47,999 Iggy Rob, Humanoid UK's $1.35B valuation and Duatic's ETH-bred Alpha coexist with the cautionary tales of Aldebaran/Pepper (liquidated 2025) and Cybedroid. German research depth (DLR, KIT, TUM, FZI, Fraunhofer lineage) is world-class and under-commercialized — a talent and IP pool a German entrant can draw on.
  • Incumbents are converging on exactly this form factor. Toyota's ELEY (QDD joints, scapular axis, wheeled base), Yaskawa's MOTOMAN NEXT + its quiet ownership of Tokyo Robotics, Midea's six-armed MIRO U, Kawasaki/Foxconn's Nurabot, Hexagon's AEON, plus component giants (Schaeffler backing Humanoid UK, Hexagon and reportedly Neura; LG investing in AgiBot and Dexmate while building its own CLOiD).
  • Data is the second product. Nearly every serious player monetizes demonstration data: AgiBot World's million-trajectory dataset, Sunday's 2,000 paid glove-wearing "memory developers", 1X's security fleet feeding NEO, China's 40+ state-funded training centers. A hardware entrant without a data flywheel strategy competes on margins; with one, on compounding capability.
  • Certification favors wheels — for now. Statically stable platforms certify under existing frameworks (ISO 13482, ISO 10218:2025, ANSI R15.08, UL 3300) while dynamically balancing robots await ISO 25785-1 (2026–27). The EU Machinery Regulation 2023/1230 (applies 20 Jan 2027, with AI-behaviour and cybersecurity requirements) is an opportunity for an EU vendor to lead on compliance-by-design.

Bottom line for a German entrant: the semi-humanoid category is real, growing, and — outside China — not yet locked up. The winning wedge combines (1) an industrial or care use case with demonstrable ROI, (2) compliance-by-design for the 2027 EU regime, (3) a data-collection strategy from day one, and (4) supply-chain positioning that exploits the new US restrictions on Chinese hardware rather than suffering them.
