12  Technology trends

Foundation models made the category investable

Vision-language-action (VLA) models are the demand engine: Physical Intelligence's π0/π0.5 run long-horizon household tasks on wheeled mobile manipulators ($5.6B valuation, ~$1B raise in talks); NVIDIA's GR00T, Google's Gemini Robotics 2 (whole-body control, adapts to new hardware with <200 examples), AgiBot's GO-2, Galbot's GraspVLA (billion-frame synthetic pretraining) and Figure's Helix define the stack. Consequence: hardware is increasingly judged as a data-collection and policy-execution vehicle. AgiBot World's million-trajectory dataset was collected on wheeled G2s; Sunday's 2,000 paid "memory developers" wear gloves whose geometry its robot hand mirrors; China operates 40+ state-funded training centers; 1X's EVE security fleet trained NEO. Teleoperation is not a stopgap but a revenue-generating data business (Reflex, ugo, Beomni) — Interact Analysis expects deployments to remain "predominantly semi-autonomous" near-term.

Hardware convergence

The 2026 median semi-humanoid: two 7-DoF arms; 20–40 total DoF; 3–5 kg per-arm payload (10–20 kg for logistics classes; outliers: Dexterity 60 kg, Duatic 30 kg peak, Realman 5 kg official); 1.3–1.75 m height, often with a lift column or folding torso (floor-to-2 m+ work envelopes: Galbot 2.4 m, AI² 0–2.4 m, Dexmate 0.66–2.2 m); omnidirectional wheels at ~1.5–2 m/s; 5–8+ h runtime with hot-swap batteries; 3D lidar + multi-RGB-D + wrist F/T sensing; NVIDIA Jetson Orin (now Thor: Richtech Dex, Dexmate) as de-facto compute; ROS 2 plus a proprietary autonomy/VLA layer. Actuation splits between QDD/backdrivable philosophies (Toyota ELEY, Duatic DynaArm, Spirit AI full force control) and harmonic/planetary precision claims (±0.03–0.1 mm: JAKA, Elite, iSageBot).

Hands and end-effectors

Parallel grippers still dominate deployments; dexterous hands are differentiating fast: Chinese five-finger hands now start under $1,400 (Inspire ubiquitous; the market grew ~150% YoY into a "mass-production race"), Western options run to Shadow's ~$300k; in-house designs (Pudu DH11, XYZ's 7-DoF 3-finger + Glove X, Sanctuary's hydraulic 21-DoF, PaXini's ITPU tactile arrays) anchor product identity. Tool-changers and media at flange remain rare disclosures — an open differentiation axis for industrial-grade entrants. Notably, several "humanoid" vendors ship off-the-shelf EEs (1X EVE used a Robotiq 3-finger; OpenDroids integrates RealMan hands).

14  Safety, standards & regulation

  • ISO 13482 (personal care robots — mobile servant class fits semi-humanoids): 2014 edition current; revision (retitled toward "service robots", broadened to professional applications, FDIS stage mid-2025) expected imminently.
  • ISO 10218-1/-2:2025 published: industrial robot safety overhauled with functional safety, cybersecurity and collaborative requirements; TS 15066 folded in. ANSI/RIA R15.08 covers industrial mobile robots with mounted manipulators; UL 3300 (service robots in public spaces) joined OSHA's NRTL list Dec 2025.
  • The wheeled certification advantage: statically stable platforms certify under all of the above today; dynamically balancing robots await ISO 25785-1 (committee draft; expected 2026–27). IEEE's humanoid-standards pathway study (2025) puts ratified humanoid standards 18–36 months out.
  • EU: Machinery Regulation 2023/1230 applies 20 January 2027 (no grace period): self-evolving-behaviour assessment, cybersecurity of safety functions, digital documentation — layered with the AI Act. Burden for laggards; moat for compliance-by-design vendors. TÜV-grade certification of a dual-arm mobile platform is an unclaimed European first.
  • USA — the 2026 discontinuity: FCC Public Notice DA 26-786 (28 July 2026) added "foreign-produced advanced robotic devices" to the Secure Networks Act Covered List: any mobile ground robot >4.4 lbs with environmental sensing, ≥200 kbps connectivity and AI autonomy loses FCC equipment authorization for new models unless granted a Department-of-War conditional approval. Fixed industrial robots are exempt; wheeled/tracked semi-humanoids are squarely in scope. Practical effects: Chinese vendors lose the US; Korean/Japanese/European vendors need approvals or US manufacturing ("domestic end product" per 48 CFR 25.101(a)); US integrators re-source components.
  • China: MIIT stood up a humanoid/embodied-AI standardization committee (Nov 2025; Unitree and AgiBot founders as deputy chairs) and unveiled the first national humanoid standard system in March 2026; existing GB/T service-robot safety standards apply meanwhile. Shanghai issued the first humanoid governance guidelines (2024).
Observed reality: among 180 main-matrix robots, published robot-safety certifications are rare to absent — including for market leaders. Oversonic's "industrial + healthcare certified" claim resolves to CE-machinery + ISO 27001. A vendor that can put ISO 13482 / MR-2027-ready CE on a dual-arm mobile platform holds a genuinely scarce asset.

16  Competitive assessment for a German entrant

Where the openings are

  • EU-certified industrial semi-humanoid. No vendor holds robot-safety-grade certification for a dual-arm mobile platform under the incoming MR 2023/1230 regime. German TÜV culture + the domestic research bench (DLR torque control, KIT ARMAR manipulation, Franka-class compliant arms) make compliance-by-design the natural differentiator against spec-sheet-led Chinese imports.
  • Care & hospital logistics in Europe. Demographics guarantee demand; the credible platforms are pilots (GARMI has no spin-off; HoLLiE stayed academic; MiPA is late; Mirokai is logistics-only). ISO 13482 + hospital-grade hygiene + Devanthro-style teleop fallback is an assemblable, defensible package.
  • US market via the FCC wall. A German vendor with US final assembly ("domestic end product") can sell where Chinese rivals now cannot — the single largest regulatory gift to Western robot makers in years. Walden's $1.1B day-one valuation prices exactly this scarcity.
  • Data flywheel as default architecture. Ship every unit teleop-capable and log demonstrations under EU-grade data governance ("GDPR-clean embodied-AI data" is itself a product for European enterprise customers wary of Chinese and US clouds).
  • Component strategy. Schaeffler (actuators; invested across Humanoid UK, Hexagon, reportedly Neura), igus (polymers), Franka/Agile (arms), SICK/IDS (sensing) form a domestic supply chain; the missing integrator role is the opportunity.

Recommended posture

Enter as the certified European industrial/care semi-humanoid with a data flywheel. Concretely: (1) wheeled omnidirectional base + lift torso, dual 7-DoF compliant arms in the 5–10 kg/arm class, tool-changer + media at flange (an undisclosed axis across nearly all competitors); (2) MR-2027/ISO-13482 compliance engineered from day one and certified loudly; (3) launch vertical where ROI is provable within one budget cycle — intralogistics machine-tending or hospital logistics — with RaaS pricing against the $3.5k/mo ADAM benchmark; (4) teleop + demonstration-data capture in every unit; (5) US strategy via local assembly partner from year one; (6) strategic component alliances (Schaeffler/Franka/igus) rather than full vertical integration. Speed matters: the Korean incumbent wave shows how fast listed industrials can pivot, and Chinese price curves compress the window in which "European + certified + trusted" commands a premium.
