2  Scope, definition & methodology

Definition

A semi-humanoid robot has a human-like upper body — head and/or torso with two arms — whose legs are replaced by another locomotion or mounting concept: a wheeled base (differential, omnidirectional/mecanum, swerve, two-wheel balancing or ball-balancing), a tracked base, a lift column, or a fixed pedestal. SoftBank's Pepper is the textbook example; AgiBot's A2-W, Galbot's G1, Rainbow's RB-Y1 and Sunday's Memo are the 2024–26 generation. Included statuses: shipping, announced, prototype, research and notable discontinued systems. Excluded: bipedal humanoids, single-arm mobile manipulators (documented in Annex A where influential — Toyota HSR, Hello Robot Stretch), arm-only cobots, armless telepresence robots, and dual-arm machines without an anthropomorphic upper body. Wheel-leg hybrids (Tencent The Five, GAC GoMate, Hexagon AEON, Moon Dynamics L1) sit in the annex as borderline.

Method & data quality

Research was conducted in August 2026 through multi-lingual web research (English, Chinese, Japanese, Korean, German, French, Spanish, Russian): official vendor documentation and datasheets, trade press, financial media and filings, academic papers, community databases, teardown/forum material, and curated robot-description repositories. Every robot has a dossier (company profile, technical dossier, machine-readable spec sheet, source-attributed imagery — all images individually visually verified). Every spec field carries a per-field source URL and one of three confidence ratings: vendor-claimed (manufacturer statement), third-party (independently reported/verified), estimated (inferred). The companion xlsx preserves this per-field register (~3,400 source rows); the color coding in its matrix mirrors these ratings. Conflicting figures are retained with both values flagged rather than silently resolved. Where a vendor discloses nothing (a frequent Chinese and stealth-startup pattern), fields read "n/a (not disclosed)" — absence of a number is itself signal.

Caveats. This market moves at weeks-scale: 2026 alone brought the FCC action, Mobileye's $900M Mentee acquisition, Walden's $1.1B debut and several WAIC launches. Chinese shipment claims are frequently sell-in, not deployment (Interact Analysis: only ~10% of 2025 humanoid production reached real applications). Analyst forecasts diverge by >25× for 2035; we present ranges, not point estimates.

4  Market size & forecasts

The headline numbers — and why they mislead

Humanoid-robot forecasts span an extraordinary range: for 2035 alone, published market sizes run from ~$9B (Precedence) through Goldman Sachs' $38B TAM to ~$250B (Future Market Insights) — a >25× spread driven by definitions, and long-horizon calls reach $5T by 2050 (Morgan Stanley) and $7T (Citi). Two structural facts matter more than any point estimate:

  • Semi-humanoids are under-counted. Morgan Stanley's definition explicitly excludes "a humanoid torso mounted to a wheeled AMR and other bases that remain stable when powered down." The deployable end of the market is thus largely invisible in the most-quoted numbers — upside for anyone who counts properly.
  • Specialists are far more conservative than banks. Interact Analysis (May 2026): >700k humanoid units/yr and ~$15B revenue by 2035, commercial-viability inflection ~2032; 2025 production >20,000 units (10× 2024) but only ~10% deployed in real applications; China took >90% of production. Bank of America: 18k units 2025E → 1M/yr by 2030; BOM ~$35k end-2025 falling to $13–17k by 2030–35.

What is actually known about the wheeled segment

  • China Blue Book (AGV/AMR Industry Alliance, May 2026): China wheeled-humanoid shipments >5,000 units in 2025, market value >RMB 1.8B (~$250M) — the only hard unit datapoint for the segment; implies wheeled ≈25–28% of Chinese humanoid volume [third-party; derivation estimated].
  • 2025 humanoid actuals (all form factors): Omdia counts 13,318 global shipments with Chinese vendors in the top six (AgiBot 5,100+, Unitree ~4,200–5,500, UBTech ~1,000); Xinhua/CSJ report ~18,000 China-only (+650% YoY). Counting rules differ; both are directionally consistent: China ≈90% of world volume.
  • 2026 outlook: GGII forecasts 62,500 China humanoid shipments; Morgan Stanley doubled its 2026 China forecast to 50,000 units in June 2026; TrendForce sees >700% YoY global growth. Chinese executives talk "100–200k level". Manufacturing deployments are described by China Securities Journal as "essentially all wheeled + dual-arm configurations, because ROI is decisive."
  • Commercial-report shares conflict irreconcilably (wheeled = 40.6% GMI vs 85.3% Roots vs ~37% FMI) — use unit-level evidence, not percentage claims.

Capital formation

Humanoid startups raised $6.1B across 139 deals in 2025 (PitchBook, +300% YoY) and $8.6B in the first seven months of 2026 — 1.8× all of 2025. China alone: ~RMB 39.8B across 325 deals in 2025 (+326%), and ≥RMB 100B invested in 2026 YTD — more than the previous five years combined — atop a ~RMB 1T (~$138B) 20-year state fund and >RMB 70B of municipal funds. Semi-humanoid-relevant rounds include Galbot (~$1.15B lifetime, $3B valuation, backers from CATL to China's IC "Big Fund"), AgiBot (Tencent, BYD, LG; backdoor-listed via Shangwei), AI² Robotics (~$735M at ~$2.8–3B, July 2026), Astribot (unicorn, June 2026), Sunday ($1.15B), Walden ($300M seed at $1.1B), Humanoid UK ($1.35B), Dyna (~$600M), Neura (Series C up to $1.4B, Amazon/Nvidia participation, confirmed June 2026), and Mobileye's $900M acquisition of Mentee. Government pull is Chinese policy: MIIT's 2023 guideline targets world-leading humanoid strength by 2027, with a reported 100,000-robot deployment goal.

5  Why wheels: the form-factor debate

The engineering case for replacing legs is quantitative, and vendors increasingly make it openly:

  • Cost. Galbot founder Wang He: a wheeled chassis is ~10× cheaper than legs and more stable; bipedal locomotion "unnecessary… would increase costs." Wheeled entry prices ($4,290 Unitree R1-D; $7,999 Weave Isaac; ~$14k Astribot T1) sit an order of magnitude below bipedal industrial pilots.
  • Energy. Legged electric locomotion suffers Joule-heating losses at low speed/high torque. Typical bipeds run 2–4 h (Optimus V2 ~2.3 kWh ≈2 h; Unitree H1 864 Wh <4 h static); wheeled semi-humanoids run 5–16 h and increasingly hot-swap (AgiBot A2-W: 2 kWh, 5 h, swappable; Galbot G1: 8 h; Dexmate Vega: 10–30 h claimed).
  • Safety physics & certification. Rodney Brooks: falling legs carry high kinetic energy — his prediction is that "humanoid robots will get wheels for feet." A statically stable base stops rather than falls on power loss, and certifies under existing standards today (see §14). Korea's March-2026 wave (T-Robotics: the wheeled hybrid is our "final form… will never build legs") and the UK's Humanoid ("wheels first") made this an explicit strategy, not a compromise.
  • Engineering focus. Budget not spent on balance goes into arms, hands and autonomy — the parts that earn revenue (1X's EVE-before-NEO data strategy; Dexterity's 60 kg-lift Mech).

The counter-case remains real: stairs, curbs and cable-strewn floors; legs as a positioning system (Agility); the "little teapot" instability of a static base reaching far under load (Melonee Wise); and household TAM arguments that pushed 1X from wheeled EVE to legged NEO. The pragmatic industry answer is increasingly modular lower bodies (Apptronik Apollo wheeled/legged variants; AgiBot X2-N leg-wheel switching) and wheel-leg hybrids (Hexagon AEON, GAC GoMate) — hedges that concede the near-term economy of wheels.
