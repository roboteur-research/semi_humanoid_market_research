6  Regional landscape — China

50 documented robots — the deepest, fastest, cheapest market. Three strata: (1) scale leaders — AgiBot (5,100+ units 2025, 15,000 cumulative by June 2026, Fulin and Longcheer factory orders, backdoor-listed), Galbot ($3B valuation; licensed robot pharmacies, ~170 unmanned kiosks, factory pilots at Mercedes-Benz China/CATL/ Zeekr), UBTech (Cruzr S2, exported to Japan via GA Robotics), Pudu and Keenon riding 100k/80k-unit service-robot install bases; (2) technology specialists — Astribot (10 m/s end-effector speed, $14k T1), PaXini (~2,000 tactile sensors), Spirit AI (full force control), DexForce (Sim2Real), Robot Era (Haier's "HIVA" is its rebadged Q5); (3) incumbent entrants — Midea (six-armed MIRO U in its own washing-machine plant; owns KUKA), JAKA/Rokae/Elite/Topstar/Siasun from the cobot/industrial world, Fourier from rehab, plus ecosystem oddities from four-armed iSageBot to ByteDance's research ByteMini. A dedicated supplier layer (SEER's C1-D humanoid chassis, Realman's joint modules with claimed 50,000 h MTBF, Inspire hands) is commoditizing construction; data-collection platforms (ARX, AgileX, Galaxea, HEXFELLOW) arm the world's embodied-AI labs. Weaknesses: near-zero published safety certifications, sell-in vs deployment gaps, financial distress cases (CloudMinds), and now the US FCC wall.

7  Regional landscape — USA & Canada

39 documented robots (incl. Canada) — the economics wave. The 2025–26 US generation sells labor, not robots: Sunday's Memo (Coatue-backed, $1.15B, glove-collected ~10M episodes, <$10k target, beta by Thanksgiving 2026), Weave's $7,999/$449-mo Isaac, Robot.com's R-Noid and Eden's $10/hour RaaS, Dyna's laundry robots at 99%+ 24-h success, Reflex's teleop-to-autonomy warehouse play (GXO; sub-$50k target; Mexico factory), and Dexterity's 60 kg "superhumanoid" Mech (Kawasaki-built arms, Sanmina manufacturing). Walden Robotics crystallizes the moment: Russ Tedrake's TRI spin-out emerged July 2026 at a $1.1B valuation with robots already in production shifts at a Toyota plant. Public-market reality check: Richtech (NASDAQ: RR) pairs a $180k ADAM install base with $5M revenue and governance flags. Canada contributes Sanctuary's hydraulic-hand Phoenix (now software-pivoted) and Kinova's discontinued MOVO. The deep research heritage — PR2, Baxter, Mobile ALOHA ($32k BOM that spawned Sunday and the ALOHA-clone industry), Robonaut 2, TRI Punyo — remains the field's intellectual substrate. The FCC Covered-List action (§14) now shelters this market from foreign hardware — and pressures everyone else's US strategy.

8  Regional landscape — Europe

34 documented robots — research superpower, fragmented commercially. Active products span PAL Robotics' TIAGo Pro/++/ARI (the EU research standard), Pollen/Hugging Face's open-source Reachy 2 (~$70k), igus's €47,999 plastics-based Iggy Rob, Oversonic's RoBee (STMicroelectronics deal, US launch — but certifications are CE-machinery grade, not robot-safety), Enchanted Tools' ball-balancing Mirokai in French hospitals, and Duatic's ETH-lineage Alpha (12 kg continuous dual-arm payload, IP66). Humanoid (UK) became Europe's newest unicorn ($1.35B; Bosch and Schaeffler backing; wheeled Beta units Q4 2026); Franka's Mobile FR3 Duo gives European labs a domestic reference platform; Wandercraft's Calvin (bipedal, hence annexed) took Renault's money into real plants; and Munich's RobCo — fresh off a $100M Series C — announced its bimanual, humanoid-form "Alfie" in April 2026 (officially shown only from the waist up — though its promo video briefly reveals a mobile base in simulation), moving Germany's best-funded modular-robot startup into the category via RaaS. Germany specifically: Neura's MiPA is the declared household/care entrant (€9,999, deliveries slipping; Series C up to $1.4B confirmed June 2026), Devanthro sells care-avatar telepresence, and the research bench — DLR's Justin, KIT's ARMAR-7, TUM's GARMI (no spin-off exists — available IP/talent), FZI's HoLLiE — is unmatched but under-commercialized. Cautionary tales are equally instructive: Aldebaran (Pepper) liquidated in 2025 with its assets sold to a Chinese buyer; Care-O-bot's maker became an AMR vendor; Cybedroid sold 6 units and died. A telling blank on the origin map: the Nordics. Norway created the archetypal wheeled humanoid (Halodi/1X's EVE) but the company relocated to Palo Alto and pivoted to legs; Sweden's entry (Hexagon's AEON, Schaeffler-backed) is a wheel-leg hybrid sitting in this study's borderline annex; and Denmark's Odense cluster — Universal Robots' arms, MiR's bases — supplies the category's components without ever assembling a semi-humanoid of its own. Europe's gap is not capability but industrialization — precisely the entry a well-capitalized German robotics company can make ahead of the 2027 Machinery Regulation.

9  Regional landscape — Japan

31 documented robots — the longest institutional memory, now re-mobilizing. Japan invented the category's ancestors (wakamaru 2005, enon, SmartPal, Twendy-One, ROBEAR) and its icon: Pepper (~27,000 produced — still the largest semi-humanoid fleet ever; B2B support continues in Japan, with a "Pepper+" rental relaunch in 2026 even as Aldebaran died in Europe). Today's activity clusters in: teleoperation-first services (ugo Pro in building security; Ory's OriHime-D café employing 70+ disabled pilots — a social-innovation model unique to Japan; Jinki Ittai's "humanoid heavy machinery" in revenue service on JR West railways); industrial dual-arm incumbents (Kawada NEXTAGE since 2009; Yaskawa's MOTOMAN NEXT AI line — and note Yaskawa wholly owns Tokyo Robotics, whose Torobo2 builds Waseda's AIREC care robot: one keiretsu quietly spans Japan's flagship care program and its industrial dual-arm line); hospital/care pilots (Kawasaki Nyokkey at Fujita Health University; Foxconn/Kawasaki Nurabot launching FY2026); and Toyota's ELEY — QDD actuators in every joint, a scapular axis, and a production-site learning agenda: the highest-credibility incumbent program worldwide (§12). Tracked outlier: Mitsubishi Electric's DiaroiD. Japanese firms also increasingly distribute Chinese wheeled humanoids (Cruzr S2 via GA Robotics), importing the competition.

11  Regional landscape — Rest of world

17 documented robots. Israel: Unlimited Robotics' Gary (~10 units in a Philadelphia hospital plus Israeli MoH deployments) — and Mobileye's $900M acquisition of Mentee (bipedal line; the rumored wheeled variant does not exist) shows the exit ceiling. India: from Invento's wound-down Mitra to Genrobotics' 200-kg-payload tracked GenBot, Perceptyne's assembly torsos and ISRO's space-bound Vyommitra (Gaganyaan G-1, now Q3 2027). Russia: Promobot's V.4 service fleet (800+ units claimed) persists, but its Robo-C android line is discontinued and the FCC action closes the US. Turkey's AKINROBOTICS, Saudi Arabia's QSS, Nepal's Paaila and Vietnam's defunct TOPIO show the long tail. The open-source movement matters strategically: XLeRobot (~$660, 5.4k GitHub stars), BamBot (~$300), InMoov and hobbyist builds like Nyro (differential-gear joints, QDD motors) are training a generation on semi-humanoid architecture — tomorrow's engineers and tomorrow's expectations.
